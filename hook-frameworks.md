# Hook Frameworks

Built from the same five research documents as `principles.md`. These
are the specific opening-line/on-screen-text patterns, as opposed to
the higher-level positioning ideas in `principles.md`.

Cited by `/ideate-personal` and `/ideate-ern` as `hook_framework:
HF<n> — <name> (hook-frameworks.md)`.

## HF1 — Consequence, not the pain itself

Don't call out the raw pain point, call out its downstream
consequence. "Are you tired of being bald?" is generic and everyone's
heard it. "Are you tired of wearing a hat all the time?" is specific,
visual, and implies the same pain without stating it directly.

*Source: Heaton Ralston brothers' material.*

## HF2 — Promise, Progress, Payoff

State a concrete promise in the hook, spend the body visibly
progressing toward it, then actually pay it off before the piece ends.
Never promise something in the hook and defer delivery behind a
comment/follow gate — that's the single fastest way to burn trust with
an audience (see P12 in principles.md).

*Source: Heaton Ralston brothers' material.*

## HF3 — Identity defense ("...and I'm the bad guy? Wrong.")

Open with a scenario where the narrator did something defensible that
sounds bad out of context, then defend it. Pattern: "I [did
questionable-sounding thing], and I'm the [negative identity]? Wrong.
I'm not the [negative identity]. [Reframe / real context]." Works well
for contrarian takes inside relationship, career, or social-norm
content.

*Source: Briar Cochran swipe file (OST research doc).*

## HF4 — Curiosity Bridge + Context Coverage

Structure a hook so it does two things at once: opens a specific
curiosity gap (a "bridge" the viewer wants to cross to find the
answer) while covering enough context up front that a New viewer (see
P3) isn't lost. A hook that's all curiosity and no context loses
casual/new viewers; a hook that's all context and no curiosity loses
everyone.

*Source: Briar Cochran ("How to Drive Watch Time With Your Hooks").*

## HF5 — Progressive intrigue

Treat every sentence, not just the hook, as needing to deliver both
payoff and mystery — the viewer should feel simultaneously satisfied
by what they just learned and hooked by what's coming next. Test:
between any two consecutive sentences, an implied "therefore,"
"however," or "for example" should fit. If a sentence doesn't advance
or complicate the "plot," cut it.

*Source: Briar Cochran research doc (uncredited creator clip on
progressive intrigue and story structure).*

## HF6 — On-screen text: 5 copywriting principles

**This is the primary framework for IG_reel/IG_carousel/YT_short
formats, not a secondary one** — see P15 in `principles.md`: on-screen
text drives reach, the caption barely matters. When `/ideate-*`
produces a `hook` for these formats, write it as the literal on-screen
text, not a spoken line or caption.

Luke's own account history (`brands/personal/voice.md`,
`brands/engine-room-nutrition/voice.md`) is full of real examples of
this pattern already working — e.g. "How to actually use strides (To
get you a PB)" is dream-outcome-in-parentheses; "How to build a sauna
into your easy run *(to make racing easier)" is the whisper technique;
"Q: How do you decide between recovery and rest?" is a direct
pain-point/curiosity question. Use those as a starting reference before
inventing something new.

For on-screen text/captions overlaid on video (not the spoken hook):

1. **Curiosity** — allude to something you'll reveal later.
2. **Pain point** — name the viewer's specific fear/frustration
   directly on screen.
3. **Dream outcome** — state the specific (not vague, not
   "$10,000/month" generic) thing they actually want.
4. **Tangibility** — make the outcome something they can picture
   holding, seeing, or feeling, not an abstraction.
5. **Whisper technique** — use parentheticals for an aside that either
   builds curiosity for later or does "risk rehearsal" (pre-empting
   the #1 reason someone would bounce, e.g. "(not much time
   required)").

Mechanical rules that go with it: one font family max (native or a
single deliberate luxury font), no function words on screen (a, the,
and, so — keep only proper nouns/verbs/keywords), max 2 lines,
line-break by structure not by character limit, hold text on screen
~10 seconds (not the native 2-3s cut speed) so it's actually readable.

*Source: Briar Cochran research doc (on-screen text segment,
referencing a tool integration with a company called Artec).*

## HF7 — Outcome/solution template swipe file

A block of ~30 fill-in-the-blank hook templates pulled from Briar
Cochran's research doc. Treat `[outcome]`, `[solution]`, `[personal
outcome]` as slots to fill with something specific to the pillar/idea,
never left literal. A sample (not exhaustive — see the source doc for
all 30):

- "If you're tired of [opposite of outcome], here's the ultimate guide
  to [outcome]. Take notes and thank me later."
- "I'm gonna give you guys my BEST [solution] as someone who [personal
  outcome], that's literally gonna help you [outcome]."
- "Here's exactly how you're gonna [verb] your first [outcome]."
- "I get this question SO often: how did you [personal outcome]? And I
  literally just [solution]."
- "So you failed to [personal outcome]. Tough love, but it's because
  you didn't [solution]."
- "After years of [personal outcome], these are the 6 rules of
  [outcome] I know to be true."
- "3 steps for becoming [outcome]."
- "5 things you need to know if you want to [outcome]."

*Source: Briar Cochran research doc (OST). Full 30-item list lives in
the original upload if a wider net is needed later.*

## HF8 — Counter-position as a hook, not just a strategy

The 3 counter-positioning tactics in P2 (add to niche, go
upstream/downstream, associate) also work directly as hook openers,
e.g. leading with the unusual combination itself: "horse riding + AI,"
or naming the association up front ("I run my business like [known
figure] would").

*Source: Briar Cochran, cross-referenced from P2.*

## HF9 — Luke's own post structure: Verbal Hook → Super Hook → Body → Feeling → CTA

Luke's own stated structure for how he actually builds a post, distinct
from HF6 (which is about the copywriting mechanics of on-screen text,
not the overall shape of a post). This is the shape:

1. **Verbal hook** (spoken, not on-screen) — states the dream outcome
   directly. His own example: "If you want a few tips for marathon
   training, I want you to feel excited again for your long runs.
   Here's how you can do it."
2. **Super hook** (on-screen text) — a credibility/authority statement,
   not a curiosity hook. His own example: establishing something like
   "I ran a marathon, I'm sub-19 for a 5k" before getting into the
   content. This is what makes the advice worth listening to,
   distinctly different from HF6's curiosity/pain-point framing.
3. **Body** — 3-5 elements/tips/benefits.
4. **Feeling** — describe how the viewer should feel while/after doing
   each element, not just what to do mechanically.
5. **CTA** — for ERN specifically, this points to the email
   list/newsletter signup (ERN is pre-manufacturing, so the CTA is
   list-building toward launch, not a purchase link).

When `/ideate-*` builds a `body_angle` for a longer-form idea (not just
a single on-screen-text hook), structure it through these 5 beats
where the format allows (works well for IG_reel/YT_short scripts less
so for a single carousel slide or a short-form hook-only post).

*Source: Luke, describing his own established format.*

## Universal reminder

Every hook produced from these frameworks still has to clear
`voice-rules.md` (no em-dashes, no hedging, no corporate filler) and
whichever brand's own `voice.md` hard rules apply.
