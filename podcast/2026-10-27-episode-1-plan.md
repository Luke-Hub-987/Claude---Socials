# Episode 1: the Dublin marathon at every 10K

## What the podcast is for (decide this first)

ERN is pre-launch and nobody is searching for a podcast by two people they
haven't heard of. Episode 1 won't find an audience on its own, and that's
fine, because that isn't its job. **Its job is to be the source file for
3 to 5 reels and to give the people already on the email list a reason to
trust the two people behind the bar.** So it's built for clips first and
listening second, and judged on clips and emails, not plays.

Expect low hundreds of YouTube views in the first week (forecast, P145).
Don't read that as a miss.

## Format (recommended)

- **35 to 45 minutes, video, two people, one topic.** Every episode is one
  race or one question, run on a clock, with the numbers said out loud.
- **Luke hosts, Jamie answers first.** One person steering stops two
  co-founders drifting into in-jokes (ledger row 20: a story with no stake
  for strangers). Luke gives his own answer second.
- **Not scripted.** A run of show with questions and a card with the
  numbers. A read script kills a conversation, and the reels already have
  the scripted voice. The exception: the one-line rule at the end of each
  10K (below) is said cleanly, to camera.

## The same three questions at every 10K

This is what turns a race story into advice (P127, P151) and makes each
10K a clip that stands alone:

1. **What was the plan?** (a number: the pace band, the gel time)
2. **What actually happened?** (a number: the split, the time you took it)
3. **What would you tell someone running their first?** (one action, one
   number, said to camera in one breath, 15 to 20 seconds)

Question 3 is the clip. After each answer, Luke says: "Say that again, to
someone who's never met us." That second take is what goes in the reel.

## Rules on the day (what can't be said)

- **Nothing that contradicts a reel** (the consistency points in
  CLAUDE.md). If one of you broke a rule (went out fast, two gels at once,
  a gel with sports drink), say it plainly as a mistake and what it cost.
  That's some of the best content in the episode. Never present it as what
  to do.
- **Fuel as grams an hour first** (P118). If you say "I took 8 gels", say
  the grams an hour before it. A bare gel count gets clipped and restarts
  the "eleven gels" argument (P124).
- **The bar's slot is before the start, never in the race** (P134). If it
  comes up, that's the only way it's described.
- No medication, no central governor, no "a study says". No claims about
  yourselves you haven't made before. If you don't remember a number,
  say so and we fill it from the watch data; never guess on air.

## The clip plan for Sat 31's reel

The reel can't be cut straight from a conversation and still pass the
checklist: the gate, line one, the pain, the dream and the CTA need the
scripted hook stack (P138). So:

- **The podcast gives the reel its story opening and its four stages**
  (the "say it again" takes at each 10K).
- **The hook lines are recorded to camera on Friday, straight after the
  podcast**, from a brief Luke has said yes to. The brief is written Thu
  night once the facts below are in.
- **Mark the clip moments on the day.** Luke claps once on camera after

  searching 40 minutes of footage.

### Two challenges to the Sat reel as planned (decide in its brief)

1. **"OF THE DUBLIN MARATHON" in the title narrows the gate after the
   race** (ledger row 11). Dublin km by km did 31.6K the week *before*
   Dublin, when Dublin runners were searching. Most of the audience now
   has a spring race. If the facts show a real mistake, a title on the
   mistake (e.g. WHAT WENT WRONG AT EVERY 10K / OF OUR MARATHON) carries a
   stake strangers feel, with Dublin in line one. If nothing went wrong,
   keep the planned title. This depends on what happened, so it waits for
   the facts.
2. **A full splits screenshot as the cover breaks ledger row 14** (every
   number on screen is in the title or line one). Crop it to the one split
   that tells the story (the slow 10K, or the halfway time) and put that
   number in line one.

## Setup

- **Two phones, vertical, one tight on each of you,** at head-and-shoulder
  height (this is what the reels are cut from).
- **One wide shot, landscape,** of both of you, for YouTube. A third phone
  or a laptop camera is enough.
- **Audio is what makes it watchable:** one clip-on mic each, recording
  separately if the kit allows. A small room with soft furnishing beats a
  big kitchen.
- One clap at the start to sync, 10 seconds of silence for room tone,
  phones on flight mode and with storage cleared.
- **The splits on a printed card or a tablet on the table**, so the
  numbers are right and you can hold one up to camera.

## Name and where it goes

- **Name:** decide by Saturday, not Friday (it only matters at upload).
  Recommended: plain and tied to the brand, e.g. *Engine Room Running*.
  Check YouTube and Spotify for clashes before committing; "The Engine
  Room" is a common podcast name. The episode title matters more than the
  show name, because that's what gets searched: *The Dublin Marathon at
  every 10K: what we did and what we'd change*.
- **Where:** YouTube (the full video) and Spotify (the same video
  uploaded as a video podcast). Apple Podcasts later, from the same feed.
  Nothing else for episode 1.
- **The plan CTA:** one sentence in the audio at ~13:30 and the first line
  of the description. Counted by day with the reels (P154). No code, no
  second link, no extra step.

## LLM Council (P150)

### 1. Contrarian: why it fails
- Two co-founders talking about one race for 40 minutes is the surprise
  reel's problem at full length: strangers have no reason to care. The
  surprise lost 44.1% in 3 seconds.
- The race was 5 days before recording. Feelings are fresh; numbers are
  already fuzzy. One wrong number on air contradicts the watch data in the
  same video.
- Recording Friday and posting a reel cut from it Saturday is one evening
  to edit, on top of Thursday's 5:00 reel.
- If both of you ran a clean race, there's no story. "It went to plan"
  is a reel, not 40 minutes.

### 2. Assumption-ripper: what we're taking for granted
- **That a podcast is worth doing now.** Pre-launch, the reels are 147K at
  best and the podcast will be hundreds. It's worth it only as the source
  of reels and founder trust. If it doesn't feed at least 3 reels, it's a
  cost.
- **That the reel can be "cut from" the podcast.** It can't pass the
  checklist that way; the hook stack has to be scripted (hence the
  Friday pickup recording).
- **That Dublin is still the topic for the reel.** It was when Dublin
  runners were searching. It may not be now.
- **That Jamie wants to be on camera for 40 minutes.** Nobody has asked
  him.
- **That the splits cover works as planned.** A full screenshot breaks
  row 14.

### 3. Expansionist: the upside we're missing
- **Every 10K is its own reel,** not just Sat's. Four "say it again" takes
  are four TikToks (TikTok rewards completion, and the surprise did 13.6%
  full there).
- **The "what we'd change" table is the first thing ERN can hand out that
  no one else has:** two real runners' data, side by side. It belongs in
  the email to the list as well as the caption.
- **Episode 2 writes itself from the comments:** ask "What happened to you
  at 30K?" in Sat's caption and answer the best ones on air.
- **Jamie's voice is a second face** for the brand; the grid has only had
  Luke.

### 4. Outsider: no context
- "Who are you two and why should I listen?" (the three sentences at 1:00)
- "What's a split?" (say "your time for each K" the first time)
- "Did it go well or not?" (say the finish time against the goal early,
  in the cold open or at 1:00, so the listener knows the stake, and keep
  the *how* for the 10K segments)
- "Is this an ad for the bar?" (the bar is mentioned once, in its slot)

### 5. Executor: the end result only
- **Deliverables:** one 35 to 45 minute episode on YouTube and Spotify;
  four clean 15 to 20 second 10K takes; the Sat reel's hook lines recorded
  to camera; a "what we'd change" table.
- **Tue 27 (today):** Luke sends the facts list to Jamie; Luke asks Jamie
  for his yes.
- **Wed 28:** facts in. **Thu 29 night:** the Sat brief (P138) for Luke's
  yes, and the numbers card. **Fri 30:** record, then the pickup lines;
  rough cut of the reel that night. **Sat 31:** reel posted; episode
  uploaded the same day or Sunday.
- **Measure:** reels cut from it (target 3 or more), emails on clip days
  against the Mon 26 baseline (11), episode retention on YouTube. Plays are
  not the measure.
