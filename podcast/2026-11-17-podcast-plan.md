# The podcast: the plan

## 1. Four things to settle first (where the current thinking is weak)

**1. "They'll relate to us" is the reason they stay, not the reason they come.**
Two amateur runners talking is the most crowded format in running. The
surprise reel showed what happens when we're the story: 44.1% skip, 0 saves.
Being relatable is how you deliver the advice. It can't be the reason someone
presses play. The reason has to be something only we have, and we do have one:
**about 200 marathon plans written for amateurs this autumn, and the results
coming back** (FACTS, "Plan results after Dublin"). Untapped has elites.
Nobody has the inbox of 3:45-to-4:30 runners we have. That's the authority:
it comes from them, not from our finish times.
- So every episode starts from a real runner's race (with their OK, first
  name only) or a real number from the inbox, not from a chat about our week.
- Careful with how the count is said (ledger row 28): "about 200 plans, and
  most of the results that came back were PBs" holds up. "17 results" on its
  own sounds like a failure.

**2. The podcast will eat the reels if we let it.** The scripted reels are
what bring the emails, and both of you have full-time jobs. Most podcasts stop
before episode 10 because the time cost was never budgeted. The answer:
- **For season 1 the podcast is the long-form YouTube.** Not a podcast plus
  separate long-form videos plus 4 reels a week. Make separate long-form only
  once the podcast runs on 4 to 5 hours a week.
- The format is built to need almost no edit (section 3). If an episode needs
  more than 2 hours of editing, the format is wrong, not the editor.

**3. Clips cut from a conversation don't reach like scripted reels, and our
own rule says why.** A talking clip has no gate, no gap in line one and no
pain-and-dream stack (P138; the 27 Oct plan saw this coming). So:
- Clips go to **TikTok, YouTube Shorts and IG trial reels**, where volume
  is what we're after.
- The **best clip each week gets rewritten as a scripted reel** for the
  Instagram profile, through the gate as usual. The podcast becomes the
  testing ground for topics, and the reels stay the reach engine.

**4. It sells the bar by making listeners part of building it, not by
pitching it.** "Nobody buys April race fuel in November: sell the bar from
January" (FACTS). People buy at launch when they feel they helped make the
thing: they voted on the texture, they heard why batch 3 failed. So there's
a short **Lab segment** from January (section 3), always at the end and always
answered as "why this matters before your start", never as "buy our bar".

## 2. What the podcast is for, and what counts as success

**Its job, in order:** (1) a source of 4 to 6 clips a week; (2) a reason for
the weekly email to exist and for subscribers to reply; (3) the trust that
turns a subscriber into an early-access buyer in January; (4) a YouTube and
Spotify audience. Plays come last on that list, because pre-launch nobody is
searching for two people they haven't heard of.

**No forecast.** We have no comparable episode, and the gate says forecasts
come from comparables. Expect small numbers and judge it on these instead:

| Measure | Where | What good looks like by episode 8 |
|---|---|---|
| Still watching at 1:00 | YouTube retention graph | Most viewers still there. If a big share leave in the first minute, the opening is the problem, not the topic |
| Average % viewed | YouTube | Read it against our own trend: episode 8 above episode 2 |
| Clips per episode | Our count | 4 or more posted, every week |
| Best clip each week | TikTok / Shorts views | One a week good enough to rewrite as a profile reel |
| Replies and questions | Inbox, story question box | Rising week on week: it shows a community is forming |
| Sign-ups on release day | Klaviyo, by day (P154) | Above the no-post baseline (Mon 26 Oct: 11) |
| Hours spent | Your diary | 5 hours a week or less, between you |

**Review after episode 8 (about early February):** keep it as it is, change
the format, or stop. Stop if it's costing more than 5 hours a week **and** the
profile reels have dropped below 4 a week because of it.

## 3. The format: one question, one runner, the same 7 parts every week

**Length: 20 to 25 minutes.** Your 15 to 30 is right; aim for the middle.
Watch time, not length, decides whether YouTube shows it, and a tight 22
minutes beats a padded 35. Many runners listen during an easy run, so 20 to
25 minutes also fits one. Let the retention graph decide whether to go longer
later. Long runs are a reason to go to 40 one day, but not before we've
earned it.

**One question per episode,** worded as runners search it (e.g. "why do
marathons fall apart after 30K"). "Episode 4 with Jamie" can't be searched.
The title is chosen **before** recording, because the title is what decides
what goes into the episode.

| # | Part | Time | What happens | Why it's there | Clip? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold open** | 0:00-0:40 | The best 30 to 40 seconds of this episode, picked in the edit (usually Jamie's pushback or the "tried it" result) | YouTube viewers decide in the first 30 seconds. "Welcome to the podcast, how's your week" loses them | Yes |
| 2 | **The promise** | 0:40-1:15 | Luke, one breath: the question, the belief we're going to break, and what they'll leave with. Then who we are in one sentence: "Two runners with day jobs who've written about 200 marathon plans this autumn" | The reels' gap, scaled up (P176): open the question here, close it in part 6 | No |
| 3 | **The runner** | 1:15-5:00 | One real race from the inbox (or a listener live on Riverside, see below): their goal, their splits, the K it went wrong. Ends on the question still unanswered | The pain picture. It has to be a problem most listeners have (ledger row 11), and a goal most listeners have, 3:45 to 4:30 (row 23) | Maybe |
| 4 | **The myth** | 5:00-7:00 | What most runners believe about it, and why it's wrong, with the fact from FACTS. The enemy is the advice they were given, never the runner (lever 1) | The most shareable 2 minutes. It's where people send the clip to a friend | **Yes, the main one** |
| 5 | **The method** | 7:00-15:00 | The fix on the race clock, in 3 stages. **Luke brings the number and the study; Jamie asks "what does that mean at 7pm on a Tuesday after work?"** The one spoken CTA goes here, once, around 9:00 | The roles stop two co-founders agreeing for 20 minutes. Jamie's question is the "day job" angle working as a lens | One per stage |
| 6 | **We tried it** | 15:00-19:00 | One of you tested the method that week on a real run, around a real job, with numbers (the splits, the grams, the scales). From January, the Lab replaces half of this some weeks (below) | This is the relatable part, but it's proof, not chat. It's also the pillar that has done best for ERN (founders testing it) | Yes |
| 7 | **The one thing + next week** | 19:00-21:00 | Each of you says the one action for this week, **to the lens, in one breath, with one number**. Back to the runner from part 3: what we'd tell them (closes the loop). Next week's question as an open question. The question box | The one thing becomes the clip, the email subject line and the caption table | **Yes** |

**The Lab (from January, 2 to 3 minutes, inside part 6 or after part 7):**
one development update on the bar each time: what changed in the batch, what
failed, and one question for listeners to vote on in the story (texture,
size, flavour). Always tied to the listener's start line ("why we want this
eaten 30 minutes before your gun, not a gel"). Never in the first 15 minutes.

**The listener live (once a month, from episode 4):** Riverside lets anyone
join from a link, so a subscriber joins for 8 minutes as part 3: their race,
their numbers, our fix, live. It does three things at once. It's the most
relatable thing we can put on the show, it's a stake strangers feel, and the
guest shares it with their own running club. First name only, their OK in
writing before we record, nothing medical.

**Rules on the day (carried over from 27 Oct, still true):**
- Nothing that contradicts a reel or FACTS. Fuel as grams an hour first
  (30 g every 30 minutes), never a bare gel count (P118, P124). The bar's slot
  is before the start, never in the race (P134).
- If you don't know a number, say so; it gets filled from the watch data.
  Never guess on air.
- **Health and food claims.** The podcast counts as marketing for ERN, so
  UK and EU rules on nutrition and health claims apply to anything said about
  what the bar does. Talk about carbs and the science as freely as the reels
  do; never say the bar makes anyone faster or stops the wall. Anything
  claimed about the bar itself gets checked against the authorised claims list
  first. No medical advice, and an injury question gets "see a physio".
- **Audio-first.** Many people will listen on Spotify while running and won't
  see the screen. Say every number out loud, and never say "as you can see".
- No "rate and subscribe" in the first 18 minutes. One ask, at the end.

**The take that makes the clip:** after each stage of the method and in part
7, Luke says "say that again to someone who's never met us". The second take
is the clip. Clap once on camera after it so the edit is finding claps, not
watching 20 minutes.

## 4. Setup: Riverside on two phones

**Separate rooms if you can.** If you're both on Riverside in the same room,
each phone also picks up the other person's voice a split second late, and
that echo is very hard to fix in the edit. Separate rooms (or separate houses)
with headphones is clean. If it has to be the same room, sit as far apart as
the room allows and use clip-on mics close to the mouth.

| Thing | What to do | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **Phone orientation** | **Vertical, 1080p or 4K.** Head and shoulders, eyes about a third from the top, space on both sides | A vertical feed crops to a clip at full quality. For the YouTube split screen each half of a 16:9 frame is close to square, so the edit keeps about the middle 60% of your vertical frame's height: **keep your head and shoulders inside that middle band**. Shooting landscape would do the opposite: the clips would be cropped out of a third of the width and come out soft |
| **Mic** | One clip-on mic per phone (wired, or a small wireless kit). Check Riverside shows that mic as the input before every recording | Bad audio is what makes people leave, more than the picture |
| **Headphones** | Wired. **Not AirPods**: the phone often switches to the AirPods' mic instead of the clip-on, and Bluetooth adds delay | Echo, and recording the wrong mic |
| **Height and eyes** | Phone at eye level on a small tripod or a stack of books. Look at the lens for the one thing; at the screen the rest of the time | Looking down at a phone on the table reads as a video call |
| **Light** | Face a window. Never sit with a window behind you | A dark face on YouTube |
| **The set** | The same spot every week, with one running object in shot (shoes, a medal) and later the bar | The car worked for the reels because it was the same shot every time; same idea here |
| **Phone prep** | Do Not Disturb on, charger plugged in, 20 GB free, Wi-Fi | A call or a full phone ending a take |
| **The upload** | Riverside records on each phone and uploads as you go. **Don't close the app until both tracks show fully uploaded** | Losing a whole recording, the most common Riverside mistake |
| **Edit** | Riverside's own editor: split-screen layout, captions on, cut the cold open. Use its auto clips as a first pass, then pick the clips yourselves | A 4-hour edit. The format above needs only the cold open, the claps and the ends trimmed |

**Episode 0, unpublished.** Record one full episode next week purely to test
the kit, the rooms, the roles and how Jamie is on camera. Throw it away if it
isn't good. The first episode anyone records is always the stiffest one, so
it's better if nobody sees it.

## 5. Where it goes, and how everything links up

**One YouTube channel (ERN), a Podcast playlist,** the same video on Spotify
(Spotify for Creators, which also sends the audio to Apple Podcasts). Don't
make a second channel: a pre-launch brand can't feed two.

**YouTube packaging (decided before recording):**
- **Title:** the searchable question plus the twist, the reels' formula at
  length: e.g. *Why marathons fall apart after 30K (it starts at 10K)*. No
  "Ep 3 |" at the front.
- **Thumbnail:** 1920 x 1080 (the 16:9 rule already in place), one face plus 3
  to 4 words, black bold on white in the top band (P205). Never the two of us
  smiling side by side, because that says "chat", not "answer".
- **Chapters** in the description with searchable names ("How many grams an
  hour", not "Part 5").
- **Description, line one:** the plan offer, the same words as the reels:
  "Want your next race planned? Email us your race and goal time" plus the
  link. **Pinned comment:** next week's question, to collect replies.
- **End screen:** next episode, or the reel or long-form video on the same
  topic.

**Launch with 3 episodes, not 1.** New listeners who like episode 1 binge, and
if there's nothing to binge they leave. Record 3 before launching, then record
2 at a time every fortnight so there's always one in the bank. Missing a week
early on is how podcasts die.

**The weekly loop (what makes it a flywheel, not just one more channel):**

```
the inbox and story replies  →  the episode's runner (part 3) and the question
              ↑                                   ↓
email: the one thing + link +           episode out (YouTube + Spotify)
"reply with your race"                            ↓
              ↑                     4 to 6 clips: TikTok, Shorts, IG trial reels
the best clip  →  rewritten as a scripted profile reel (through the gate)
```

| Day | What |
|---|---|
| Sunday (both off work) | Record (2 episodes every other Sunday) |
| Mon to Tue | Riverside edit (cold open, claps, chapters), clips cut |
| **Wednesday** | Episode out on YouTube and Spotify. **Weekly email the same morning:** the one thing, the table, the link, "reply with your race and what's going wrong". Story: question box for next week |
| Thu to Sun | 1 clip a day on TikTok and Shorts; the best one as an IG trial reel. The 4 scripted reels carry on as usual |

Why Wednesday: it lands mid-week for the commute and gives the clips Thursday
to Sunday, when long-run listeners are planning their weekend. That's my
reasoning, not data: change it if your email sends better on another day.
**What matters is that it's the same day every week.**

## 7. Roles: who does what

- **Luke: the host and the numbers.** Steers, holds the clock, brings the
  fact and the study (he already vets the science), says the promise and the
  CTA.
- **Jamie: the listener's voice.** Asks what they'd ask: "what does that mean
  on a Tuesday", "I've done that and it didn't work", "why should I believe
  that". He's allowed to disagree, and should. **The best moments in a
  two-host show are the disagreements; two co-founders nodding along is the
  quickest way to lose people.**
- Every story either of you tells ends with "so for you, that means...". The
  podcast is where our story is allowed (P158), but only as proof for them.
- **One page of prep per episode, not a script:** the title, the belief we
  break and the fact behind it, part 3's runner and their 3 numbers, what we
  tried with its numbers, each person's one thing, the CTA line. Fill it in on
  the Friday; it's on a phone or a card while you record.

## 8. Name

The show's name matters less than the episode titles, because the titles are
what people search. Keep it tied to the brand so every episode builds ERN.
**Recommended: Engine Room Running,** subtitle *Marathon advice for runners
with a day job*. The subtitle is the positioning: it says who it's for and
how it's different from Untapped and the elite shows. Check YouTube, Spotify
and Apple for clashes before committing ("The Engine Room" on its own is a
common podcast name).

## 9. LLM Council (P150)

### Contrarian: why it fails
- Two co-founders, phones, no guests: it's the same as a thousand other
  running podcasts, and the surprise reel already showed strangers don't stay
  for us.
- Weekly on top of 4 reels and two jobs: a missed week by week 5, and
  stopped by week 10.
- Clips from a conversation won't pass the gate, so they'll do worse than the
  reels and drag the account's averages down.
- December launch: half the audience is between races and Christmas kills
  listening.

### Assumption-ripper
- **That relatability is the hook.** It's how the advice comes across, not
  the reason to press play. The inbox is the reason.
- **That 15 to 30 minutes is short enough to be easy.** The edit, the clips
  and the email are where the time goes, not the recording.
- **That separate long-form YouTube can run alongside it.** Not in season 1.
- **That Jamie wants to be the sceptic,** or on camera weekly. Nobody's
  asked him in this plan; it's on the list below.
- **That we can talk freely about the bar.** Health claims rules apply to
  what we say about it on air.

### Expansionist
- The inbox becomes content: every plan request and race result is a
  possible part 3, and replies to the email become next week's show. No
  other running podcast has that supply.
- Listener live via Riverside: each guest shares it with their club, which
  is distribution we don't pay for.
- The Lab turns pre-launch delay (slow progress with the food scientist)
  into a story, so the wait becomes content instead of an embarrassment.
- Season 1 ends on Seville, and Season 2 is ready to go for April, which is
  when London, Manchester and Brighton runners are actually spending money on
  fuel.

### Outsider
- "Who are you and why should I listen?" One sentence in part 2: day jobs,
  about 200 plans written.
- "Is this an ad?" The bar doesn't come up until episode 8, and then as the
  answer to a race-morning question.
- "Can I listen on my run?" Yes, if every number is said out loud.
