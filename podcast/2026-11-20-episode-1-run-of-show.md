### This week's question: the four prompts
1. **Where you are now (Luke).** Dublin: 3:44:13 chip. Your guess for the Art
   O'Neill if it were tomorrow (blank 2) against the target (blank 1). Say the
   gap as one number.
2. **What 8 weeks can't change (Luke brings the fact).** "In the studies, the
   fitter you already are, the less a few weeks adds" (Milanović 2015, a
   meta-analysis: bigger gains in the less fit). "Your heart and lungs catch up
   in weeks; your tendons take a lot longer" (FACTS, the 10% rule). So you can't
   cram 8 weeks of mileage into the last 3.
   **Jamie:** "So what's the point of the bet, then?"
3. **What 8 weeks can change a lot.** The race itself:
   - **Pacing:** the road section to Kippure (25K) is where you're tempted to
     go out fast. The marathon version is in FACTS: the first 10K 5 to 10
     seconds a K slower than goal pace.
   - **Fuel:** about 60 g of carbs an hour, the first at 20 minutes, kept up
     through the night for 8 to 10 hours. Say grams an hour only, never a
     total count of gels (P124).
   - **Practising the night and the ground:** a head torch, the mountain section
     in the dark, walking the climbs on purpose.
   - **For your spring marathon,** it's the same split: your fitness for March
     is mostly built across the block, but the race plan you can change any
     time.
   **The call to action, once, about here (10:00):** "If you've a spring
   marathon and want it planned, email us your race and goal time. The link's
   in the description."
4. **What it looks like on a Tuesday (Jamie asks).** "Walk me through a normal
   week of yours between now and January." Luke: the plan (blank 3), and
   what gets cut when work runs late.

### Quick-fire: 5 questions, 45 seconds each
Start every answer with the runner, give one number, look at the lens. Every
number below is from FACTS.
1. **"When should I take my first gel in a marathon?"** At 20 minutes, then 30
   g every 30 minutes: about 60 g an hour. Grams, never a count.
2. **"How slow should my first 10K be?"** 5 to 10 seconds a K slower than goal
   pace. Up to a minute behind at halfway is fine.
3. **"Do I really need to carb load?"** 8 to 10 g of carbs per kilo a day, for
   36 to 48 hours before. A 70 kg runner eats 560 to 700 g a day. You'll
   usually weigh about 1 to 2 kg more on race morning, and that's the point.
4. **"Will £250 super shoes make me faster at 4-hour pace?"** One study found
   a third of runners at that pace used more energy in them. A newer one found
   most saved about 2%. For some, that's minutes; for others, almost nothing.
5. **"My long run jumped from 16K to 20K. Is that OK?"** Count from your
   longest run in the last 30 days, not last week: from 16K, the next one is
   17.6K. Runs 10 to 30% longer came with a 64% higher injury rate. Say "came
   with", never "causes".
