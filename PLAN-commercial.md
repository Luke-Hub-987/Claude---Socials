# The commercial plan

## The uncomfortable opening

The content engine works. Two videos at 6,683 and 7,727 on Instagram
plus 2,478 and 5,090 on TikTok, 30 new followers, 346 saves, and skip
rates better than baseline every time.

**And we have no idea how many email signups any of it produced.**

Every number we optimise against right now is a proxy. At pre-launch,
with no product to sell, **an email address is worth many times more
than a follower**, because it is the only asset that survives an
algorithm change and it is the entire launch mechanism. We are not
measuring it.

That is the first thing this plan fixes, and it costs nothing.

---

## What we are actually building

```
content → email list → early access → first production run → repeat purchase
```

Everything below serves that chain. Views are an input, not an
outcome.

---

**3. One metric added to the review of every video from now on:

the video worked.

**⚑ Needed from Luke:** current list size, the landing page conversion

## Phase 1 — harvest the race window (now to race day)

The marathon window is a one-off concentration of **exactly the right
people**: runners who fuel, in the month they care most about fuelling.
This will not repeat until spring.

**The single highest-leverage change: make the lead magnet race
specific.** "Free fuel guide" is a generic trade. "The race week fuel
plan, day by day to the gun" is the thing they want at the exact moment
they want it, and it is the same content we are already scripting.

**Also in this phase:**

- **Every video is a collab, on both accounts, on Instagram and
  TikTok.** Two of the four lower performers were not collabs. It costs
  nothing.
- **The CTA order is fixed: save, then the guide, then early access.**
  Saves are the proven behaviour, the guide is the conversion, early
  access is for the small number already sold.
- **⚑ The race expo.** Thousands of the exact customer, in one room,
  three days before a race, in the precise window the bar is built for.
  If samples exist by then this is the highest-value thing on the whole
  plan. Needs: stand cost, sample quantity, and whether we can legally
  hand out pre-manufacture product ⚑.

**Target for the phase:** a specific number of signups by race day.
Cannot be set until Phase 0 gives a baseline.

## Phase 2 — November, the test month

Race over, no event, and this is where most brands go quiet.

**The experiment that answers the strategy question.** Run two videos
head to head:

- **A manufactured deadline:** the clocks going back, dark and cold
  running, one date, universal.
- **A pure evergreen:** a "why does X happen" search asset with no date
  at all.

Compare views, saves and **signups**. That tells us whether the winning
mechanism was the race or the deadline, which decides the next six
months.

**The list starts working.** Everyone who downloaded the race week plan
just ran a race. That is the single best moment to ask them what went
wrong, what they ate, and what they wished existed. **Those replies are
product development and copy research at the same time**, and they cost
one email.

**⚑ Needed:** is there an email sequence at all, or just a download?

---

## Phase 3 — December and January, the bigger window

**January is a larger opportunity than Dublin was.** More people start
running, sign up for a first marathon, and buy gear in January than in
any other month, and almost none of them are being served well.

Spring blocks start in December and January, which means the entire
five-week-out to race-week content library we are building now **gets
reused in March and April**, pointed at a bigger audience.

The pitch changes here from "a bar for the 30 minutes before the gun"
to something a beginner understands, which is where the broader
positioning starts to matter (below).

---

## Phase 4 — launch

**The list is the launch.** A founding customer offer to the people who
have been on it since September, before any paid acquisition,
before any retail conversation.

**⚑ The numbers that decide everything here, and I do not have them:**

- Minimum order quantity for the first production run
- Unit cost at that quantity, and target price
- Therefore: **how many customers the first run needs to break even**

Until those exist, the list target is arbitrary. With them, every
content decision has a number attached.

---

## The diversification, and why it protects the business

Right now both accounts are becoming marathon-week accounts. That
audience is **transient**: a chunk of them stop caring in November.

Three layers keep them, in increasing order of effort:

1. **Rotate the race calendar.** Valencia, spring blocks, London,
   Manchester, Paris ⚑. Same machine, different date.
2. **Deadlines that are not races.** Clocks going back, the start of a
   block, coming back after illness, Christmas.
3. **The evergreen search library.** The gels video had the best skip
   rate we have ever recorded and the second lowest views. It did not
   lack quality, it lacked a date and a collab. Videos like that
   compound quietly for years. **One a fortnight, forever.**

**And the one that actually grows the market rather than refilling the
calendar:** the brand doc already says the long-term position is
pre-activity fuel, not race fuel. Halftime, before a match, on the way
to training. Hyrox, triathlon, sportives, team sport. Same promise,
same thirty minute window, a different person standing in it, and each
has its own event calendar.

That is the Maurten path, and it is the difference between a marathon
brand and a fuel brand.
