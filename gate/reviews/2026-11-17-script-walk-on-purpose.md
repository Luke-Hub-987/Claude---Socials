| G2 | FAIL | Mockup: "YOU'LL WALK AT 32K / UNLESS YOU WALK AT 2.5K"; OST field: "WALK AT 2.5K / OR YOU'LL WALK AT 32K" | The layout is clean (title only, top band, black on white, AT 32K in red, Luke alone), but the mockup shows a different title from the script's OST, so nothing has checked this script's frame one at phone size. Fix: pick one wording, put it in the H1, the OST field and the OST score, and re-shoot the mockup with exactly that text. |
| G12 | PASS | "WALK AT 2.5K / OR YOU'LL WALK AT 32K" | Someone who skips gets when the first walk comes, but not how long it lasts, how often it repeats, the gel or the 7-minute cost, so skipping costs them something; 2.5 against 32 isn't a rate, so nothing reads as failure. |
| G21 | FAIL | Caption: "For a 4:00 marathon, 2.5K takes about 14 minutes, so you walk about 16 times... For 3:30 and 4:30 it's the same 2.5K, about every 12 and 16 minutes." | "A minute every 2.5K, run again at 50 seconds" sticks after one watch, and the caption gives an interval but no schedule, so there's nothing to save. Fix: add a caption table for 3:30, 4:00 and 4:30 giving the clock time of each 2.5K walk (4:00: 0:14, 0:28, 0:43, 0:57...) with the gel walks marked, and change the save line to "Save this, the walk and gel times for your goal are in the caption." |
| G22 | FAIL | "But at 32K, the runners who never walked are walking, and you're still running past them." | Hottenrott measured finish times and soreness afterwards, not who was walking at 32K, and the run-only group finished about 7 minutes ahead, which the line before says out loud, so this payoff and the title's "OR YOU'LL WALK AT 32K" have no source and clash with the study (line one also drops the "about" from "about every 2.5K" that the facts table relies on). Fix: close on what the study measured, e.g. "But you cross the line with less sore legs, and every walk was one you picked", say "about every 2.5K" in line one, and either source the title's 32K claim or mark it "Luke to OK". |
| G23 | FAIL | "take each one on the walk nearest its time. You get 3 or 4 sips from the station without spilling them." | The walks come every 2.5K and the stations don't, so sipping from a station during the walk means carrying a bottle, and "the walk nearest its time" moves gels up to 7 minutes off the clock (at 4:00 the second gel goes to 0:57 and leaves a 43-minute gap), and C22 rules out both. Fix: "Your gel stays on the clock every 30 minutes, so if it's due near a walk take it walking, with 3 or 4 sips when you're passing a station." |
| G29 | FAIL | Rehook "There's one catch, and it's why plenty of runners never try it."; send "Follow if you'd rather pick your walk than have your legs pick it." | Both copy the fourth hour's shapes from two scripts ago ("The last 20 minutes has one rule, and it's the opposite of what you'd do"; "Follow if you'd rather finish hour 4 than survive it"), which ledger row 16 bans (no two-way meanings, mockery or fragments found). Fix: rewrite the rehook without "one X, and it's Y" (e.g. "The walkers paid for those legs at the finish, and here's the bill.") and the follow line without "you'd rather X than Y" (e.g. "Follow, then walk your first 2.5K on Sunday's long run."). |
