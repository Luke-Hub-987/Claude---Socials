If you're running a spring marathon, the flattest course here needs the one change you'd never make on a flat road.

You pick it for the PB, you feel brilliant at 10K, and you pay for it at 35K.

Do it once, and you turn for the finish with your goal time still on the clock.

The K that costs you most is the one where you feel best.

Every course on this list has one, and we call it your one change, so save this.

Belfast drops steeply out of Stormont and climbs hardest near 30K. Don't let the drop pull you quicker than your plan for the first 10K.

Email us your spring race and goal time through the link in the bio for your free plan.

Rome is flat, but about 6K is cobbles. Shorten your steps there, and let the watch pace go. Brighton's short, sharp climbs are mostly done by 18K. Climb them at the same effort, and let the pace drop.

Barcelona peaks near 14K, with less climbing in the second half. Hold the effort up to 14K, not the pace. In Paris, the tunnels by the river cut your watch's GPS, so ignore its pace in there. London crowds you till the starts merge. Then Canary Wharf scrambles your watch past 30K, so trust the clock at the 30K sign.

Manchester has about 54 metres of climbing all race. Let the rise into Altrincham go, and check the clock at the 20K sign.

And the flattest course needs the change you'd never make on a flat road.

It's Seville. Nothing slows you early, so run the first 10K about 5 seconds a K slower than goal pace. Then check the clock at the 10K sign.
