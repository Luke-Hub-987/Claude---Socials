# The race day walkthrough

## Why this one works, beat by beat

Luke's script, and what each move is doing.

**1. The hook names the belief they already hold, then flips it to a scene.**
> "You already decided the last 10k of your marathon is going to hurt
> like hell. But after watching this, you'll be passing people at 32k
> like a breeze."

Sentence one is not a pain, it is a **prediction they have already
made about themselves**, which is far harder to scroll past than a
described symptom. Sentence two hands them the opposite scene, with a
kilometre marker in it. No mechanism, no promise of information, just a
picture of them winning.

**2. It announces the shape.**
> "Here's how your perfect race day is gonna play out."

The viewer now knows exactly what they are getting and how long it will
take. That is the processing-fluency job the "10/10" numeral does in
the other format.

**3. Every step has a name.**
> "the ego check", "the sleep walk", "the hunt"

This is the single most transferable thing in the script. Named steps
are **a small vocabulary the viewer can take away and repeat**, which
is Briar's nameable-keyword idea applied three times inside one video
instead of once in the title. It also makes the video quotable, which
is what gets it saved.

**4. Real numbers inside ten seconds.**
> "run 15 seconds slower per kilometre than your target. If you wanted
> 5:30 per K, run this at 5:45."

Value before any theory, and the example is worked rather than
described (P69).

**5. The objection is pre-empted at the exact moment it will occur.**
> "Everybody will pass you, and you'll be convinced you're going too
> slow."

Not "some people find this hard". The precise feeling, at the precise
kilometre. This is what stops them abandoning the plan on race day, and
it is why the advice feels lived rather than researched.

**6. The mechanism arrives late and in one sentence.**
> "Because you took it easy at the very start you were only burning
> fat, so your muscles are still packed with sugar, while everyone who
> tried to bank time is now dying."

Physiology is simplified almost to the point of being a metaphor, and
it is placed **after** the payoff rather than before it. ⚑ Worth one
citation if he wants to keep the fat and sugar framing exactly as is.

**7. The payoff is an experience, not a metric.**
> "start claiming a few souls", "watching you glide past them", "you
> will feel powerful"

Nobody remembers a time goal. They remember passing people.

**8. The CTA is conditional and it has a second door.**
> "If you want a change or a break from gels... early access is in the
> bio. You also get a free endurance guide."

The product is offered as a solution to a problem the video already
established, and the free guide gives someone who is not ready to buy
a reason to hand over an email. **This is the only video on either
account with a real conversion mechanism in it**, which matters given
follows and list growth are the actual objective (P66).

## The template

```
1. The prediction they have already made about themselves
2. The opposite scene, with a real marker in it
3. "Here is how it plays out"
4. Step 1, named, with a real number and the objection pre-empted
5. Step 2, named, with what it should feel like
6. Step 3, named, the emotional payoff
7. One sentence of mechanism, late
8. Conditional product line, plus a free thing for everyone else
```

## Why it keeps getting reach: two things stacked

**Search.** It is phrased as a query and the topic is evergreen.

**An event.** Dublin is in late October ⚑, and the whole city's worth
of first-timers is searching this exact question right now. Race season
creates a demand spike that no amount of hook craft can manufacture.

**So the calendar is a content input.** For roughly six weeks before a
major race, high-intent race-execution questions get searched far more
than usual, and a video published into that window keeps earning after
it. This is the cheapest reach available to either account and we have
been ignoring it.
