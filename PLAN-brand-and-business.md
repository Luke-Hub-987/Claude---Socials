# Engine Room Nutrition: the business and the brand

## 0. The short version

**Your moat is the plan, not the bar.** In seven weeks you've built
something most food start-ups never get: strangers trust you with their
race. About 500 of them have sent you their race and goal time. The bar
wins only if it becomes **the first step of that plan**, eaten before every
long run in the training block and again on race morning. Then the oldest
rule in marathon running, **"nothing new on race day"**, does the locking
in for you: whoever is in the block owns the start line.

**The line (P26, the open item since September): "The race starts before
the gun."** It's the point of view your best content already proves (the
wall starts in the first 10K; the cause is always earlier than the symptom),
it's the bar's slot (before the start), and it's the frequency (the race
starts 16 weeks out, in the block).

**The constraint isn't content. It's the product and its numbers.** The
five questions in PLAN-commercial.md (list size, MOQ, unit cost, price, an
email sequence) have been open for six weeks while 60 new reel rules were
written. Every hour on reels now is worth less than an hour on the bar,
the price and 20 customer calls.

## 2. First principles: why anyone buys one bar over another

Strip it down to what's true in every food category:

1. **People buy what comes to mind at the moment of need, and what's in
   reach** (Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow*). Most buyers aren't loyal; they
   buy the brand that's easiest to think of and easiest to get. So "nobody
   picks another" isn't won by being better. It's won by **owning a
   moment** and being there at it.
2. **A product people use once a year can't build a habit or a business.**
   A race-morning-only bar is bought 2 or 3 times a year per runner.
3. **Runners have a switching cost most categories don't: "nothing new on
   race day."** Every marathoner believes it. Whatever they ate before 16
   long runs is what they eat in the start pen. **This is the only real
   lock-in available to a small food brand, and it's free.**
4. **In the EU and UK you can't lean on performance claims.** The
   authorised carbohydrate health claims are narrow and come with
   conditions (check the exact wording and conditions with the FSAI and
   the UK FSA before any pack copy). So "it fuels you better" can barely be
   said on the pack. **"Beyond the benefits" isn't a nice-to-have for you;
   the law forces it.** The brand has to carry meaning the label can't.
5. **The first purchase is bought by the brand; the second by the
   product.** Both matter. Luke's instinct ("nobody cares unless it does
   something beyond its benefits") is right for the first purchase and
   wrong for the second: if the bar sits badly before a long run, no story
   saves the reorder.

**What that means for ERN:** own one moment (before the run that matters),
get there 16 weeks early (the block), make the moment mean something (the
plan starts here), and make the bar good enough that the second box is a
no-brainer.

## 4. The positioning

**Who:** amateur marathon runners in Ireland and the UK, mostly 3:30 to
4:30, in a training block. (Hyrox, GAA, triathlon later. One beachhead
first: Thiel's "dominate a small market before a big one".)

**The moment we own:** the last thing you eat before the run that matters:
every long run in the block, then race morning.

**The point of view:** your race is decided earlier than you think. The wall
starts in the first 10K. Fuelling starts before the gun. The race starts 16
weeks out.

**The line:** **The race starts before the gun.**

**The tension (P26):** a kitchen-table rice crisp square, made to lab
numbers by two accountants who count everything. Familiar and exact at
once. Keep the rice crisp framing (voice.md is right about it).

**The spec, never the category (ost-cashvertising.md):** say the grams, the
ingredients and the numbers on the front, in the RxBar way. "30g of carbs.
Rice crisp, [real ingredients]. Eaten 30 minutes before. [N] batches to get
here." The count is the credibility. **Get the batch number. It's been
blank in every script since September.**

### Why this makes ERN the one they don't swap

1. **The plan is the brand.** You already give runners their race (pace
   bands, gel times). Precision Fuel & Hydration built their business on a
   free planner first and products second; you're already doing the first
   half by hand.
2. **The bar is the plan's step one.** Every plan you send says: "the night
   before: X; 30 minutes before every long run and the race: the bar; 20
   minutes in: your first gel."
3. **The block locks it in.** 16 long runs with it means race morning with
   it. Nothing new on race day.
4. **The ritual gives it meaning.** In the start pen everyone's nervous and
   wondering whether they've eaten enough. Eating the bar is the moment the
   plan starts and the worrying stops. That's the painkiller (P46: fear
   relief), and it's something a banana can't be.

## 5. The remarkable idea (the Purple Cow, P29)

**The wrapper is your pace band.** The inside of the sleeve carries the
runner's plan: their 10K splits in 5-second bands (P132), their gel times,
the talk test. You eat the bar in the pen and wrap the plan round your
wrist, or keep it in your pocket.

- **Retail version:** goal-time editions. The same bar in 3:30, 3:45, 4:00,
  4:30 and 5:00 sleeves. Your goal-time videos are already your best email
  drivers; the shelf would match the content.
- **Direct version:** personalised with the runner's name, race and plan
  from the email they sent you. Made for a photo; people post their race
  plan.
- **Why it's ownable:** it can only come from the brand that gives people
  their plans. A gel brand copying it looks like a copy.

**Test it now, with no bar and no money:** send the next plans as a
printable, ERN-branded pace band (one PDF, same layout every time). Ask
people to send a photo from the start line. Count prints and photos at the
December and spring races. If they don't use a free paper band, they won't
pay for one on a bar, and you'll have saved the packaging money.

## 6. The business model, as accountants would want it

### The architecture

| Layer | What | Price | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Plan | Personalised race plan (pace + fuel + the bar's slot) | Free | Trust, the list, the reason to choose you |
| The Block | Bars for every long run in a 16-week block plus race morning, shipped on a schedule synced to their race date | Subscription with an end date | Frequency, lock-in, predictable revenue |
| The Box | 12 bars, one-off | Full price | The first try |
| Clubs | Long-run packs for Irish and UK running clubs | Wholesale | Distribution with social proof |
| Retail | Run shops, then Irish grocery | Wholesale | Penetration (Sharp), later |

A subscription that ends when the block ends is honest, matches how runners
think, and renews itself when they enter the next race.

### Why frequency decides whether you can quit (illustrative, replace every number)

Assume a 12-bar box at €30, a landed bar cost of €1.10 including packaging,
€6 to pick, pack and post, about 4% in payment fees. That leaves **about €10
a box** before marketing. Two salaries plus running costs might need about
**€150K a year of contribution**: about **15,000 boxes a year**.

| How they use it | Bars a year per customer | Boxes a year | Customers needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Race morning only | 3 | 0.25 | **60,000** |
| Before every long run in 2 blocks, plus races | ~35 | ~3 | **~5,000** |

The first row is impossible from a standing start. The second is hard and
possible in two to three years. **The block isn't a nice extra; it's the
business.**

### The things you'll know better than me (check before you print a box)

- **VAT on the bar.** Confectionery versus food treatment in Ireland, and
  the UK's standard rating of most cereal bars, can move your margin by up
  to 20 points.
- **Selling to the UK from Ireland.** More of your list is UK than Irish.
  Direct sales to UK consumers likely need UK VAT registration and a plan
  for customs and labelling (FSA rules differ from FSAI). A UK fulfilment
  partner may be cheaper than posting from Sligo.
- **Food business registration** if you make it yourselves (FSAI, the
  local environmental health officer), and allergen labelling (rice crisps
  often carry barley malt: gluten).
- **The name.** Check "Engine Room" in classes 29 and 30 with the EUIPO and
  UKIPO before any packaging spend.

### Irish supports worth an hour each (check current eligibility)

- **Local Enterprise Office (Sligo):** feasibility and priming grants,
  mentoring.
- **Food Academy (LEO, Bord Bia and SuperValu):** the route small Irish food
  brands use to get onto SuperValu shelves.
- **Teagasc food research centres:** product development facilities for
  start-ups, a real alternative to a slow food scientist.
- **Bord Bia / Enterprise Ireland / Teagasc Food Works,** and Enterprise
  Ireland's pre-seed supports, once there's a product and early sales.

## 8. When to quit (decide the triggers now, while you're calm)

Taking risk is good. The smart version is taking it **once the constraint
is solved**, one founder at a time. My proposed triggers (yours to set the
numbers):

| Gate | Trigger | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Landed cost and MOQ known; 50 people have eaten samples before long runs; at least 60% say they'd reorder at the target price; the founding pre-order covers at least half the first run | One of you cuts to part-time or takes a sabbatical, if your employers allow it |
| 2 | First production run at least 70% sold within 90 days; **at least 30% of buyers reorder within 60 days**; 12 months of personal runway, or funding | The first of you goes full-time |
| 3 | Three months in a row of contribution covering one salary, plus a wholesale account or growth that covers the second within six months | The second goes full-time |

The reorder rate at Gate 2 is the number that matters most. Reach and
sign-ups can be bought or borrowed; reorders can't.

## 9. LLM Council (P150): what's the strategy that lets you go full-time?

- **Contrarian:** It fails because the list wants free pacing plans, not
  bars; a race-morning bar is bought three times a year; the category
  isn't empty (and a banana is free); and the product still doesn't exist.
  The expo idea is backwards: nobody tries a new pre-race bar three days
  before the race. Quitting before you know the landed cost and the reorder
  rate is gambling, not risk.
- **Assumption-ripper:** Every load-bearing belief is untested: that the 30
  minutes before is painful, that the gel is the only option, that sign-ups
  are buyers, that the founder story doesn't sell. Luke's own one too:
  "beyond the benefits" gets the first box, but only the product gets the
  second. And "nobody picks another" isn't how most food categories work;
  it's mental availability and being in reach. The one real lock-in is
  "nothing new on race day".
- **Expansionist:** You already own the plan. That's Precision's whole
  model. Put the plan on the wrapper (goal-time editions for shops,
  personalised for direct), sell the block not the bar, go through running
  clubs (Ireland's club culture is distribution with trust built in), and
  use Food Academy for SuperValu. Later the same moment (before the thing
  that matters) works for Hyrox, GAA and triathlon.
- **Outsider:** "I follow you for pacing. I've never seen the bar. What
  does it taste like and what does it cost? I'd eat a banana. But if it had
  my splits on it, I'd buy one for the photo."
- **Executor:** Numbers sheet by 20 Nov, 20 calls by 15 Nov, the racers'
  email by 5 Nov, a sequence by 15 Nov, samples in January for the spring
  blocks, quit triggers written down this week.

**Blind cross-review:**
- The frequency point is accepted by all five: it's arithmetic. It moves
  the bar's job from race morning to **every long run and race morning**.
- "The 30-minute pain is unproven" is accepted and becomes the customer
  calls.
- The wrapper pace band: the Contrarian calls it a gimmick and a stock
  headache (five sleeves, retail buyers who hate extra lines); the Outsider
  says it's the only thing they'd tell a friend about. Resolved: **test it
  as a free printable now; decide packaging on data.**
- Clubs, GAA, Hyrox: the Executor says not yet. One beachhead until
  reorders are proven. Accepted.
- The expo: demoted from "the highest-value thing" (PLAN-commercial) to
  awareness only. Launch at the start of a block.
- The founder story: stays out of reels, goes on the pack, the site, the
  podcast and the launch emails.

**Chairman's verdict:** **ERN's moat is the plan. Make the bar the plan's
first step, used before every long run in the block and on race morning,
so "nothing new on race day" keeps them. The line is "The race starts
before the gun."** For the next four weeks the work is the product's
numbers, 20 customer calls and an email sequence, not more reel rules.
Quit on triggers, one founder at a time, with the reorder rate as the
deciding number.
