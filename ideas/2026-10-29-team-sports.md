## 1. The verdict

**Team sport fixes the biggest weakness in the marathon plan, so it's worth
testing now.** A club player has a match most weeks for 30 weeks or more,
and trains two or three times a week. That's more bars a year than a
marathoner (about 35). It's also a better fit for the bar: you eat it
standing in a dressing room 30 minutes before throw-in, or at half-time.
And the name already speaks the language: **in GAA, soccer and rugby
"the engine room" is what people call the midfield.**

**Where I'd push back:**
1. **"Carbs are carbs" argues against you.** If carbs are carbs, a bag of
   jelly babies and a bottle of Lucozade win on price every time. The
   reason to buy can't be the carbs. It has to be the plan, the trust and
   the timing, same as for runners.
2. **"No products made for these sports" is only half true.** The
   sport-specific *positioning* is thin at amateur level. But Lucozade
   Sport, SiS, Kinetica, Applied Nutrition and Precision already work with
   clubs and teams (check current deals). And there's a reason there's no
   GAA bar: the need isn't very different between sports. **So the answer
   isn't a different bar per sport. It's one bar and a different plan per
   sport.**
3. **Weight and position.** Weight is a sound input for adults. Position
   matters only as a stand-in for how much running someone does
   (midfielders more, goalkeepers less), and the evidence for
   position-specific carb targets at amateur level is thin. Use minutes
   played and match length first; position only nudges it.
4. **Kids.** Most GAA, soccer and rugby players are under 18. **No
   weight-based numbers for under-18s**, and no body-weight questions at
   all for them. For underage teams, the plan is meal timing and food
   choices for parents and coaches, with no weights.
5. **Focus.** Two founders with day jobs, no bar yet, and one audience
   that works. Three new sports at once is how small brands stall. **Pick
   one.**
6. **The calculator isn't what gets you in.** The pace builder works
   because the reels bring 1M views to marathon runners. Nobody searches
   "carbs before a GAA match" in numbers. **In team sport the person who
   decides is the coach.** One coach's yes reaches 30 players.

**GAA first.** It's Irish-only, so the global brands don't talk to it.
It's your home ground. And club pre-season in January lines up with the
spring marathon blocks, so the bar's first season can cover both.

## 3. The calculator, done right

**What it gives (the format that works for us: a schedule with clock
times, P152):** the day before, the pre-match meal and when, **the last 30
minutes (the bar's slot)**, half-time, and after the final whistle.

**What it asks:**
- Kick-off time
- Match length (club or county; GAA, rugby and soccer differ)
- Minutes they expect to play
- Body weight (**adults only; the tool asks for age first and drops the
  weight question for under-18s**)
- Position, only as a small adjustment for how much running they do

**What makes it trusted, not just useful:**
- **A registered sports dietitian checks the numbers and is named on the
  page** (CORU-registered in Ireland; SENr in the UK). That's the line
  that lets a coach hand it to a squad. The starting guidance to give them
  is the UEFA expert-group statement on nutrition in football and the IOC
  and ACSM sports nutrition guidance. **No numbers go live before they
  sign off.**
- **It's honest about alternatives:** "the bar, or a banana and a few
  jelly sweets". Trust comes from being the one who doesn't oversell. The
  bar wins on convenience and on being part of the plan.
- **A squad version for coaches:** one printable page for the dressing
  room, with the kick-off time in and every player's timings out. That's
  the thing the Outsider said would actually get used (section 6).
- **"Send this plan to my inbox"** (optional) records sport, position
  and fixture day, so the emails can follow their season the way Flow 2
  follows a race.

**The hero on the day (P30, P158):** the player still sprinting in the
last 10 minutes when everyone else is gone. It's the marathon wall
transposed: **the last 10 minutes are lost before the throw-in.**

## 6. LLM Council (P150): should ERN lean into team sports?

- **Contrarian:** Two founders with day jobs, no bar, one audience that
  works. Three sports is how you stall. You have no team-sport audience,
  so calculators get no traffic. Most players are kids. Lucozade and SiS
  are already in the dressing rooms. Position-based carbs is weak science,
  and if a coach spots one wrong number, the trust you're trying to build
  is gone.
- **Assumption-ripper:** "Carbs are carbs" means jelly babies win on
  price. "Players use gels": many use nothing, or a Lucozade and some
  sweets. "Year-round revenue": GAA's busiest stretch overlaps the autumn
  marathons; rugby is the winter sport. Club budgets are small and slow to
  spend. But our own earlier worry, that the bar is bought too rarely, is
  answered by team sport. That one is real.
- **Expansionist:** A match every week means more bars a year. The name
  already means midfield. One bar with many plans keeps one product line.
  A coach is a 30-player sale. GAA clubs abroad (London, New York, Boston)
  come later. Parents of underage players are a huge buying group for
  meal-timing advice with no weights.
- **Outsider:** "I play club football. Before a match I have a Lucozade
  and maybe a few jelly babies. If my manager handed me a bar, I'd eat it.
  I'd never look up a calculator myself."
- **Executor:** GAA only. Calculator and squad page dietitian-checked by
  mid-December; 10 calls by 15 November; 4 to 6 trial reels in January;
  one squad tastes the bar when samples exist; decide at the end of
  February.

**Blind cross-review:**
- The Outsider's point reshapes the plan: **the squad page for coaches is
  the main tool**, and the individual calculator is second.
- The Contrarian's focus point is accepted: **marathon stays the launch
  market; GAA gets about a fifth of the time** until the end of February.
- Position stays as a small workload input; the dietitian decides the
  numbers.
- Kids: weights for adults only; underage advice is timing and food, for
  parents and coaches.
- "One bar, many plans" is accepted by all five.

**Chairman's verdict:** **Test team sport now, through GAA only, with the
coach as the customer.** Build a dietitian-checked squad match-day plan
and an individual calculator (adults only for weight) in time for
January's club pre-season. Test the content as trial reels, run 10 calls
with players and coaches, and get one squad tasting the bar when samples
exist. Marathon stays the launch. Decide at the end of February on
calculator use, coach interest and sign-ups per 1,000 views against the
marathon reels.
