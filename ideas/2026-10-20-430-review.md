# How to pace a 4:30 marathon, at ~10.5 hours

**Instagram**: **14,593 views** (7,325 IG + 7,268 FB, half on Facebook),
5,679 viewers, **21s average on 1:38**, 131 likes, 16 comments, 1 repost,
78 saves, 0 follows. **Skip 37.0%: the best of the goal-time series**
(sub-4 40.8%, 3:45 44.1%, 3:30 48.9%). Share 0.4%, like 1.6%, save 1.3%,
comment 0.2%. Still climbing at 10h (posted lunchtime).
Retention: ~55% after 3s, ~35% at ~0:38, then **a sharp step to ~15% at
about 0:40**, flat to the end.
**TikTok**: 8.5K at ~10.5h, 16.6s average, 1.62% full, 168 likes, 22
shares, 81 saves, 8 follows (3:30: 4.9K at 12.5h; 3:45: 10.9K at 17h).
**Emails**: "lots this week" (Luke); per-video count still to get.

## Read
1. **The front fixed itself: skip 37.0%, 12 points better than 3:30.** Three
   things changed together: the slowest goal (the biggest group), a cover
   of one runner at a drink station (the hook as a picture, no crowd), and
   a lunchtime post. The goal-time pattern holds in reverse: the slower
   and more common the goal, the lower the skip.
2. **The pacer line worked as a series echo.** Luke's call, and the data
   backs it: the picture carried, and the reveal was new.
3. **The step at ~0:40 is the CTA,** bigger here than on 3:30. It costs
   about half of the remaining viewers, and it's what brings the emails.
   Worth it while emails are the scoreboard; keep it one sentence.
4. **Saves are low (1.3%) and follows zero on IG.** The method lives in the
   caption, and the people who stay are the ones it's for. TikTok took the
   follows (8).
5. **Half the views on Facebook**, where nobody can follow the account.

## Lessons
- **Covers: the hook as a picture, one person, no crowd** (37.0% against
  48.9% with a crowd). Same image in the opening frame.
- **Post at lunchtime**, not the evening.
- **Goal-time videos: slower goals for Instagram reach; faster goals for
  emails and TikTok.**
- **Track the CTA step:** if emails per video drop while the step stays,
  move the CTA later.
