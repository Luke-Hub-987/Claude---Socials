# Five creative tests, ranked

## What every option has to clear

- **P92, all three:** marathon in line one, the viewer's race outcome
  at stake, and a line one that reads as a query or an irresistible
  promise without handing over the answer.
- **CCN (Galloway):** it works for **Core** (marathoners in a block),
  **Casual** (runners who have not entered one), and **New** (Paddy's
  "my mother" test: someone who has never run would still get it and
  still want to know).
- **Briar:** gate, open loop and pain in the first sentence; widen the
  TAM without leaving the niche; counter-position where it is true.
- **No stunts, no fabricated personal claims, no new unverified
  science** where it can be avoided.
- **Each one tests something we have not tested**, named explicitly,
  so the result teaches us something either way.

## What the research says people actually talk about

From `reddit-running-research-manual.txt`, rough mention counts:

| Pain point | Mentions |
|---|---|
| **Portaloos / toilets** | **94** |
| Negative split / pacing | 40 |
| GI / stomach | ~29 |
| The wall (20 miles, 30K, 32K, bonk) | ~43 combined |

Portaloos are the most-mentioned single pain point in the whole
research set, and we have never made a video about them.

## 1. The lead pick: your body at every 10K

> **What happens to your body at every 10K of a marathon**

**Tests: upstream broadening (Galloway).** Every video so far has been
for somebody already in a block. This one widens the audience to
anyone curious about what a marathon does to a body, which is the
biggest TAM available without leaving the niche.

| | |
|---|---|
| Core | It tells them exactly where their race goes wrong |
| Casual | Everyone who has thought about entering one wants to know |
| New | **Passes the "my mother" test.** Nobody needs to run to be curious |

- **Ten words, 52 characters**, same length as the 3 hours video.
- **A timeline is the save**: four stages, one sentence each.
- **Each stage carries a share fact**, and **almost every one is
  already verified** in scripts we have filmed: adrenaline hiding the
  effort (first half), blood leaving the gut (long run), the sugar
  running down (gels count), running long of 42.2 (tangents).
- **First sentence:** "If you are running a marathon in the next few
  weeks, your body runs four completely different races on the way
  round, and the third one is where most people lose it."
- **Cover:** a runner in trouble somewhere around 30K. The moment
  (P93).
- ⚑ Keep anything on heart rate directional. That is the exact ground
  where the cardiac drift mistake happened.

## 2. The research pick: the portaloo queue

> **How to skip the portaloo queue at a marathon**

**Tests: humour and the single biggest pain point in the research.**
Ninety four mentions, more than pacing and the wall, and nobody makes
a video about it because it feels beneath them.

| | |
|---|---|
| Core | Thirty minutes of race morning stress, gone |
| Casual | Every runner at every race |
| New | Everyone has stood in a festival toilet queue |

- **Nine words**, marathon in line one, a real query.
- **Race outcome at stake:** starting stressed, missing a wave,
  stopping on course.
- **Share lever:** everyone has a portaloo story, and this is the video
  people send to the mate they are travelling with.
- **Material** ⚑, all practical and checkable: the toilets furthest
  from the entrance usually have the shortest queues, join the queue
  early and rejoin straight away, bring your own tissue.
- **Cover:** your own photo of a real start line queue. **Post it at
  least a week after the night before video**, because that one also
  opens on portaloos and two in a row is fatigue (P72).

## 3. The format test: the whole race plan in 30 seconds

> **Your whole marathon race plan in 30 seconds**

**Tests: length at the extreme.** Every video we have made runs 1:20
to 1:50, and TikTok full-watch sits between 1.7% and 7.7%. TikTok
ranks on completion. We have never run a video short enough to see
what completion does to reach.

| | |
|---|---|
| Core | The whole playbook in one screenshot-worthy run |
| Casual | Easiest possible entry point |
| New | Thirty seconds is a low ask |

- **Eight words.** Marathon in line one, and "marathon race plan" is a
  real query.
- **Zero new science.** Every line is a distilled version of a video
  already filmed and checked: the 3 hours, the 30 minutes, the first
  5k, the first half, the gels, the corners.
- **It is a hub.** Each line points to a full video on the profile,
  which is the cheapest way to send people to the compounders.
- **Save lever**, and the fastest video to make on the list.

## 4. The comments test: five pieces of advice to ignore

> **5 pieces of marathon advice that wreck race day**

**Tests: the contested claim at scale.** The first half video's
"biggest mistake is banking time" produced the first Higher comment
rate on the account. This stacks five contested claims in one video.

| | |
|---|---|
| Core | Counter-positioning against advice they have been given |
| Casual | Saves them from it before they start |
| New | Weaker. Needs some running context |

- **Eight words**, a promise rather than a query.
- **Material, all from videos already made:** the pasta party the night
  before, banking time in the first half, doubling your half and
  adding ten, drinking at every station ⚑ (drink to thirst), and one
  more he chooses.
- **Briar's counter-positioning, directly.** Every item is a common
  belief with a better alternative.
- **Also a hub** to three existing videos.
- ⚑ Five items in about 300 words is fifty words each. Tight.

## 5. The mystery test: why the wall arrives on time

> **Why marathon runners all hit the wall at the same K**

**Tests: the "why" OST shape.** P83 found instructions beat
explanations, on one comparison. This tests it again with the
strongest mystery available.

| | |
|---|---|
| Core | They know it is coming and not why |
| Casual | "Hitting the wall" is the most famous phrase in the sport |
| New | Passes the "my mother" test on the phrase alone |

- **Ten words.** Marathon in line one, and the curiosity is "why the
  same place".
- **Share lever:** the reason is the sugar running out at about the
  same point for most people at marathon pace, which is a fact people
  repeat.
- ⚑ **Overlap risk with the gels count video**, which stalled at
  3,784. The difference is packaging: that OST handed over the number,
  this one keeps the mystery. **He has not hit the wall himself, so no
  personal claim** (standing rule).
