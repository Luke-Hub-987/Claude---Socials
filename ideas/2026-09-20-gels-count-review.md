## Read it in one line

**The video was fine. The promise was spent before it started.**

29 seconds of average watch on a 2,000-viewer sample is a working
body — above sprint sessions, near the winners. The people who stayed
got value. 42.9% is the worst stop rate recorded, above the carbon
shoes video at 42.4%. Everything that went wrong happened in the first
second, and the six-hour flatline says the algorithm read the same
signal and stopped pushing.

## Why they skipped

**The OST answered its own question.**

> How many gels you actually need in a marathon
> *(it's about 10, and most people take 3)*

Line one asks. Line two answers. Then the verbal hook answered it a
third time — *"you need somewhere around ten gels"* — inside three
seconds. A viewer could screenshot the cover and walk away with the
entire payload. There was no open loop left to hold anyone.

That is mine, not the topic's. I built the whisper off the Two-Number
Delta device, and every example of that device in our own notes is a
*transformation*, never the answer to the title. It is now written up
as **P80** with a screenshot test in `ost-formula.md`.

## The second reason, which is bigger

**A count is not a moment.**

| Promise | Video | IG views |
|---|---|---|
| Clock moment, in order | 3 hours before | 18,000 |
| Clock moment, in order | 30 minutes before | 13,800 |
| Clock moment | The first 5k | 4,291 |
| **Quantity** | **How many gels** | **2,809** |
| Prescription | Sprint sessions | 877 |

Every winner is a **named moment the viewer is personally going to
arrive at**. They put themselves inside a clock and stay to find out
what happens to them there. "How many gels" is inventory — one answer,
held in one word, a shopping question rather than a fear.

It explains the saves too. 21 on 2,809 is 0.75%; the 3 hours video ran
1.28%. **Nobody saves a number. They save a sequence they cannot hold
in their head.** Zero follows follows from the same thing: a fact
answered is not a reason to come back.

So P78 gets sharper. It is not *race day beats training*. It is **the
moment beats the category** — a race-day topic with a quantity promise
performs like training advice.

## The spine

**Counter-position, stated positively:** the fitness is already banked.
The last long run is a dress rehearsal, and it is the last day anything
can still be changed for free.

Three counted beats, each with its own consequence (the structure that
took average watch from 25s to 39s):

1. **The fuel.** The exact gels, at the exact timing, in the exact
   amounts you will use on the day. The last chance to find out your
   stomach hates something while it costs you nothing.
2. **The morning.** Same wake-up, same breakfast, same gap before you
   start. Testing the timing, not the food.
3. **The kit.** Everything you will wear and carry, shoes included, so
   nothing on race day is new.

Close on what it costs to skip it: the first time you test any of this
is otherwise 30k into the thing you trained sixteen weeks for.

minutes before both have their own video on the profile."*

CTA: save it, then the two doors — early access and the free race week
