## Aha #1 (cadence lock, first 2-3 seconds, before any credibility beat)

A real poster's first marathon: 5 hours, average heart rate 180bpm the
entire way, and they felt completely fine, could still talk normally,
only got winded near 200. Two real explanations, not one. First:
genetics, some people's actual max heart rate is 215+, which makes 180
just a comfortable, sustainable zone for them, not a redline. Second,
the one nobody talks about: a lot of wrist optical sensors lock onto
your cadence, your footstrike rhythm, instead of your actual pulse.
It's a documented flaw on wrist monitors. That 180 might not have been
a heart rate reading at all.

## Aha #2 (body block 2, the framework, HF9's 3-5 elements)

Heart rate only means something as a percentage of your own max, not
as a raw number, and that target percentage drops the longer you're
racing, because your body only stores so much sugar before it runs out.

- **Sub-3 hour marathoners:** 82-88% of max HR. Racing for roughly 2.5
  hours, the body can burn glycogen at a higher rate near lactate
  threshold without running out before the finish.
- **3:30-4:30 marathoners:** 75-82% of max HR. The balance point between
  burning fat and carbs, go higher than that and you hit the wall
  around kilometer 30 because the sugar tank drains early.
- **5+ hour marathoners:** 70-75% of max HR. You physically cannot burn
  high-grade sugar for 5 straight hours, your body doesn't store that
  much, so the heart rate has to sit low enough to force fat-burning
  instead.

## Payoff (body block 3, counter-position + practical action)

Stop copying someone else's heart rate number. A 180bpm effort could be
an easy jog for a 20-year-old with a 215 max, and a dangerous redline
for a 40-year-old whose max is 180. A real marathon heart rate should
feel frustratingly comfortable for the first 20 kilometers, you should
be able to speak full sentences without gasping. Test your actual max
heart rate, then set a watch alarm to trigger if you cross 80% of it in
the first half of the race.
