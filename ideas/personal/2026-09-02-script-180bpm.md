## Aha #1 (first 2-3 seconds, before any credibility beat)

A real first-time marathoner averaged 180bpm for 5 hours straight and
asked if their heart was broken. Here's the non-obvious part: an average
heart rate on its own can't tell you if something's wrong, because a
number alone doesn't say how hard that actually was for you. That same
poster could still hold a normal conversation at 180bpm and only got
winded near 200. The number scared them. The number wasn't the story.

## Super hook (on-screen text only, compressed, not spoken)

**"1 marathon, 3 ultras (60k, 100k, 55k)"**

## Aha #2 (body block 2, the specific mechanism)

Heart rate climbs on its own the longer you're out there, even at the
same effort and pace, because of heat, dehydration, and hours on your
feet — it's called cardiac drift, and it's normal. So a training zone you
hit in a clean 45-minute session (say, 150bpm) was never going to hold at
hour 4 of a marathon. A higher number late in a race usually isn't your
heart in trouble, it's your heart doing exactly what hours of drift does
to everyone.

## Payoff (body block 3, the practical close)

The test that actually matters isn't the average on your watch after the
fact, it's two things in the moment: can you still talk, and is the
number climbing sharply and staying dangerously high, or drifting the way
hours on your feet always makes it drift. Check the trend, not a single
scary peak.

## Feeling

Relief, not alarm — realizing a scary number pulled out of context isn't
a diagnosis, and learning to trust the talk test over a static number on
a screen.
