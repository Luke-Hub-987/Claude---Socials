# Legends of Running — a real series concept (Personal)

Same structure as the Ogilvy post Luke pointed to: his photo next to
a real, named, verifiably-titled legend, real proof-of-scale
artifacts standing in for the brand logos, an upward visual cue
between the two, and a series banner instead of a one-off caption.
Every figure below is real and well-documented, nothing invented
about their credentials (P23 applies to a legend's claim to the title
the same as anywhere else).

**Series banner (on-screen text, bottom of every entry):**
**"legends of running"**

## Entry 1 — Arthur Lydiard

- **Title card:** "Arthur Lydiard, 'Father of Modern Distance
  Running'"
- **Proof artifacts (replaces the brand logos):** the periodized
  base-building training system he developed in the 1960s, still the
  foundation of most marathon plans run today, including the
  base-building phase in your own adventure/training content.
- **The pull, per Luke's own framing of why this works:** "he built
  the training system every marathon plan still uses, I can learn
  from him to build my own base better."
- **On-screen text hook (the specific entry title):** "The man whose
  training plan you're probably already running"

## Entry 2 — Roger Bannister

- **Title card:** "Roger Bannister, first sub-4-minute mile (1954)"
- **Proof artifact:** the actual record itself, a single, singular,
  checkable achievement rather than a body of work.
- **On-screen text hook:** "The 4-minute mile everyone said was
  physically impossible"

## Entry 3 — Emil Zátopek

- **Title card:** "Emil Zátopek, 'The Czech Locomotive'"
- **Proof artifact:** 3 gold medals at a single Olympics (Helsinki,
  1952, the 5000m, 10000m, and marathon, his first ever marathon),
  known for brutal, unconventional interval training most coaches at
  the time thought was reckless.
- **On-screen text hook:** "He won an Olympic marathon he'd never run
  before, on a training method everyone thought was insane"

## Entry 4 — Kathrine Switzer

- **Title card:** "Kathrine Switzer, first woman to officially run
  Boston (1967)"
- **Proof artifact:** bib number 261, now retired by the Boston
  Marathon itself, real and checkable.
- **On-screen text hook:** "The bib number Boston retired forever"

## Why this fits, beyond just looking good

- **P2/HF8 association**, executed visually: the credibility lands
  before any word is spoken, same as Luke's own read of the Ogilvy
  post.
- **P11 world-building**: a real named series, not a one-off, gives
  repeat viewers something to collect.
- **P30 StoryBrand**: each entry's real pull is what the viewer learns
  and applies (base-building, training philosophy), not a history
  lesson for its own sake, the legend is the vehicle.
- **P23-safe**: every fact above is real, well-documented public
  history, nothing about Luke's own training is asserted as fact
  inside these entries, only real facts about the legends themselves.
