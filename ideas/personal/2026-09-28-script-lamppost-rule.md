*Hook. The secret, with the collision (P87):* "If you want to quit a

*Pain twisted, then dream outcome:* "Keep thinking about the kilometres
left and every step gets heavier until you walk. Shrink the goal and
you get to the end of runs you would have given up on."

**[THE RULE. Save prompt]**
"Save this for the next bad run. When you want to stop, pick the next
lamppost and run to it. When you get there, pick the one after. Forget
the distance left and only think about the lamppost in front of you.
It is how I went from run-walking to running 3K without stopping, one
lamppost at a time."

**[WHY IT WORKS]**
"Psychologists call it the goal gradient ⚑. The closer people get to a
finish line, the harder they work for it. In one study, coffee drinkers
came back faster and faster the closer their loyalty card got to a
free coffee ⚑. A lamppost puts the finish line just up the road, so
you are always on the part where people try hardest."

**[REHOOK]**
"And here is what to do when even one lamppost feels too far."

**[WHEN IT STILL FEELS TOO FAR]**
"Walk to the next one, and run the one after. It still counts. No
lampposts? Use anything that repeats, like trees, parked cars, or the
next corner. In a race, it is the next kilometre marker."

**[CTA]**
"Save it for the run you want to quit, and follow for the rest of the
rules I actually run by."

## ⚑ Science, checked

| Claim | Source | How it is said |
|---|---|---|
| Goal gradient: effort rises as the goal gets closer | Clark Hull, 1932, first shown in rats running faster near the food | "The closer people get to a finish line, the harder they work for it" |
| Coffee card study | Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng, 2006, Journal of Marketing Research: cafe customers bought coffee more often as their reward card neared the free one | "Coffee drinkers came back faster and faster the closer their loyalty card got to a free coffee" |

**Not claimed:** that it is proven for running specifically, any
brain or "central governor" mechanism (cut from ERN for the same
reason), or any number for how much further people run. The dream
line is "runs you would have given up on", which the rule is built
for, with no promise attached.

**Personal facts used:** only the real one already posted, run-walk to
3K straight, one lamppost at a time. Nothing about Dublin, work or
career.
