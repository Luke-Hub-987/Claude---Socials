# Script — the 5 uncomfortable habits

## Briar's verbal hook formula, applied (HF13)

Three separate checks, not one blended judgement:

- **Gate:** the same injury coming back every time they build mileage.
  Narrow on purpose. It filters out people who have never been injured,
  which is fine.
- **Open loop:** the habits all feel like weakness, which is why nobody
  does them. The viewer has to know which ones.
- **Pain or benefit:** they get all five, plus the one they have
  backwards right now.

## On-screen text hook

> **10/10 uncomfortable habits of runners who never get injured**

Held on screen roughly 10 seconds, top of frame, one font, two lines
maximum (HF6).

## Super hook (on-screen only, not spoken)

> **1 marathon, ultras: Connemara 60k, Seven Sisters 55k**

Compressed to a near-instant credential between aha #1 and aha #2, per
the corrected HF11 beat order. Uses only the facts confirmed in

on-screen hook → verbal hook → **aha #1** → super hook (silent) →

## Verbatim script

**[ON SCREEN, held ~10s]**
"10/10 uncomfortable habits of runners who never get injured"

**[VERBAL HOOK, plays under the on-screen text, runs straight into aha #1]**

"If the same injury keeps coming back every time you build your mileage
up, there are five habits that actually prevent it, and every single
one of them feels like weakness. That is exactly why almost nobody
does them. By the end of this you'll have all five, including the one
most runners have completely backwards."

**[AHA #1 — the unifying frame]**

"Here is the thing that connects all five. Not one of them looks like
discipline. They look like quitting, sleeping in, going slow, and
looking a bit stupid in public. Every habit that keeps runners healthy
costs them something socially, and that social cost is the actual
reason people skip them, not the training itself."

**[ON SCREEN ONLY, super hook, not spoken]**
"1 marathon, ultras: Connemara 60k, Seven Sisters 55k"

**[AHA #2 — habit 2, the one people have backwards]**

"Start with the one people get wrong the most. Your tendons, so your
Achilles, your patellar tendon, the tissue around your IT band, behave
like high-tension springs, and they only stiffen and adapt when you
load them heavily. Light bodyweight lunges at home essentially do
nothing for them. They need real weight, in the range of three to five
rep maxes. However, that means giving up the comfortable home workout
and going to squat and deadlift heavy in a gym, next to people who
lift far more than you do, which is exactly the part most runners
quietly avoid."

**[HABIT 1 — the mid-run abort]**

"The second one is the hardest to actually do. When you feel a twinge
in a tendon, something around a three out of ten, that will usually
settle with about forty-eight hours of rest. However, if you push
through it to finish the session, because the plan says twelve miles
and you want to tick the box, you can turn a minor irritation into a
structural tear that takes months. So the habit is stopping in the
middle of a run, turning around, and walking home feeling like you have
quit, purely to protect the rest of your season."

**[HABIT 3 — cadence]**

"The third one just looks silly. If your foot lands out in front of
your knee, you are effectively braking with every step, and that sends
a large multiple of your body weight straight up into your knees and
shins. Shorter, quicker steps, up around a hundred and seventy per
minute, land your foot underneath your hips instead. For example, it
will feel like you are taking rapid little baby steps and going
nowhere, and it feels genuinely goofy for a few weeks until your
nervous system locks it in."

**[HABIT 4 — sleep]**

"The fourth one is the one people are proudest of getting wrong.
Running breaks tissue down, and sleep is the window where your body
actually repairs it. Running on under six hours of sleep raises your
injury risk substantially, because your nervous system is fatigued and
your stabiliser muscles react more slowly. Therefore the habit is
turning the five a.m. alarm off, taking the guilt that comes with it,
and staying in bed, because the extra sleep is doing more for you than
the run would have."

**[HABIT 5 — slow easy runs]**

"And the fifth one costs you nothing physically and everything
socially. Running in the grey zone, that medium-hard effort that feels
productive, gives you heavy joint impact without the full aerobic
benefit. Keeping your easy runs genuinely, embarrassingly slow lets you
run far more with almost no wear and tear. However, it also means
uploading a pace to Strava that you would rather nobody saw, and
choosing your tendons over your ego."

**[PAYOFF]**

"So every one of the five asks you to look weaker in the short term to
stay running in the long term. The runners who never seem to get
injured are not tougher than you. They have just made peace with
looking like they are taking it easy."

**[CTA]**

"Which of the five would you find hardest to actually do? I already
know which one it is for most people."

## Why this structure

- **HF13 verbal hook**: gate, open loop, and pain-or-benefit all
  present and checked separately.
- **HF11 corrected order**: aha #1 lands before any credibility beat,
  the super hook is compressed to silent on-screen text, aha #2 follows.
- **Two genuinely distinct ahas**: the social-cost frame, and the
  heavy-load tendon mechanism. Not two phrasings of one idea.
- **P41 progressive intrigue**: the transition test is run on every
  pair of sentences, which is why "however", "therefore" and "for
  example" appear as real connectives rather than decoration.
- **P50**: full spoken sentences throughout, no clipped fragments.
- **P54**: the viewer cannot write this list themselves, which is what
  makes scrolling cost them something.
- **P35**: no negative parallelism, no AI vocabulary, no generic close.

## Verify before filming (P39)

Luke supplied this content, and it is claim-heavy. Each of these is a
factual assertion that will be on camera, so each needs a real source
behind it, exactly as the cardiac-drift failure taught:

1. **48 hours of rest for a 3/10 tendon twinge.** Needs a source, or
   soften to "usually settles quickly with a couple of days off".
2. **Tendons adapt mainly to heavy load, 3-5 rep max range.** Well
   supported in the strength literature, but worth one citation.
3. **Overstriding sends roughly 3x body weight into the knees.** The
   multiple varies by study and by speed. Either source it or say
   "several times your body weight".
4. **170+ steps per minute.** This is a common coaching target rather
   than a universal optimum. Safer as "up around 170" rather than a
   rule, which is how it is written above.
5. **Under 6 hours of sleep raises injury risk by over 50%.** There is
   a real, widely-cited study behind this figure, but confirm the exact
   number and population before saying it on camera. The script above
   deliberately says "substantially" instead of the percentage. Put the
   number back in only once it is checked.

The two habits with the strongest support (heavy loading and sleep) are
the two carrying the most weight in the script, which is deliberate.
