# Script — Grief has 5 stages. So does a bad race.

Bullet points to riff on, per P19, not a word-for-word script. Real

anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance) is genuine, well-documented

your own specific race, per P23.

## On-screen text hook

**"Grief has 5 stages. So does a bad race."**

## Verbal hook (P30, StoryBrand, viewer as the hero)

"After this, you'll know exactly which stage you're stuck in after a
bad race, and how to actually move through it faster instead of just
sitting in it."

Names the transformation the viewer gets, not what you did, per P30.

## Body — Double Aha placement (HF11) + counter-positioning (P2)

- **Aha #1, right after the super hook (the counter-position):**
  Psychologists mapped grief into 5 real stages: denial, anger,
  bargaining, depression, acceptance. Most runners either shrug off a
  bad race as "just disappointing" or feel weirdly guilty for still
  thinking about it weeks later. Neither is right, it's not
  disappointment, it's the exact same psychological sequence as any
  other loss, just compressed into a few days. This is the
  reframe: not a character flaw, not "just get over it," a real
  process with real stages.
- **Aha #2, body block 2 (the specific mapping, this is the new
  packaging):** Walk through what each stage actually looks like for
  a runner:
  - Denial: "that time doesn't count, my watch was wrong, I'll just
    not look at the official result"
  - Anger: blaming the course, the weather, the shoes, anything
    external
  - Bargaining: the "if only I'd done X differently" loop, the one
    people get stuck in longest
  - Depression: the flat, low week after, motivation gone
  - Acceptance: ready to actually look at what happened and train
    again
- **Payoff, body block 3 (not a third aha, the practical close):**
  Name bargaining as the stage most people get stuck in, because it
  feels productive (it's not, it's just rumination dressed up as
  analysis). The actual move: write down the 1 real factor you'd
  change, then close the loop deliberately instead of replaying it.

## Feeling

The viewer should feel *seen*, not judged, "oh, that's literally what
I did" instead of feeling weak or dramatic for still thinking about a
bad race.

## CTA

Soft engagement: ask which stage they're in right now, or which one
they always get stuck in.
