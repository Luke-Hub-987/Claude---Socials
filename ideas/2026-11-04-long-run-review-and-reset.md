## Luke's diagnosis, checked against the data: he's right
1. **No specificity.** Our winners all had a famous thing or a number in
   the title: the wall, 7 days, the first 10 minutes, 3:45, carb-load.
   "How hard your long run should feel" has neither.
2. **No time pressure.** The winners went up 1 to 4 weeks before the
   viewer's race. A long run is every week for 5 months: nothing is lost
   by scrolling past it today.
3. **Nothing new.** Every runner has heard "run your long run easy". The
   10-word test is ours, but the title never said so.
4. **The pond.** In November most marathoners don't have a race in the
   next month. The match reel, in a different pond, stopped people
   (26.2%); the familiar marathon lure didn't (55.5%).

This repeats across posts, so it's a ledger row now (row 27, P178):
the long run 55.5%, where your marathon goes wrong 52.1%, what your last

## First principles: what Briar's titles actually do
His stake is money; his viewers want content that makes them money. Ours
is the race: a time on the clock, a wall at 30K, 16 weeks of training
wasted. Read through his swipe file, every title does at least two of
these:
| Move | His titles | Our equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| **A specific result with numbers** | "$3K/mo to $40K/mo", "1.7M views", "your first 10K video" | A finish time: 4:12 to 3:51, sub-4 |
| **Proof from a big sample** | "Study on 1000 videos with 100K+ views" | Our plans: "we've planned [N] marathons" |
| **A deadline** | "You have 2 years left", "(2026)", "Trial reel hack (May 2026)" | The spring race date; Valencia in 4 weeks; Christmas |
| **A constraint** | "With ZERO credibility", "post 3x a day without losing quality", "while working a 9-5" | "On 3 runs a week", "on 4 hours' sleep", "without a watch" |
| **A named method that's his** | "The Accordion Method", "the View Ladder", "Stair steps theory" | The 10-word test, the hidden wall, the speed tax |
| **A ranking** | "Ranking content formats" (S to D) | Ranking what runners eat, race by race |
