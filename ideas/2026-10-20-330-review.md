# How to pace a 3:30 marathon, at ~12.5 hours

**Instagram**: **8,934 views** (5,922 IG + 3,012 FB). **Skip 48.9%, our
worst on record** (carb load 46.7%, 3:45 44.1%, sub-4 40.8%). Share 0.4%,

**Demand signal**: lots of comments and DMs asking for 4:30.

## Read
1. **The front failed, the body held.** Retention after 0:40 is flat, so
   the people who stayed got what they came for (save 2.1%, level with
   3:45's 2.0%). The loss is all in the first 3 seconds.
2. **Goal-time skip rises as the goal gets faster:** sub-4 40.8%, 3:45
   44.1%, 3:30 48.9%. The title tells everyone not chasing that time to
   swipe, and fewer runners chase 3:30 than 3:45 or sub-4. Three goal-time
   videos in a row also means followers have seen the format.
3. **The opening frame is a crowd photo** (a race start with pacer
   balloons), which the cover rule bans. We recommended a portaloo
   picture; a crowd reads as generic race footage.
4. **Posted at 6pm**, against 2:52pm for 3:45: the overnight flat line
   means the 12h read understates it. Day-3 reach will tell more, but at
   this skip it won't reach 3:45.
5. **TikTok halved** against 3:45 on lower watch time (12.0s): the same
   narrower audience.
6. **The CTA step at ~0:35 is small**, so the mid-video CTA isn't what's
   losing people. Emails are the real test.

## Lessons
- **Faster goal times shrink the audience; slower ones grow it.** 4:30 is
  the other direction (the biggest group in a mass race) and the
  comments and DMs are asking for it.
- **Covers: no crowds, even inside the opening frame.** The hook picture
  (portaloo, a watch) or Luke on camera only.
- **Post in the afternoon** (3:45 at 2:52pm), not the evening.
- Don't run a fourth goal-time video in a row on Instagram without a new
  hook shape: the format is now familiar to followers.
