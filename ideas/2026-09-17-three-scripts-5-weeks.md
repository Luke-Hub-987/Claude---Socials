# Three scripts, five weeks out

**Why the device changed.** The last three winners were countdown
walkthroughs. These three are **swaps**: one thing stops paying today,

# SCRIPT 1 — The session that stops paying

## OST

> **The last session that can still make you fitter**
> *(marathon edition)* ⚑

Reverted to the original at Luke's call, and he was right: this names
an irreversible deadline in words anyone understands, where "the
session to stop doing" named neither the session nor the stake.

**What it costs to use it.** The hook promises a deadline, so the
script has to say why five weeks is one. That is the ⚑ line in the WHY
beat below, and it is load-bearing. Without a real source for it the
title writes a cheque the video does not cash, which is worse than a
weaker title.

## Script (~250 words)

**[HOOK STACK — P71 / HF14, rebuilt]**

*Hook and gate:* "If you are running a marathon in about five weeks,
maybe Dublin, and you are still doing 400s and 800s on a Tuesday, that
session has stopped making you fitter."

*Super hook, pain twisted:* "You will keep it because it is the one
that feels hard, and it will keep leaving you flat for the long runs
that are the only thing still building anything. Five more weeks of
that and you turn up fit but hollow, with race pace feeling like a
fight from kilometre one."

*Super hook, dream outcome:* "Swap it for one marathon pace session and
by about week three, race pace stops being something you hold on to and
starts being the pace you just run at. Here is the exact session."

**[STOP DOING]**
"Delete the short, fast stuff. 400s, 800s, anything at 5k pace. The
speed is already there and you are not going to need it, and the cost
is a couple of flat days every single week from here to the start
line."

**[ON SCREEN ONLY]**
"1 marathon. Ultras: Connemara 60k, Seven Sisters 55k."

**[START DOING — the marathon pace simulator]**
"Replace it with three by five kilometres at your exact target marathon
pace. Three kilometres easy to start, then five K at race pace, one
kilometre easy float without stopping, and repeat that three times.
Two kilometres to cool down."

**[HOW IT SHOULD FEEL]**
"This is the important bit. It should sit at about a six or seven out
of ten. You should not be gasping. Your legs get a heavy, steady warmth
and you should finish feeling like you could have done one more block
if somebody made you. If you are hanging on, the pace is wrong, and
that tells you something about your goal time too."

**[WHY — this beat is what earns the hook]**
"And this is why five weeks is the line. Anything you do inside the
last two weeks is too late to land ⚑, so these next three weeks are the
last ones that change anything. After that you are only protecting what
you already have. All they need to do is teach your legs to hold race
pace while they are already tired."

**[THREAD + CTA]**
"The three hours and the thirty minutes before the gun both have their
own video on the profile. Save this one for Tuesday."

## ⚑ Checks
1. "Flat for days" replaced the central-nervous-system language, which
   is contested in the popular form. Keep it as the felt experience.
2. "Almost nothing you can use over 42k" replaced "zero return", which
   is not defensible as an absolute.
3. The 3x5km at marathon pace with 1km floats is standard and safe to
   state.

# SCRIPT 2 — The last free mistake

## OST

> **The last long run where a fuelling mistake is still free**
> *(marathon edition)*

The deadline does the work without being announced. "Still free" is the
whole stake in two words.

## Script (~260 words)

**[HOOK STACK — P71 / HF14, rebuilt]**

*Hook and gate:* "If you are running a marathon in about five weeks,
this weekend's long run is the last one where you can find out your
fuelling does not work and have it cost you nothing."

*Super hook, pain twisted:* "Get it wrong on the day instead and you
find out at thirty kilometres, in the one race you trained sixteen
weeks for, with twelve K left and absolutely nothing you can do about
it."

*Super hook, dream outcome:* "Run the test this weekend and at twenty
eight kilometres on race day you are still eating, still moving, and
going past people who are walking. It is eighty grams an hour, and here
is exactly how to take it."

**[STOP DOING]**
"Stop doing fasted long runs, and stop going out with just water. That
was useful earlier in the block. What you are training now is your
stomach, and you cannot train it on nothing."

**[START DOING — the 80 gram test]**
"On a 28 to 32 kilometre run, take a gel or a thirty gram bar every
twenty minutes, starting at twenty minutes. A mouthful of plain water
with every one, about 150 millilitres, and at least 250 milligrams of
sodium an hour ⚑. That lands you around eighty grams of carbohydrate an
hour, which is roughly what you want on the day."

**[ON SCREEN ONLY]**
"2 accountants. ⚑[N] failed batches. Still pre-launch."

**[WHY — plain, no jargon]**
"Your gut physically adds absorption capacity when you make it work,
and it takes a couple of weeks to do it ⚑. That is the entire reason
this is a deadline. Start today and it is ready. Start in race week and
it is not."

**[HOW IT SHOULD FEEL]**
"Your stomach will feel full and busy for the first hour, and that is
normal. What you are looking for is what happens at twenty eight
kilometres. If your legs still have something in them, it worked. If
you are gagging on gel number five, you have found your problem five
weeks early, which is the whole point."

**[THREAD + CTA]**
"If gels are the thing that turns on you, why that happens at 30k is
its own video on the profile. Save this before the weekend, and if you
want something for that window that is not another gel, early access
and the free race week guide are in the bio."

## ⚑ Checks
1. **"Fat adaptation is locked in" was cut.** Contested, and it is not
   needed for the argument.
2. **The two to three week gut adaptation window needs a source.** It
   is the load-bearing claim of the whole video, because it is what
   makes it a deadline.
3. **Jargon removed:** no SGLT1, no GLUT5, no "transport proteins".
   "Absorption capacity" says it in plain words.
4. **"Blood sugar never drops" was cut** as an overclaim and replaced
   with the honest version of what to look for.

# SCRIPT 3 — The niggle protocol

## OST

> **What a niggle 5 weeks out actually means**
> *(and the decision you make today)*

## The boundary, and it is not optional

This is the only script of the three that could hurt someone. It must
state its own limits **on camera, early**, and it must never sound like
a diagnosis:

- **Only** a single-spot, roughly 3 out of 10 ache that settles when
  you warm up.
- **Not** sharp pain, **not** anything that changes how you walk,
  **not** swelling, **not** bone pain.
- **See a physio.** Say it, do not imply it.

## Script (~270 words)

**[HOOK STACK — P71 / HF14, rebuilt]**

*Hook and gate:* "If you are running a marathon in about five weeks and
you have picked up a small ache this week in an Achilles, a shin or
around a knee, the next four days decide whether you are on that start
line."

*Super hook, pain twisted:* "You will run the midweek filler runs
anyway, because four days off feels like binning sixteen weeks. That is
exactly how a three out of ten ache becomes six weeks off and a race
you end up watching on Instagram."

*Super hook, dream outcome:* "Cut the right four days and it is usually
gone by the weekend with your long run still intact. It is two things,
and neither of them is rest."

**[THE BOUNDARY, said immediately]**
"This is for one specific thing. A single spot, about a three out of
ten, in an Achilles, a shin or around a kneecap, that eases once you
are warm. If it is sharp, if it changes how you walk, if there is
swelling, this is not that video and you need a physio, not a phone."

**[STOP DOING]**
"Cancel the midweek filler runs. The twelve to sixteen kilometre easy
ones that are there for volume rather than purpose. Cut your weekly
mileage in half for four days, and leave the sore spot alone. No
stretching it, no rolling it."

**[ON SCREEN ONLY]**
"1 marathon. Ultras: Connemara 60k, Seven Sisters 55k."

**[START DOING]**
"Replace the running with sixty minutes on a bike at an easy effort,
three or four out of ten, so the aerobic side keeps ticking over. Then
twice a day, five sets of forty five second heavy holds. Wall sits with
weight for a knee, heavy single leg heel raises for an Achilles. Hold,
do not move."

**[WHY]**
"Four days off running costs you nothing in fitness ⚑, and heavy holds
both settle the pain and load the tendon without any pounding ⚑. You
are not resting it, you are loading it differently."

**[HOW IT FEELS]**
"You will be restless and convinced you are losing the race. In most
cases the pain is gone inside about seventy two hours ⚑, and you have
saved a sixteen week block for the price of four easy days."

**[THREAD + CTA]**
"Save this one now rather than when you need it. And if it has not
settled in four days, that is your answer, go and get it looked at."

## ⚑ Checks
1. **"14 days of inactivity before aerobic capacity declines"** was
   softened to "four days costs you nothing", which is the defensible
   version of the same point. Source it if the number goes back in.
2. **"Turn off pain receptors" was cut.** Heavy isometrics do reduce
   tendon pain ⚑, but that is not the mechanism, and it is the line a
   physio would screenshot.
3. **"Pain drops to zero within 72 hours" became "in most cases"**, so
   it is not a promise.
4. **The boundary block is mandatory.** Do not film this without it.
