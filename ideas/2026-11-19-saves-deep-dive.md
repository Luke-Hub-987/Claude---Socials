## The pattern
1. **Every reel at 1.6% or more handed over numbers to carry into the race for your own goal time**: written on your hand or wrist, a pace per section, a gel time per half hour. Too many to remember, needed on the day: so they save it.
2. **Rules, decisions, feelings, rankings and stories sit at 0.3% to 0.8%,** however good the watch time. A rule you can say in 5 words ("wait 10 minutes", "a tenth past your longest", "10 words in one breath") is remembered, not saved. The 30K reel had our second-best skip and 0.65% saves for this reason.
3. **The save line that worked names the act:** "take a screenshot and save this" (sub-4), "write them on your wrist" (goal gone), "write those 3 numbers down" (last marathon). The 30K's "save this so the 3 steps are with you" asked them to keep something they'd already remembered.
4. **A race close by helps but isn't needed:** NYC (3 days out) and goal gone (4 days before Dublin) were near a race; sub-4 (secretly) and the fourth hour weren't.
5. **Reach doesn't make the rate:** the fourth hour had 15K reach and 2.0%; sub-4 had 4K and 2.7%.

## For the next script (P224, gate test G34)
- **Build the method as numbers per goal time**, on screen in the last stage and the last frame: 3:30, 4:00 and 4:30 rows (gel times, a split at a sign, a pace per section). One worked example aloud (4:00), the other rows on screen only (row 21: no maths in the audio).
- **The save line names the act:** "screenshot this before your race" or "write these 3 numbers on your wrist".
- **A rule can still be the hook,** but the payoff has to be numbers: the 30K reel would have been "wait 10 minutes" plus the gel and walk-break times per goal on screen.
- **No medical-tent line** (P223): the 30K's end drop (16% to 8% at 1:13) started on it.
