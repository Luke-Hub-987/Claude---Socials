## Why (checked against the ledger first, P148)
1. **The opening frame was busy. A repeat of P147 and ledger rows 1 and 14:
   my miss.** The motion graphic sits in the top band of the reel, which is
   small on a phone, and it opened on a splits screen (a list of around 15
   small numbers), with pins, a "your 3 numbers" strip along the bottom,
   then tiles reading 5:10 and 5:20, a running figure and labels. Under it,
   the title and Luke. In one second there are 6 or 7 things to look at
   and nothing big enough to read. The 5:10 against 5:20 also shows the
   answer to number 1 (ledger row 1), and neither number is in the title or
   line one (row 14).
2. **The gate was narrower than our rule. A repeat of ledger row 11 and
   P153: my miss.** "If you're already thinking about your next marathon"
   filters out everyone who isn't, which is most of the feed. The rule is
   "If you've got a marathon coming up".
3. **The topic looks back.** It's close to "after the finish line". The
   people who stayed liked it (saves and comments Higher, 9 follows), so
   the topic works for the right viewer; it just doesn't stop a stranger.

## LLM Council (P150)
- **Contrarian:** The graphics look like an ad. Polished panels read as
  "brand content" and people scroll ads. Luke in the car with a beanie is
  more native; the graphic pushes him into the bottom half.
- **Assumption-ripper:** We changed three things at once: a busy frame, a
  narrow gate and a backward-looking topic. We can't say which cost most
  without testing one at a time. Goal gone shows a motion graphic can give
  our best skip, so "motion graphics are bad" isn't proven.
- **Expansionist:** The graphics earn their place in the body: saves 1.8%
  and comments 0.3% are both Higher. Keep them from stage 1 onwards, where
  they help people save.
- **Outsider:** "In the first second I saw a phone with a list, two
  numbers, a little runner, a title and a lad in a car. I didn't know where
  to look, so I scrolled."
- **Executor:** On the next reel, frame one is one thing, big. Test it with
  trial reels: version A, Luke full screen for the first 3 seconds and the
  graphics after line one; version B, one big number or picture as the
  graphic. Keep the gate "If you've got a marathon coming up".
- **Blind cross-review:** All agree the busy first second is the main
  cause and the narrow gate the second. "Looks like an ad" is accepted as a
  risk to test, not a fact. Everyone keeps the graphics in the body.
- **Chairman:** **Keep the motion graphics, but not in the first 3
  seconds unless it's one big thing.** A/B the first frame with trial reels
  on New York. New rule P159; ledger row 22.

## Lessons
- **P159: the first second is readable in a glance.** One element, big
  enough to read on a phone in the top band, or Luke alone with the title.
  No lists, tiles, labels, progress strips or small numbers until after
  line one.
- The gate stays "If you've got a marathon coming up" (P153).
- The topic is worth a second go with a clean frame and the standard
  gate: it earned saves, comments and follows from the people who stayed.
