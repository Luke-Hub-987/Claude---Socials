## Five things the winners share

**1. A famous monster, not a topic.** The wall (5 reels, median 46K),
cramps (38.5K), quitting (31.6K). Everyone running a marathon has heard of
the wall before they've hit it; Reddit writes it as a horror story ("POV:
you hit the wall at Mile 20 💀"; "the wall" 21 mentions, "quit" 62).
*Psychology:* loss aversion. They've put months in, and the fear of it all
going wrong at 32K is bigger than any wish to go faster. A named fear also
needs no explaining in the first second, so skip stays low.
**Fuel isn't a monster:** gels are something they use, not something they
fear (fuel median 6.5K). Carb load worked because it's a schedule for the
days before the race, not a fuel topic.

**2. The clock is ticking.** 7 days before (84K), the day before hour by
hour (34.6K), the last 3 hours (24.9K), 30 minutes before (22.5K), carb
load's "Friday and Saturday" (53.1K). *Psychology:* race-week anxiety and
the need for control. In the taper they have spare time, nervous energy
and nothing to do with it ("taper" 31 mentions, "nervous" 12, "sleep" 40),
and a schedule turns worry into a list. These get the highest saves (3.2%
to 3.4%): they save it to use on the day. The topic works only when it's
an action on a date: "heavy legs" (8.6K) and "taper" (16.3K) talked about
feelings and training, with no race-day action.

**3. A moment they can picture themselves in.** First 10 minutes (46.7K),
30 minutes before, the last 3 hours, the night before. *Psychology:* mental
rehearsal. Runners already play the start over in their heads; a video
about a precise slice of the day lets them rehearse it with you. Vague
scopes ("first 5K", 11.3K; "just run the distance", 15.9K) do worse.

**4. It protects them from their own instinct.** Get ahead of the wall
(the wall starts in the first 10K), first 10 minutes (start slower than
you want), out of the wall (slow down to speed up), quit (what to do when
your head says stop). *Psychology:* the curiosity gap plus self-protection:
"the mistake I'm about to make". The counter-position is about *them*,
which is why "4 types who hit the wall" (sorting them into groups, 9.9K)
failed: viewers decided it wasn't them.

**5. Everyone running a marathon, not a slice.** Broad fears median 46K;
goal times median 13.6K; one goal is a small group (3:30 8.2K). *But* the
narrow ones bring the emails: 3:45's day brought 51, 3:30's 39 on 8.2K
views (2 to 5 emails per 1,000 views against about 1 for broad videos).
*Psychology:* a goal time is identity ("I'm a 3:45 runner"), so the right
people act on it. Broad = reach; narrow = emails.

Bonus: **tribe.** Dublin km by km had the highest share rate (1.8%): people
send it to the friends they're racing with.

## The formula
**[a famous fear or an imminent moment] + [one precise slice of race day
or race week] + [an action that goes against their instinct] + [numbers
they can save] + [for everyone running a marathon] + [posted when that
moment is days away]**

Check every idea against it. Score 1 point each; 5 or 6 is a reach video,
3 or less won't travel. Goal-time videos score 4 by design and are for
emails.
