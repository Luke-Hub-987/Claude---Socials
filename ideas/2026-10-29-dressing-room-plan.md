# The dressing-room plan: how a kids' snack became the NFL's snack, and our version

## 1. Luke's correction, and what it changes

**Right.** At club level the manager picks the team, runs the drills and
sends the WhatsApp. Nobody hands out nutrition advice. **The player
decides what's in their own bag**, so the reels speak to the player. The
coach's squad page drops to an optional extra. New rule P163.

## 2. What actually happened with Uncrustables (and what didn't)

The story people tell is "Uncrustables did an NFL deal". The story that
matters runs the other way round:

1. **It wasn't a sports product.** A frozen, crustless peanut butter and
   jam sandwich made for kids' lunchboxes. No performance claims.
2. **Players and team dietitians picked it** because it was cheap, quick
   carbs, easy on the stomach before a game, and food they'd grown up
   with.
3. **The behaviour was seen before it was paid for.** Players talked about
   it unprompted in interviews, posted it, and journalists wrote about
   teams going through huge numbers of them a week (check the exact
   numbers before quoting any).
4. **Only then did Smucker's lean in** with athlete deals and sports
   marketing. **The money followed the behaviour; it didn't create it.**
5. **It was already everywhere.** Big distribution and a low price meant
   any player could buy one at the shop on the way to training.

**The lesson isn't "sign a league". It's: be the familiar, simple carb that
athletes are seen eating, then pour money on what's already happening.**

### Why this fits ERN better than it fits most brands

**Your bar is the Irish and UK Uncrustable.** A rice crisp square is
everyone's birthday-party food. Grown athletes eating their childhood snack
before a match is exactly the picture that made Uncrustables spread, and
it's a story the EU claims rules can't stop you telling, because it isn't a
claim. Keep the rice crisp framing at the front of everything.

## 3. What's real for two founders, and what isn't

| Route | Real? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A Premier League, GAA or IRFU official partnership | **No** | Official partner deals cost far more than your whole first year's revenue (the GAA's and IRFU's existing sponsors are national retailers, banks and insurers). And Uncrustables didn't start there either |
| A Premier League club's dressing room | **Not yet** | Pro nutritionists choose, and they want products batch-tested for banned substances (Informed Sport, check the cost per batch). It's a 2028 goal, not a 2027 one |
| Inter-county GAA and AIL rugby players | **Yes** | Amateurs with big local followings, drug-tested (so batch testing matters here too), and very reachable. Check the GPA's and WGPA's rules on personal endorsements |
| Club players (GAA, soccer, rugby, hurling and camogie, ladies' football) | **Yes, through reels** | Luke's point: they choose their own snack. Trial reels first (P156), so the marathon grid isn't disrupted (P72) |
| Clubs as a shop | **Yes** | Every GAA club fundraises all year. A club fundraiser box (the club keeps a cut) puts the bar in the club bar, the shop and the kit bags, and gives the club a reason to push it |
| The shop near the pitch | **Yes, later** | Food Academy is the route into SuperValu, and SuperValu already sponsors the GAA championships (check), so the shelf and the sport sit in the same place |

## 4. The plan, in four stages

### Stage 1: now to samples (no bar needed)
- **Player reels, as trial reels.** The wall formula on the match clock:
  "Where the last 10 minutes of a match are actually lost", "What to eat on
  match day, hour by hour", "What to do at half-time when your legs are
  gone". Every number dietitian-checked first, the same way FACTS.md works.
- **The list of 20.** Inter-county and club GAA (men's and women's), AIL
  rugby and League of Ireland players with real local followings who
  already post their food and training. Follow and talk to them now, and
  ask for nothing.
- **A quote from Informed Sport** (or an equivalent batch-testing scheme),
  so the cost is known before you need it.
- **The match-day plan on the site**, built for the player (kick-off time,
  minutes played, body weight for adults only), with the bar's slot 30
  minutes before throw-in and at half-time.

### Stage 2: samples
- **Send boxes to the 20, with no contract and no ask.** Then watch.
  **Whoever posts it unprompted is your Uncrustables signal.** That's the
  only test that tells you if athletes choose it.
- **One or two club squads taste it** across 3 or 4 matches.

### Stage 3: launch season (2027)
- **Pay the 3 to 5 who used it unprompted** (small retainers or product
  plus a fee: check what county players charge locally).
- **The dressing-room shot as the brand's picture:** players with the bar
  before throw-in. Ask every player and club to send theirs.
- **Club fundraiser boxes** with 5 to 10 clubs near you first (Sligo and
  Galway), then wider.

### Stage 4: when it's already happening
- Once players are seen with it and clubs are reordering: a county board
  or AIL club partnership, SuperValu through Food Academy, then pro
  nutritionists with batch-tested product. **Pay to amplify only what's
  already true.**

## 5. LLM Council (P150): how do we do an Uncrustables?

- **Contrarian:** Uncrustables worked because it was cheap and on every
  shelf, so players could buy it themselves. ERN will be dearer, direct to
  consumer at first, and on no shelves. A player who likes it can't pick
  one up on the way to training. Seeding athletes costs product and gets
  you nothing if they don't post. County players can have commercial
  limits. And a single failed batch test would end you in elite sport.
- **Assumption-ripper:** We assume Uncrustables succeeded through the NFL;
  the NFL was a megaphone on something already popular. We assume athletes
  want a "sports" product; Uncrustables proves they want familiar food.
  We assume reels reach club players; we've never tested a team-sport reel.
  And Luke's correction kills our own assumption that the coach decides.
- **Expansionist:** The rice crisp square is the perfect Uncrustables
  story: childhood food, grown athletes. Club fundraising is a ready-made
  sales force in every parish. Women's GAA and rugby players are often
  more reachable, post more, and are under-served by brands. Half-time is
  a second moment in every match.
- **Outsider:** "Where would I buy it? If it's not in the shop beside the
  pitch, I'll grab a Lucozade."
- **Executor:** Monday morning (2 Nov): write the list of 20 athletes and
  follow them; email Informed Sport for a quote; brief 4 GAA trial reels
  on the match clock; message 3 club players you know to ask what they
  ate before their last match.

**Blind cross-review:**
- The Outsider and the Contrarian land on the same weak point: **where to
  buy it.** Accepted. That's why club fundraiser boxes and the Food Academy
  route aren't extras; they're the Uncrustables "it's in the shop" part.
- "Seed with no contract and watch who posts" is accepted by all as the
  cheapest real test.
- Batch testing becomes a must before any inter-county or pro athlete is
  paid or seen with the bar.
- League partnerships are ruled out until the behaviour exists.
- Women's teams are accepted as a first target inside the list of 20.

**Chairman's verdict:** **Don't chase leagues. Copy what Uncrustables did
first: be the familiar carb athletes are seen eating.** Reels to the
player (as trial reels), 20 athletes seeded with no strings when samples
exist, batch testing before any elite athlete is involved, and club
fundraiser boxes plus Food Academy so a player can actually buy it. Pay
only the athletes who already use it. A league or club deal comes last, on
top of something that's already happening.
