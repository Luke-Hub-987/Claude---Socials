## The three findings

### 1. The save card worked, and saves were the whole point

Saves were the one metric still marked Lower after the not hungry
video, and the fix was a card worth pausing on. This video ran two:
the three checks as a decision rule, and the goal pace table.

**Saves went from 34 to 90.** That is the largest single-metric move
in the data set and it came from the thing we changed on purpose.

**A table or a decision rule is what gets saved.** A fact does not.
Every script from here gets one card that is a rule or a table, held
long enough to screenshot (P84).

### 2. Instagram and TikTok moved in opposite directions, and the gate
is why

| | IG | TikTok |
|---|---|---|
| Not hungry | 3,681 | 3,567 |
| Goal pace | **5,692** | **1,633** |

**Combined reach was flat.** The mix moved.

"Anyone who has just done a long run" is a wide gate. "Anyone training
for a marathon with a goal time" is a narrow one. Instagram's audience
for this account is already marathon-shaped, built from marathon
videos and collabs, so a narrow gate concentrates rather than costs.
TikTok's For You was 99.8% of traffic, which is a cold, broad audience
where a narrow gate is a wall.

**This is not a reason to widen the topics.** The narrow gate produced
the best saves, the best watch time and the first comments, and the
combined total did not drop. It is a reason to expect the platforms to
diverge and to stop reading one as the verdict.

### 3. TikTok lost them at 0:02, and the OST is the suspect

Most viewers stopped at **0:02**, before a single word of the hook
landed. At two seconds all that exists is the cover.

**The OST wrapped to three lines and the play button sat on top of
it.** "How to know what marathon time you are in shape for" is eleven
words, which read fine on paper and became three lines of small text
over a stock photo on a phone.

Compare the winners: *What to do in the last 3 hours before your
marathon* renders in two lines at a much bigger size.

That is P89, and it is free to fix.
