# Why ours: what the bar has to do that a square, porridge, Lucozade or a gel can't

## 1. The honest starting point

**On carbs alone, it can't be different enough.** Your own line, "carbs are
carbs", is true, and it means a bigger, dearer rice crisp square loses to
the 70c one. No recipe tweak changes that. The difference has to be
designed into four things at once: **what it does in the stomach, its
shape, what it means, and the price per use.** And it has to be decided
**now, before the recipe is locked**, because two of the four are physical.

## 2. What each one actually is, and where it fails

| What they have now | When they eat it | What it does well | Where it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| A supermarket rice crisp square | Whenever | Cheap, tasty, familiar | Made as a treat, not a dose: check the pack for carbs, fat and salt against ours. Squashes and sticks in a kit bag |
| Porridge | The meal, 2 to 3 hours before | Slow, filling, cheap | **Not a competitor for our slot.** Nothing covers the last 30 to 60 minutes, and most players eat nothing then |
| Lucozade | The car, the dressing room, half-time | It's everywhere; drink and carbs in one | A lot of liquid sloshing before you run; a big bottle at half-time |
| A gel | Runners, and some players | Fast, exact | The texture runners hate (FACTS.md), and nobody grew up wanting one |
| **Nothing** | The last hour, and half-time | Free | **The real competitor for most players** |

**What that table says:** porridge is a partner, not a rival (the meal hours
before, then the bar). The fight is for the last 30 minutes and half-time,
against a square, a bottle, a gel or nothing.

## 3. The four differences (one headline, three that back it up)

### The headline: two halves (one bar, two moments)
**Two pieces in one wrapper, like a Twix.** Half 30 minutes before throw-in,
half at half-time. For runners, both halves 30 minutes before the start
(never during the race, P134).

- **It matches how every match is played:** two halves, so two moments.
  Nothing on the shelf is made for that.
- **It's the reason the pack can say something a square can't,** without a
  health claim: "One before throw-in. One at half-time."
- **It spreads without anyone posting** (Luke's point: players don't post
  their meals). The other half gets handed to a teammate in the dressing
  room. **Hand to hand, not feed to feed,** is how a product spreads
  through a squad.
- **It's the picture for the reels:** the snap at half-time.
- Two pieces in one wrapper is easier to make than a scored bar that has
  to snap cleanly (rice crisp bars crumble). Ask the food scientist which
  is possible.

### The spec on the front (what it does in the stomach)
Say the numbers, never the category (ost-cashvertising.md): the carbs per
half, the fat, the salt. **Light in the stomach** (low fat and fibre, so it
doesn't sit), **a pinch of salt** for anyone sweating, and **a dose they
can count on** (your 30g, as two halves of 15g, or more if the food
scientist says it still sits well). Put the supermarket square's numbers
next to yours when you check them: if yours isn't plainly better on the
pack, it's just a dearer square.

### It survives a kit bag
Doesn't melt in a car, doesn't squash under boots, isn't sticky on the
fingers, opens with cold hands. A square in cling film fails all four. It
sounds small; it's what players notice every week.

### The meaning: match day's ritual
Players are superstitious: the same socks, the same meal, the same seat in
the dressing room. **Be part of the ritual and you're never swapped**
("nothing new on match day", same as race day). And the name already does
the work: **"the engine room" is the midfield.** Every player wants to be
the engine room in the last 10 minutes. The line on the pack can be as
simple as: **Fuel the engine room.**

### And the price per use
Players are replacing a purchase, not adding one. **The cost of one match's
worth (both halves) should sit close to the bottle of Lucozade they buy
now** (check shelf prices). Dearer than that and it's a treat; close to it
and it's an easy swap.

## 4. The brief for the food scientist (send this week, before the recipe locks)

1. **Format:** two pieces in one wrapper (or one bar that snaps cleanly in
   two). Which can you make?
2. **Dose:** carbs per piece and per bar. Our current line is 30g a bar.
   Can each piece carry 15g or more and still be light in the stomach?
3. **Light:** as little fat and fibre as the recipe allows. What's the
   lowest you can go and keep the taste and texture?
4. **Salt:** a pinch, enough to be on the label. How much without tasting
   salty?
5. **The kit bag test:** survives a warm car, a squash under boots, cold
   hands; not sticky.
6. **Shelf life** at room temperature, and what it does to cost.
7. **Allergens:** is the rice crisp gluten-free (barley malt)?
8. **Cost** of each of the above against the current recipe.

## 5. How we'll know it's different enough

Not on paper. **The squad swap test** when samples exist: one squad, 3 or 4
matches, the bar in place of whatever they had. After each match, three
questions:
1. What did you have before throw-in and at half-time last week?
2. Would you swap it for this every match?
3. At [price], would you buy it yourself?

**The signal is the swap, not a post.** If most of a squad says yes to
question 2 and a fair share to question 3, the product is different
enough. If not, the recipe or the price changes before launch, not after.

## 6. LLM Council (P150): what makes the bar the one they choose?

- **Contrarian:** "Two halves" is a gimmick if the bar's ordinary. Half-time
  is 10 to 15 minutes with a dry mouth; players may not want to chew
  anything. Twin packs cost more to wrap. A pinch of salt and low fat are
  invisible to the player. And Lucozade is in every shop; you're not.
- **Assumption-ripper:** We assume players want to eat before a match;
  most eat nothing in the last hour, so we're creating a habit, not
  stealing one. We assume half-time eating is a need; test it. Luke
  assumes the recipe has to be "so different"; the data says the
  difference people feel is usually the format, the ritual and the price,
  not the formula.
- **Expansionist:** Two halves fits every sport played in halves (GAA,
  soccer, rugby, hockey) with one product. The handed-over half is a
  sampling machine inside every dressing room. "Fuel the engine room" sells
  to every midfielder who's ever heard the phrase.
- **Outsider:** "Why would I pay more than a Kellogg's square? Is it just a
  bigger square? Tell me on the front, in one line, what it does that the
  square doesn't."
- **Executor:** Monday morning: send the brief in section 4 to the food
  scientist. Buy the competitors (a supermarket rice crisp square, a
  Lucozade, two gels, a flapjack), line the packs up against ours, and
  work out the price per 30g of carbs for each. Ask 3 players what they
  have before throw-in and at half-time, and whether they'd eat something
  at half-time.

**Blind cross-review:**
- The Outsider's question becomes the test for the pack front: one line
  that says what the square doesn't. "One before throw-in. One at
  half-time" passes; "a performance carb bar" fails.
- The half-time doubt is fair: the 3 player questions and the squad test
  settle it. If players won't eat at half-time, the two halves still work
  as "one now, one for a teammate", and the pre-match slot stands alone.
- "Invisible" spec is answered by printing the numbers next to the
  square's (the accountants' way: show the sums).
- The price per use against Lucozade is accepted by all as the swap test.

**Chairman's verdict:** **The bar wins on format, ritual and price per use,
not on carbs.** Make it two halves in one wrapper (one before, one at
half-time), light in the stomach, with the numbers on the front, tough
enough for a kit bag, priced close to the Lucozade they buy now, under the
line "Fuel the engine room". Send the brief to the food scientist before
the recipe locks, and let a squad's swap decide.
