# Who actually hits the marathon wall, at ~8 hours

**Instagram (ERN side)**: 3,415 views (3,164 IG + 251 FB), 2,419
viewers, 31s average on a 1:23 cut, 44 likes, 2 comments, 1 repost,
**17 saves**, 1 follow. Skip 32.1% (Lower), share 0.7% (Lower), like
1.7% (Lower), **save 0.7% (Lower)**, comment 0.1% (Higher). It ran
above the typical reel for three hours, then fell below it.

**TikTok (ERN)**: 820 views, 26.5s average, **6.74% watched full**
(second best ever), 2 follows, 2 saves, 1 comment.

**The front held** (skip in line with cramp). **The watch held.** **The

comments came back Lower. People watched it and did not keep it.

## Why, in order of weight

### 1. Four types of runner is not a timeline. Each viewer got half a video

Every top video is a sequence **every viewer goes through**: the last
3 hours, the last 30 minutes, every 10K, the first sign to the way
back. The viewer is the subject of all four stages, so the whole video
is their plan, and they save it.

"Who hits the wall" sorted people into groups. **A woman is not in
stage two. A 3:15 runner is not in stage four. A first-timer is not in
stage one.** Most viewers were the subject of one or two stages and a
bystander for the rest. That is a fact video, and fact videos do not
get saved (tangents 1.1%, gels count 1.0%).

Luke said it three days ago: the stages that work are the ones broken
down like the wall video. I followed the letter (one axis, numbered)
and missed the point (one axis **the viewer travels along**).

### 2. The gate shut out the biggest group

"If you are running a marathon in the next few weeks **and you are
chasing a PB**" narrowed the proven gate. First-timers, a large share of
this audience, were told in the first three seconds it was not for
them.

### 3. The advice was a repeat

Five to ten seconds a K, carbs every twenty minutes, race the pace your
long runs proved: all three were filmed in the last ten days. For
anyone who has seen those, there was nothing new to save. The new part
was statistics, which earn a share at best.

### 4. Numbers dense, outcome thin

Four million, 36%, 23%, 28%, 17%, five hours, thirty K, ten K, half an
hour. Ralston: do not make people think hard. The wall video had one
idea per stage.

### 5. The setting broke the package

A stairwell, a shirt and earphones, at 9:40pm. The two biggest videos
were in the car in running kit after a run. One video cannot prove it
mattered; it is the second miss in a row away from the car (cramp was
indoors), and both under-ran the car videos.

## What it got right

- **TikTok 6.74% full watch**, second only to the wall (9.41%). 1:23
  and a numbered list held people to the end.
- **Comment rate Higher.** Men against women did draw argument.

## The lesson (P103)

**Broad works when everyone is the subject, not when everyone is
sorted.** The wall reached 110K viewers because every runner, and every
non-runner, lives through 0 to 10, 10 to 20, 20 to 30. Broadening by
statistics about groups gets watched and not kept.

**My recommendation, my miss.** The spec in the full analysis rated
this 10 out of 10 because none of its lines asked "is every viewer in
every stage?". That line is now on the checklist.
