# What Instagram's head says ranks a reel, and what we change

## What he said (parts 3 and 4 of his ranking series)
1. **Instagram ranks on proxies for interest:** how much of the reel people
   watch, how likely they are to send it to a friend, how likely they are
   to like it. **Look at the rates, not the counts:** the percentage of
   viewers who liked, sent, and the average watch time.
2. **Experiment.** What works now may not in a month. Find the overlap
   between who you are and what finds an audience. **Trial reels** go only
   to non-followers: a low-pressure way to test.
3. **Post as often as you can sustain.** More posts means more chances of
   a hit, but a pace that burns you out is worse.
4. (Part 3, for viewers) People can shape their feed: a **Favorites** list
   (those accounts go to the top of feed every time), "Not interested",
   and the topic sliders.

## What we already do
- Every review reads the rates in the order Instagram's own insights list
  them for reach: skip, share, like, save.
- Watch time against length is in every review.

## What changes
1. **Sends come before saves for reach.** His proxies are watch, send and
   like; saves are fourth on our own insights screen. P152 (saves need
   something too long to remember) stays for value and emails, but **every
   video now needs a reason to send it,** said in the audio: who would they
   send it to, and why that person (Dublin km by km, 1.8% shares, was sent
   to running partners). Our recent share rates (0.1% to 0.4%) are the gap.
2. **Trial reels become standard for the reach reel each week.** Two
   versions with different first lines and opening frames, posted as trial
   reels the evening before; the one with the lower skip and higher watch
   goes to the profile. Only the first 10 seconds are filmed twice.
3. **A pace Luke and Jamie can keep: 4 reels a week**, with one reach reel
   built to the wall formula, plus the podcast. The near-daily run over
   race weekend was a sprint for a date, not a pace.
4. **Favorites, for followers:** a story once a month ("add us to your
   Favorites so you don't miss race-week videos"). Stories only, never in a
   reel's audio (the CTA stays one sentence).

## LLM Council (P150)
- **Contrarian:** It's generic advice from the platform, partly PR. Trial
  reels double the filming of the hook and can split the best version's
  first day. Four a week means fewer chances at a hit, his own argument.
- **Assumption-ripper:** We assumed saves were the key signal (we built
  P152 on them). He doesn't list saves; Instagram's insights put them
  fourth. Our biggest reel (the wall) had 1.3K saves *and* it was the
  most sent. We also assume Luke can keep up near-daily: nobody asked him.
- **Expansionist:** Trial reels let us test the things the ledger can't
  settle: reframe or open loop, the save prompt in the hook or after
  stage 1, a motion graphic or a photo in frame one. Favorites turns
  followers into first-hour viewers, the early signal.
- **Outsider:** "I send a reel when it's about someone I know: 'this is you
  at 32K'. I save one when I need it later."
- **Executor:** From this week: the walk (Wed) is the first trial-reel
  test (two first lines, posted Tue evening). Every script gets a send line
  and a "who would they send it to" check. Reviews lead with the rates.
