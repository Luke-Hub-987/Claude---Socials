**Not 100%, and nobody honestly could be.** Fifteen videos, with topic,
title and structure moving together, cannot guarantee the next one.
What I can say is that this is the **closest match to the account's

## The secret (Thiel), v2 on Luke's input

**The day before, most runners treat their body like glass: couch,
panic stretching, a huge dinner. That is how you wake up heavy.** A
short jog, grazing instead of feasting, and a light early dinner is
how you wake up light. It is contested (rest against shakeout), which
is the comment lever.

| Stage | Opens on the unexpected | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Resting completely is how you get heavy legs | |
| MORNING | Run the day before | 15 to 20 min easy, 3 or 4 x 15s strides at marathon pace; no new deep stretching |
| THROUGH THE DAY | Graze, do not feast; do not flood yourself with water | Small low-fibre carbs every few hours, biggest at lunch; water with a pinch of salt |
| DINNER | Light and early beats the pasta party | Half five or six, white rice or pasta, plain sauce |
| BEDTIME | Sleeping badly is normal | Lay it all out; bank sleep beforehand; rest if awake |

**[TWO. THROUGH THE DAY]**
"Through the day, graze. Have small, plain, low fibre carbs every few hours, like white rice, plain toast or pretzels, with your biggest meal at lunch. "At dinner, keep it light and early, around half five or six. Fat, fibre and a lot of protein slow your stomach down, so have white rice or pasta with a plain sauce." ⚑1

**[FOUR. BEDTIME]**

## Luke's input, checked

**Kept:** the glass-vase framing (now the hook), the shakeout with
strides, no new deep stretching, sip and graze, the light dinner by
half five, don't flood with water, the flat lay.

**Changed or cut, and why:**

1. **"Your glycogen tanks were already filled 48 hours ago" and "carb
   loading should be finished two days before": out.** The standard
   guidance puts carb loading in the last 36 to 48 hours, so **the day
   before is a loading day.** The fix is how: little and often, low
   fibre, biggest at lunch, which is exactly "graze". The night-before
   script made the same correction.
2. **"Sitting still puts your central nervous system to sleep… the
   electrical pathways have powered down": out.** Not a defensible
   mechanism. A short jog with strides the day before is standard
   coaching practice, and the script says what it does in plain words:
   "wakes your legs up".
3. **"Deep static stretching physically weakens your muscles… ruins
   your spring tension… tightness is stored elastic energy": changed.**
   Long static holds do reduce strength briefly, for minutes rather
   than until the next morning, so it is not a reason on its own. The
   honest reason is the oldest race-week rule: nothing new. "If it is
   not in your training, race eve is not the day to start."
4. **Kipchoge: cut.** "None of them are resting in bed, they all do a
   15 to 20 minute shakeout" is a claim about a named athlete's
   routine I cannot verify (standing rule).
5. **"Burns virtually zero stored energy": softened** to "barely
   touches your fuel". Twenty minutes easy uses a little, and the day's
   grazing replaces it.
6. **"Chugging plain water dilutes your blood sodium… leaving you
   dehydrated by race morning": changed.** Drinking far more than you
   need mostly means your kidneys send it straight out, so you are up
   in the night. It does not leave you dehydrated. A little salt helps
   you hold on to what you drink (ACSM fluid guidance).
7. **The timing chip:** most big races now carry it in the bib, so the
   line is "bib pinned on".

## ⚑ Science

| # | Claim | Source / status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fat, fibre and a lot of protein slow stomach emptying; a big rich dinner can send you to the portaloo | Checked for the night-before script (v3). "Can be", never "is the main reason" |
| 2 | Biggest carb meal at lunch the day before; dinner a light top-up, early | Standard sports-dietitian race-eve practice, consistent with the night-before script ("tonight's meal is a light top-up", "around five or six"). Said as advice |
| 3 | A day on your feet adds up; a short easy jog at most | Practical advice, no study claimed |
| 4 | Nearly 300 elite athletes, almost two thirds slept worse before a big competition, mostly from thinking about it | **Juliff et al., 2015, J Sci Med Sport**: 283 elite Australian athletes, 64% reported worse sleep before an important competition at least once in 12 months; main reason thoughts about the competition (83.5%). Nothing claimed about performance |

| 5 | Day-before shakeout with strides | Standard coaching practice; said as advice, no study claimed |
| 6 | Nothing new, including deep stretching | Race-week rule; acute stretch-induced strength loss is short-lived, so it is not used as the reason |
| 7 | Overdrinking is excreted; a little salt helps retention | ACSM position stand on exercise and fluid replacement (2007) |

**Not claimed:** grams per kilo, that one bad night has no effect on
the race, anything about Luke, anything named.
