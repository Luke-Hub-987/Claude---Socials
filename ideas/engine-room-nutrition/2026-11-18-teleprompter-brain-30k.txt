Never decide to walk at 30K of your marathon until you've waited 10 minutes. That voice saying just walk this hill isn't weakness. Cyclists who rode 3 hours without carbs saw their blood sugar fall by almost half, and the effort felt harder.

There are 3 things to do the moment it starts, and each one is harder than the last. First, know the voice when it comes: you'll get going again shortly, I'll never do this again, why am I doing this. That's a fuel gauge reading low, so treat it as your cue to act. Save this so the 3 steps are with you when it starts.

Second, take the gel that's due, even if you're sick of the texture. On a 4-hour marathon, that's the 2:50 gel, right as the voice starts. Wash it down with a few sips of water if a station's close. The gel you least want at 30K is the one your head needs most.

Send this to the friend who walked at 30K and blamed their head.

Third, and the hardest, make no decision about walking for 10 minutes. The sugar starts reaching your blood within minutes. So keep running, 15 to 20 seconds a K slower if you need to, with shorter steps. Your watch already shows the race time, so after the 2:50 gel, decide nothing before 3:00.

The last 10K is where you find out whose voice it was.

If it was fuel, you'll be counting down the 10K signs instead of arguing with yourself. Your last gel on a 4-hour clock is the 3:20 one. But if you're dizzy, shivering or weaving across the road, that's more than fuel, so stop and find the medical tent.
