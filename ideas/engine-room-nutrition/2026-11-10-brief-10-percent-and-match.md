# Two briefs: the 10% rule, and the last 20 minutes of a match

## 1. The 10% rule

### The science, vetted (keep, change, cut)
- **Keep: "there's no good evidence it protects you."** The one trial of the rule
  (Buist 2008, GRONORUN, 532 novice runners): a 13-week graded plan built on the
  10% rule against a standard 8-week plan; injuries 20.8% against 20.3%, no
  difference. Source: PEDro record https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/20220
- **Keep, with care: one run matters more than the week.** Frandsen et al. 2025,
  BJSM, about 5,200 runners' watch data: injuries went up when a single run was
  more than 10% longer than the longest run in the last 30 days; the
  week-to-week ratio showed no link. Exploratory, self-reported injuries, so
  "linked to", never "causes". Source: https://blogs.bmj.com/bjsm/?p=11751
- **Keep as context only: big jumps.** Nielsen 2014 (JOSPT, 874 novice runners):
  more than 30% over 2 weeks looked riskier than under 10%, not statistically
  significant (P = .07). Not in the audio.
- **Cut: "rubbish".** True as "it doesn't do what people think", but the honest
  claim is that it counts the wrong thing. No injury percentages in the audio
  (the summaries disagree on the bands).
- **For the gate chat:** add the Buist and Frandsen lines to FACTS before
  scripting (this chat doesn't edit FACTS).

## 2. The match: the last 20 minutes

### Why "the last 20 minutes"
One clock that fits all three sports, so no viewer is sorted out (the match
trial's photos didn't fit the soccer clock): soccer 70 to 90, rugby 60 to 80,
GAA 50 to 70. Every player knows the moment the legs go.

### The science, vetted
- **Keep:** Krustrup 2006 (Med Sci Sports Exerc, 31 Danish fourth-division
  players, muscle biopsies): about 47% of leg muscle fibres were empty or almost
  empty of glycogen after the game; sprints were slower after the game and after
  intense spells. Source: https://www.pure.fo/en/publications/muscle-and-blood-metabolites-during-a-soccer-game-implications-fo/
- **Change:** the sprint drop didn't track total glycogen; the fibre-level
  emptying is the likely reason. So say "about half the fibres in your legs
  can be close to empty by the final whistle", never "you run out of fuel".
- **Needs Luke:** the hour before kick-off (what, grams, when) and half-time,
  for a club player. No grams until he sends them.
