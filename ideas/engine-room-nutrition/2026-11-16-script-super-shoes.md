## Caption
Everyone says £250 carbon shoes take minutes off a marathon. At the sharp end they do, but at 4-hour pace the research is split, and some runners get nothing back.

What the studies found: at fast speeds (13 to 18 km/h), super shoes cut the energy cost of running by about 2.7 to 4.2%. In a 2023 study of 16 runners (Joubert and others), the average saving fell to about 1.4% at 12 km/h and 0.9% at 10 km/h, where it wasn't significant, and about a third of the runners used more energy in the super shoe at the slower speed. A 2026 study of 14 recreational runners (Bolliger and others) found an average saving of about 2.5%, with no drop at slower speeds (it didn't report how many runners did better or worse). 4-hour pace is about 10.6 km/h, right between them. So for some runners they're worth minutes, and for others almost nothing.

The test before you keep a pair: borrow some, or buy from a shop that takes them back after a run. Run the same 5K loop on two different days at your easy-run heart rate, once in your old shoes and once in the new ones, and compare the average pace on your watch. Same effort, no quicker pace: send them back.

The minutes that cost nothing: run the first quarter of the race (about the first 10K) 5 to 10 seconds a K slower than goal pace. Your watch reads about 1% long, so here's what it should say for the first 10K, the clock at the 10K sign, and your gels (30 g, first at 20 minutes, then every 30, none in the last 20). 3:30: between 5:00 and 5:05 a K (8:03 to 8:11 a mile), about 51:00 at 10K, gels from 0:20 to 2:50. 3:45: between 5:20 and 5:25 (8:35 to 8:43 a mile), about 54:15, gels from 0:20 to 3:20. Sub-4: between 5:40 and 5:45 (9:07 to 9:15 a mile), about 57:30, gels from 0:20 to 3:20. 4:30: between 6:25 and 6:30 (10:20 to 10:28 a mile), about 1:05, gels from 0:20 to 3:50. With 20 to 25 g gels, take one every 20 minutes; with 40 g gels, every 40.

In Dublin I passed runner after runner wearing them. The shoes might buy you minutes, but a slower start gives you them for free.
