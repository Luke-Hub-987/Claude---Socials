## Script

"If you've said 'I don't need gels', the first 2 hours of a 4-hour marathon agree with you, and that's the lie. By 2 hours about half your stored carbs are gone. The next 2 hours pay for it, but the fix starts early."

**[HOURS 1 AND 2]**

"By 2 hours you've burned about half the carbs you store, but you feel completely fine. That's with no carbs at all, so no gels, no drink mix and no sweets. At this pace you burn over 100 grams an hour, but you only start with about 500. Save this and screenshot the gel clock, so you've got your times."

**[HOUR 3]**

"About 3 hours in, around 32K, the stores in your legs run short. You're trying just as hard, but the pace on your watch keeps sliding. In one lab study, trained cyclists with no carbs were done at about 3 hours, but fed carbs, they lasted about 4."

**[HOUR 4]**

"Your head goes next, because blood sugar drops too. In a 3-hour study with no sugar, it fell by almost half, so the same effort felt harder. The first 2 hours feel free, but the last hour sends the bill."

"So the fix has to start while you still feel great."

**[THE FIX]**

"Take your first gel at 20 minutes, then one every 30 minutes, with a few sips of water. With 30 gram gels that's 60 grams an hour, but drink mix or sweets count too. The sugar burns alongside your stores, so there's still some left when you reach 32K. On a 4-hour marathon your last one goes in at 3:20. If you miss one, let it go, and never take 2 at once."

"If your running mate always has a spare gel for you, send them this, because they've been right all along."

## Caption
"I don't need gels" feels true for the first 2 hours of a 4-hour marathon. You store about 500 g of carbs, and at 4-hour pace you burn roughly 110 to 140 g an hour, about 55 to 70% of your energy. By 2 hours about half is gone, and you feel fine. About 3 hours in, around 32K, the stores in your legs run short. In the lab, trained cyclists with no carbs tired at about 3 hours; fed carbs, they went to about 4 (Coyle, 1986). In another small study of cyclists, blood sugar fell by almost half over 3 hours with no sugar, and the same effort felt harder (Nybo, 2003).

Your gel clock with 30 g gels, first at 20 minutes, then every 30, none in the last 20 minutes. 3:30: 0:20, 0:50, 1:20, 1:50, 2:20, 2:50. 4:00: 0:20, 0:50, 1:20, 1:50, 2:20, 2:50, 3:20. 4:30: 0:20, 0:50, 1:20, 1:50, 2:20, 2:50, 3:20, 3:50. With 20 to 25 g gels, take one every 20 minutes; with 40 g gels, every 40. Each one with a few sips of water. Drink mix and jelly sweets count; it's the carbs, not the gel. Missed one? Let it go, never 2 together.
