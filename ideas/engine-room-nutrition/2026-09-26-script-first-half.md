# How to pace the first half of a marathon

## Why this one

**It is the direct sequel to the second biggest video on the
account.** Goal pace told 19.8K people what time they are in shape
for. The very next question every one of them has is how to run the
first half at it without blowing the second.

**It passes all three compounder tests (P92):**

| Test | |
|---|---|
| Marathon in line one | Yes |
| The viewer's race outcome at stake | Yes: the second half, the finish time |
| Line one is a query, and the OST gives nothing away | Yes: "how to pace the first half of a marathon" |

**It is a clock moment**, the first half, which is the other thing the
two biggest videos share. And it is a different video from the first
5k one: that was the adrenaline start, this is the whole 21K and the
halfway check.

**It carries both devices.** The share fact ("you cannot bank time")
is the first spoken sentence. The save is three numbers and a check,
with the save prompt where they land.

**It is ERN's argument without a pitch.** Running ahead of pace burns
fuel faster than it buys speed, so the first half is a fuelling
decision as much as a pacing one.

**[HOOK. Value in the first sentence, ~15 seconds]**
"If you are running a marathon in the next few weeks, the biggest
mistake you can make is trying to bank time in the first half. Every
minute you get ahead before halfway, you pay back after thirty two,

people who did."

**[ONE. WHY BANKING FAILS]**
"In the first ten K, adrenaline hides the effort. Five or ten seconds a
K too fast feels like nothing, but it shifts your body further onto
sugar ⚑, and you only carry a fixed amount of it. Spend it early and
it is gone by thirty two.

When Kipchoge broke the world record in Berlin in 2018, he ran the
second half thirty three seconds faster than the first ⚑."

Five K to halfway, right on goal pace, with your carbs every twenty
minutes.

On a four hour goal that is five forty one a K, so the first five at
around five fifty, and halfway at about two hours."

## ⚑ The science, checked

Luke supplied it. The plan is sound and the Kipchoge example is
accurate. **Five things were changed on the way in**, each for the same
reason: a precise claim nobody can check is the cardiac drift failure.

**1. "Every minute you bank costs you four minutes after 32K" became
"you pay back after thirty two, with interest."** The one to four
ratio is a popular coaching line with no source behind it. It is a
great sentence and it is exactly the kind of number somebody corrects
in the comments. "With interest" keeps the meaning and loses the
exposure.

**2. "Forces your body to burn pure sugar instead of fat" became
"shifts your body further onto sugar."** Running faster changes the
mix, it does not switch fuel sources over. "Pure sugar" is wrong.

**3. "Guarantees a violent crash" is out.** Nothing guarantees it.
"Spend it early and it is gone by thirty two" says the same thing
honestly.

**4. Kipchoge, correct and now dated.** Berlin 2018: 2:01:39, halves of
61:06 and 60:33, so the second half was 33 seconds faster. Checked.
**Say "in 2018".** It is no longer the world record, and his 2022
Berlin record was run the other way round, slower second half, so an
undated "his world record" invites a correction. "He ran the second
half faster because he was conserving sugar while everyone else was
dying" is also out: he ran most of the second half alone. The split is
the fact, and it is enough.

**Correcting myself on this.** In v1 I wrote "do not claim world
records are run with negative splits, Kipchoge's Berlin record was
not". That was the 2022 record. The 2018 one was a negative split, and
that is the one Luke used.

**5. Breathing rhythm dropped for the sentence test.** The goal pace
video already taught the full sentence check to 19.8K people. Using
the same test keeps the two videos consistent and saves twenty words.

**Flags still open:**

- **"Five to ten seconds a K slower"** ⚑. His earlier science said
  ten to fifteen. This is the newer, and it has to match whatever he
  said in the first 5k video.
- **"Within thirty to sixty seconds at halfway"** ⚑ is a rule of thumb.
  Delivered as a target, not a law.
- **Worked example.** 5:41 plus five to ten seconds is 5:46 to 5:51,
  so "around five fifty". Halfway comes out at about 2:00:34, so
  "about two hours" is honest. **No finish time prediction.**
- **The failed-batch count** ⚑[N].
