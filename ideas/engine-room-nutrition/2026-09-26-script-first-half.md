# How to pace the first half of a marathon

## Why this one

**It is the direct sequel to the second biggest video on the
account.** Goal pace told 19.8K people what time they are in shape
for. The very next question every one of them has is how to run the
first half at it without blowing the second.

**It passes all three compounder tests (P92):**

| Test | |
|---|---|
| Marathon in line one | Yes |
| The viewer's race outcome at stake | Yes: the second half, the finish time |
| Line one is a query, and the OST gives nothing away | Yes: "how to pace the first half of a marathon" |

**It is a clock moment**, the first half, which is the other thing the
two biggest videos share. And it is a different video from the first
5k one: that was the adrenaline start, this is the whole 21K and the
halfway check.

**It carries both devices.** The share fact ("you cannot bank time")
is the first spoken sentence. The save is three numbers and a check,
with the save prompt where they land.

**It is ERN's argument without a pitch.** Running ahead of pace burns
fuel faster than it buys speed, so the first half is a fuelling
decision as much as a pacing one.

## OST

nothing depends on a whisper.

**[ONE. WHY BANKING FAILS]**
"Run faster than goal pace and your fuel use climbs faster than your
speed does ⚑, because the harder you go, the more your body leans on
sugar instead of fat. Every second a K you are ahead in the first half
is fuel you will not have at thirty K."

**[TWO. THE NUMBERS. The save moment]**
"This is the bit to save.

First five K, ten to fifteen seconds a K slower than goal pace ⚑. It
will feel ridiculous, and that is the point.

Five K to halfway, dead on goal pace, and hold it there even when it
feels easy.

On a four hour goal that is five forty one a K, so the first five at
around five fifty five, and through halfway at about two hours."

## ⚑ Before filming

1. **"Most people run the second half slower than the first"** ⚑. This
   is well documented across large race datasets. **Say "most people",
   never a percentage.**
2. **"Fuel use climbs faster than your speed"** ⚑. The direction is
   solid: carbohydrate use rises steeply with intensity. **No numbers,
   and never say "carbohydrate oxidation" out loud** (P81).
3. **"Ten to fifteen seconds a K slower"** ⚑ matches the range in the
   science he supplied for the goal pace video. **Check it against
   what he said in the first 5k video** and use the same number, or
   the two videos contradict each other.
4. **The worked example.** 5:41 a K is a four hour marathon, the same
   figure as the goal pace video. Fifteen seconds slower is 5:56, so
   "around five fifty five". Halfway at goal pace is 1:59:54, and the
   slower first five adds under a minute and a half, so "about two
   hours" is honest. **Do not give a finish time prediction.**
5. **Do not claim world records are run with negative splits.** Some
   are, and Kipchoge's Berlin record was not. "Most people slow down"
   is the defensible claim.
6. **The failed-batch count** ⚑[N].
