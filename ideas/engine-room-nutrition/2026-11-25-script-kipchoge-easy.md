## Script

"What if I told you Kipchoge actually starts his easy runs at your sub-4 marathon pace? He sets off at about 5 to 6 minutes a K, but you speed yours up from the first step. So here's your easy run in 3 paces."

**[FIRST 10 MINUTES]**

"His race pace is under 3 minutes a K, so his easy start is nearly twice as slow. Your easy run lasts 30 minutes, and each third is a little quicker. Yours starts between 7:00 and 7:05 a K for a sub-4, so you can still chat without gasping for air. It'll feel embarrassingly slow, but a 2:01 marathoner starts the same way. Screenshot the card and save this, because every goal's 3 paces are on it."

**[NEXT 10 MINUTES]**

"Now let it come up a little, to between 6:30 and 6:35, still slow enough to chat. His pace creeps up the same way as his legs warm up. On a hill, let the pace drop and keep your breathing the same. You're still well slower than race pace, but your legs feel ready. He's fast on race day, but slow on his easy days."

"So how quick is too quick?"

**[LAST 10 MINUTES]**

"Never quicker than 6:05 a K for a sub-4. He finishes his easy runs at about 3:40, still about 45 seconds a K slower than his race pace. Your ceiling does the same job, so your easy days stay easy and your long run gets your best legs. If your watch says 5:50, ease off, even when you feel great, and stay under it until the 30 minutes are up. Every other goal's ceiling is waiting for you on the card."

"Know someone who apologises for their pace on easy runs? Send them this, because Kipchoge starts his at a sub-4 runner's race pace."

## Caption
Eliud Kipchoge's easy runs start slow. In a 2021 clip of his training group, an easy 10K set off at around 5 to 6 minutes a kilometre and finished at about 3:40 a K (Canadian Running). His marathon race pace is under 3:00 a K; his 2:01:09 in Berlin was about 2:52. So he starts an easy run at roughly a sub-4 marathoner's race pace, and finishes it well slower than his own.

Your 30-minute easy run in 3 paces a K (first 10 minutes, next 10, then never quicker than the last number). 3:00 marathon: 5:20, 4:57, 4:38. 3:30: 6:11, 5:45, 5:22. 3:45: 6:36, 6:08, 5:44. 4:00: 7:01, 6:32, 6:06 (in miles, 11:18, 10:31, 9:49). 4:30: 7:51, 7:18, 6:51. These come from Jack Daniels' easy-pace range for your marathon time, worked out, not a study of these runners, and not his ratio scaled (an elite's race pace sits much closer to his maximum than yours does). Hills and heat push the pace slower; go by the talk test when they do.
