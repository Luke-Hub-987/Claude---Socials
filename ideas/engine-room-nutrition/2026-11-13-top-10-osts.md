# The 10 best OSTs (ranked)

The test for each: **would a runner repeat it to a clubmate the next day?** The
nerves of steel edit (count, verb, escalation) is one way there; the others here
are the oldest tricks in copywriting: a sum that doesn't add up, a fable flipped,
a saying bent, two words one letter apart.

| # | OST (red word) | Device | First spoken line (Morning Coffee shape) | Truth / needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **YOU CARRY 90 MINUTES / OF CARBS. YOUR RACE IS / 4 HOURS** (red: 4 HOURS) | A sum that doesn't add up | "Your body stores about 90 minutes of carbs at race effort, and you've signed up to run for 4, so where are the other two and a half hours coming from?" | **Vet the 90 minutes** (muscle and liver stores at marathon effort; the classic glycogen studies). The brand's whole reason, in one line. The fix is the gel clock (FACTS) |
| 2 | **TRAIN LIKE A TORTOISE, / FINISH LIKE A HARE** (red: HARE) | A fable everyone knows, flipped | "Every runner knows the tortoise beat the hare, and then they run every training run like the hare." | Easy runs genuinely easy (**vet** the 80/20 research) + a patient start (FACTS) + the finish. G8: the payoff is the finish, not "start slow" again |
| 3 | **NERVES OF STEEL ARE / USELESS ON EMPTY LEGS** (red: USELESS) | A verdict against a whole genre | "You can have the best mindset in the race, and at 35K on an empty tank it's worth nothing." | The nerves of steel series note; **vet** the 2 techniques and the glucose claims |

**Close calls:** RUN SLOWER, OR STAY / SLOW FOREVER (the same idea as #2 with less
picture); YOUR LEGS HAVE A / 30-DAY MEMORY (the 10% rule repackaged as a trial in
January: its body earned 3 follows); 3 'LAZY' HABITS OF / SUB-4 MARATHONERS / (AND
YES, LAZY); HOPE IS NOT A / FUEL PLAN (better as #1's repeat line than a title).

## Pre-mortem
| Why they'd flop | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Wordplay that needs a second to decode** fails the one-second read (row 33) | Only plays on things everyone already knows: the fable, "rise to the occasion", fat and fast. Nothing to work out |
| **A clever line with no method under it** | Every title has its method in the right-hand column; the line is the door, the method is the room |
| **Four fuel titles (1, 3, 7, 9) feel like an ad** | They're facts about the body, the bar never named; spaced at least a week apart |
| **"Useless", "empty", "fat" read as harsh** | Harsh about the myth, never about the viewer (the Contrarian's "brutal titles, friendly voice") |
| **A kill that's wrong** | Nothing goes up until the status column is cleared |

## Council (P150)
- **Contrarian:** #7 is Morning Coffee's topic; runners who follow him will spot
  it. Cross-review: the myth is everyone's, the fat/fast line is ours, and it's
  our product's home turf.
- **Assumption-ripper:** "Is 90 minutes true for a 4-hour runner?" At easier
  effort the stores last longer, so the script says "about 90 minutes at race
  effort" and the vetting decides the number. If it doesn't hold, #1 becomes HOPE
  IS NOT A FUEL PLAN.
- **Expansionist:** #2 and #5 are the two people will quote at club runs; both
  could be the line on a T-shirt, which is the send.
- **Outsider:** #1, #4 and #6 stop me without running. #9 needs you to be a runner.
- **Executor:** I vet #1, #4 and #8 this week; Luke sends his 5 tactics and his
  Dublin shoes.
- **Chairman:** #1 is the one: it's a sum every viewer does in their head in one
  second, it's true once vetted, and the answer is our reason to exist. Then #2,
  #4 and #6 in the order the calendar allows.

## Update (13 Nov): #1 vetted
"90 minutes" doesn't hold for a 4-hour runner: about 500 g stored (400 muscle,
100 liver), a marathon burns about 480 to 550 g of carbs at 4-hour pace, and the
stores run short at about 32K, about 3 hours (Rapoport 2010; Coyle 1986). #1 is now
**YOU CARRY 3 HOURS OF / CARBS. YOUR RACE IS / 4 HOURS**. Brief:
`2026-11-13-brief-fourth-hour.md`.
