# Brief: YOU CARRY 3 HOURS OF / CARBS. YOUR RACE IS / 4 HOURS

## The vetting: "90 minutes" is wrong (Luke was right)

### What you store
- **Muscle about 400 g, liver about 100 g: about 500 g, roughly 2,000 kcal**
  (4 kcal a gram). Hearris and others, *Nutrients* 2018, review of
  carbohydrate and glycogen
  ([PMC5872716](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5872716)). Other
  reviews give muscle anywhere from 350 to 700 g depending on training, diet
  and carb loading (Knuiman and others, *Nutrition & Metabolism* 2015). So
  Luke's 400 g in the muscles is the textbook figure.
- **You can't use all of it.** Muscle glycogen mostly stays in the muscle it's
  in, so what's in your arms and shoulders doesn't fuel your legs; only the
  liver sends sugar into the blood (basic physiology). And fatigue comes before
  zero: in Coyle 1986, muscle glycogen was down to about a quarter of its
  starting level when people gave up (secondary summary; check the full paper).

### What a marathon burns
- **About 1 kcal per kilo per kilometre** (Rapoport, *PLoS Computational
  Biology* 2010, [PMC2958805](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2958805)).
  A 70 kg runner: about 2,950 kcal for 42.2K. At 4-hour pace that's about 740
  kcal an hour.
- **About two thirds of it from carbs at marathon pace** for an amateur (the
  harder you run, the bigger the share). A 2024 commentary cites 68% in
  recreational runners ([Frontiers in Nutrition](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1507572/full)).
  This is the weakest number in the chain: **to firm up with a primary source.**
- **So:** 65% to 75% of 2,950 kcal is 1,920 to 2,210 kcal, which is **480 to 550 g
  of carbs for the race, about 120 to 140 g an hour.**

### When the stores run short
- On paper the stores (about 500 g) and the race (480 to 550 g) are close. In
  practice they run short, because part of the 500 g is in muscles that aren't
  running and fatigue comes before zero.
- **Rapoport's model puts the wall at about 32K** for a typical runner with about
  2,000 kcal of glycogen at 75% to 85% of their max (secondary summary of the
  paper; check the figure in the full text). **At 4-hour pace, 32K comes at about
  3:02.**
- **Coyle 1986** ([PubMed 3525502](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3525502/)):
  trained cyclists riding hard gave up at about 3 hours on a placebo, and **about
  an hour later (4.02 hours) when fed carbohydrate.** Cyclists, not runners, and the
  intensity is to confirm in the full paper, but it's the closest thing to the
  title in a lab.

### Where "90 minutes" comes from
The common line "glycogen lasts 90 to 120 minutes" is for hard efforts, around
half-marathon effort or above, where carbs supply far more of the energy. At
4-hour marathon pace the stores last about twice as long. **So the honest
number for a 4-hour runner is about 3 hours, about 32K.**

### What the gels add
On our clock (FACTS: 30g gel at 20 minutes, then every 30, none in the last 20
minutes) a 4-hour runner takes 7 gels at 20, 50, 80, 110, 140, 170 and 200
minutes: **210 g, about 60 g an hour.** That's roughly the gap between what's
stored and what's burned. **The fourth hour runs on what's in your pocket.**

### The caveats the script has to respect
- "About 3 hours" is a 70 kg runner at 4-hour pace on a normal diet. Heavier,
  faster or under-fuelled runners run short sooner; carb loading pushes it later
  (our load: 8 to 10 g/kg, the carb load script). Say "about".
- No kcal sums in the audio (row 21). One number per line; the maths goes in the
  caption.
- People who've run 4 hours without gels will comment. True for some (slower,
  lighter); the script says "for most runners at 4-hour pace".
