## Script

"You can steal the long run that won the New York Marathon in 2:06, even at sub-4 pace. You need its shape, not his speed, so it fits into 40 minutes. Yours dies at the end, but this one finishes faster than it starts."

**[10 MINUTES EASY]**

"His 40K ran in 4 blocks of 10K, each one faster than the last. The first was about 30 seconds a K slower than his race pace. So yours is the 4 x 10, and the first block is easy. For a sub-4 that's between 6:40 and 6:45 a K, slow enough to talk without gasping. Save this and screenshot the card, so you've got your 4 paces."

**[10 MINUTES STEADY]**

"His second 10K was still about 10 seconds a K slower than race pace. So your next 10 minutes go between 5:55 and 6:00, quicker but still steady, not hard. You're not racing yet, but you've stopped jogging, so hold it for the full 10 minutes."

**[10 MINUTES RACE PACE]**

"Your third block is marathon pace, so between 5:40 and 5:45 for a sub-4. That's where his third 10K sat, right on the pace he won New York at. You don't need his speed, but you do need his shape, so hold it for all 10 minutes."

"So why finish faster than race pace?"

**[10 MINUTES FASTER]**

"Because the end of a marathon is where the pace slides. His last 10K was his fastest, quicker than the pace he won at. Your last 10 minutes go between 5:25 and 5:30, a bit quicker than race pace on legs that are already tired. It'll feel hard, but it's only 10 minutes, and it's the bit a steady long run never gets. When it feels easy, stretch each block to 20 minutes at the same paces. On any run over an hour, take your first gel 20 minutes in, because his drink took about an hour to count."

"Send this to the friend who's chasing their first sub-4, because their hardest long run just got shorter."

## Caption
Andreas Almgren won the 2026 New York City Marathon on his debut in 2:06:01, about 2:59 a kilometre. One of his long runs in the build-up was 40K in four 10K blocks, each faster: about 3:30, 3:08, 2:59 and 2:51 a K (as reported by Marathon Handbook). That's easy, steady, race pace, then a bit quicker than race pace. Your version keeps the shape and shrinks the time: 4 blocks of 10 minutes, 40 minutes in all, with no rest between them, each block running straight into the next, as his did.

Your 4 paces a K (easy, steady, race pace, faster). 3:00 marathon: 5:01, 4:29, 4:16, 4:04. 3:30: 5:51, 5:14, 4:59, 4:44. 3:45: 6:16, 5:37, 5:20, 5:05. 4:00: 6:41, 5:59, 5:41, 5:25 (in miles, 10:45, 9:38, 9:09, 8:43). These are his blocks scaled to your race pace, not a study, so if the last block feels too much, hold race pace instead. When 4 x 10 feels easy, try 4 x 15, then 4 x 20. Once a run goes past an hour, take your first gel at 20 minutes and one every 30: in Maurten's testing it took about an hour before his drink was a steady share of the fuel he burned.
