# How to run 42.2k in a marathon

## Why today rather than Thursday

**Nothing in this video is tied to a day.** It is a pure race day
tactic, so Thursday buys nothing, and in a window that closes on
25 October a free day is a day lost. The goal pace video went up
Tuesday lunchtime and is still climbing, so the gap is normal.

That leaves **Sunday for the gel alternatives video**, which is tied
to a day, because people will have just finished a long run and found
out their gels do not agree with them.

**Friday 25 is free** for the long run rehearsal script, which is
written, banked and now has its checklist card built.

## OST, v2, re-audited against the winners

> **How to run 42.2k in a marathon**
> *(and where the extra kilometre comes from)*

### Why v1 was weaker

> ~~How to avoid running 43k in a marathon~~ *(and the 3 minutes it
> costs you)*

Two real problems:

1. **"Avoid" is a negative verb.** Every winner leads with a positive
   action: what to do, what to eat, how to know. "How to avoid" frames
   the promise as preventing a nuisance rather than doing something,
   and it reads as a chore.
2. **No coordinate.** Every big video has a position: 3 hours, 30
   minutes, the first 5k. "In a marathon" is a container. v2 uses
   **42.2k itself as the anchor**, which does the same job.

### Why v2 works

**Line one looks tautological, and that is the whole trick.** "How to
run 42.2k in a marathon" makes a runner stop and think *it is 42.2 by
definition, what is there to know*. That is a **tiny information gap**,
which is the condition for maximum curiosity (P21, Loewenstein). A
large gap produces no curiosity. This one is a centimetre wide and
they cannot step over it.

- **Seven words, 32 characters.** The shortest OST on the account and
  it renders biggest (P89).
- **Positive instruction verb**, marathon in line one, race
  positioned (P86).
- **The whisper confirms the gap exists and says nothing about where**
  (P80). Screenshot test passes.
- **The collision is inside the OST itself** (P87), so the hook does
  not have to carry it alone.

**Alternative, exact winner template:** *What to do at every corner of
a marathon* *(and why your watch says 43k)*. Better architecture,
weaker one-second read, because the work happens in the whisper rather
than line one (P62).

**Alternative if he wants the number up front:** *How to avoid running
43k in a marathon* *(and the 3 minutes it costs you)*. Arresting, but
the negative verb stands.

bend wherever the crowd puts them.

Here is the whole technique. **Look at the next turn while you are
still a hundred metres away, and move across to the inside before you
get there.** Cutting across during the bend is how you end up boxed in

the kilometre comes from."

**[THREE. THE START, THE STATIONS, AND THE HONEST BIT]**

by five K anyway. And at the water stations, pick your side before you
get there rather than at the table.

Do those three and you finish having run four or five hundred metres
less than the person beside you. At four hour pace that is about three
minutes, for nothing.

it perfectly. Do not chase the number. Run the line."

## ⚑ Before filming

1. **"42.8, sometimes over 43."** This is the honest range for a big
   city marathon and the round 43 in the OST is shorthand. **Ground it
   in the first ten seconds** exactly as written so the title is not
   left overstating.
2. **The shortest possible route** ⚑. Courses are measured along the
   shortest route a runner could take, and measurers add a small
   margin so a course is never short. Both are real. **Do not name the
   governing body, do not quote the percentage**, and say "a small
   margin" rather than a figure.
3. **"Dozens of turns"** ⚑ rather than a number for Dublin, unless he
   counts them off the official course map.
4. **The 3 minutes** ⚑ is checkable: 400 to 600 metres at around 5:41
   a kilometre is 2.3 to 3.4 minutes, so three is the conservative
   middle. Say "about three minutes".
5. **The GPS line stays in.** It costs nothing and it is the
   difference between a tip and something true. Leaving it out invites
   the comment that undoes the whole video.
6. **No forced bar mention.** There is no honest fuel angle in a
   tactics video, and bolting one on is the P57 failure running
   backwards. The on-screen card and the CTA carry the brand, and that
   is enough for one video.
7. **The failed-batch count** ⚑[N].

**The fragment one is the worst of them**, because it is a rule in
`voice-rules.md` that the pre-delivery scan does not check for. The
scan looks for "not X, it is Y" and em dashes. **It now needs a third
pass for fragments**, and "Not 42.2." would have been caught by
looking for any sentence under four words starting with a negative.

**The rehook is the genuinely new thing here.** HF14 allows one and we
have never written one, while every retention graph on the account
shows the same shape: a steep drop to roughly a third by ten seconds
and then a long flat tail. A rehook belongs exactly where that tail
starts to sag, which is around thirty five seconds, and "the next one
is the one nobody does, and it is free" costs eleven words.

If it works here, it goes in every script.
