**[LEAVING HOME: DRESS 10 DEGREES WARMER]**
"Before you leave, check the temperature at the start and dress like it's ten degrees warmer. So if it's six degrees, dress for sixteen. For most people that's shorts, a vest or T-shirt, arm sleeves you can roll down, and a pair of cheap gloves. Colder mornings are all in the caption."

**[THE START PEN: THROW IT AWAY]**
"The start pen is where most people get it wrong. You can be stood there an hour, and shivering burns the fuel you spent two days loading. So bring something you'll throw away, like an old fleece from a charity shop or a bin bag with holes for your head and arms. Keep it on until a minute before you start, then chuck it to the side. Most big races give them to charity."

**[THE FIRST 10K: STRIP DOWN]**
"Once you're moving, you'll warm up within a few K. When your hands are warm, tuck the gloves into your shorts and roll the sleeves down to your wrists. If you're sweating by five K, you've got too much on."

## Caption (prose; every temperature)

What to wear for a cold marathon. Dress for the temperature at the start plus 10°C (about 20°F), and keep a throwaway layer for the pen.

Below 0°C (32°F): a long-sleeve top, shorts or half-tights (full tights only if it is freezing and windy), gloves, and a headband or light hat over your ears. 0 to 5°C (32 to 41°F): a T-shirt or vest with arm sleeves, shorts, gloves and a headband. 5 to 10°C (41 to 50°F): a vest or T-shirt, shorts, arm sleeves and cheap gloves. 10 to 15°C (50 to 59°F): a vest and shorts, nothing else.

In the pen, wear something you'll throw away: an old fleece from a charity shop, or a bin bag with holes for your head and arms. Take it off a minute before you start. Most big races give them to charity.

Once you're moving, roll the sleeves down and tuck the gloves into your shorts when your hands warm up. If you're sweating by 5K, you're wearing too much. Leave the rain jacket at home unless it's freezing rain: it traps sweat, and wet kit gets cold fast when you slow down late in the race. Put anti-chafe on anywhere that rubs, because wet kit chafes more.

Want a plan for your race? Email us your race and goal time through the link in the bio and we'll build you one, free.
