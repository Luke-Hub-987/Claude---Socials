## Script v1 (338 words, 1,789 characters spoken, about 1:40)
If you're running a marathon in the next few weeks, your watch should say you're quicker than three thirty pace for most of the race, and that's your race going to plan.

Hold a perfect five minutes a K on that watch and the text with your result can still say three thirty-three. Do this instead, and it'll say three twenty-eight.

There's a three-minute gap between your watch and your chip, and it changes every pace you run. Save this.

Start with four times. Fifty minutes at ten K, one thirty-nine at twenty, two twenty-nine at thirty and three eighteen at forty. Write them on your wrist, or put them on your lock screen.

If three thirty isn't your goal, email us your race and goal time through the link in the bio and we'll send you your own four numbers, free. Back to three thirty.

For the first ten K, run four fifty-nine a K on your watch. You should be able to say "I am running a marathon today and I feel good", all ten words in one breath, in your normal voice. If the clock says fifty at the ten K sign, you've nailed it.

From ten to thirty, drop to four fifty-two. Yes, that's quicker than three thirty pace, and it's meant to be. At twenty, look for one thirty-nine, ten words but a bit breathy. At thirty, two twenty-nine, and about five words before you need a breath.

So why isn't five minutes a K good enough?

Two things are hiding in that five. Real three thirty pace is four fifty-nine, so a round five loses a second and a bit every K, and that's nearly a minute by the finish. Then your watch adds distance on every bend, so it beeps each K just before the real sign, which costs about two minutes over the race. Hold four fifty-two to forty, about three eighteen at that sign, then run the last two K at four forty-two, and you'll finish around three twenty-eight.
