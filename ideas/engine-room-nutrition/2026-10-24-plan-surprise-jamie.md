# My co-founder has no idea I'm running this marathon

## LLM Council (P150)

### 1. Contrarian: why it fails
- **Nobody watching knows Jamie or Luke.** Most of our reach is non-followers.
  A reaction only lands if the viewer cares, and in 3 seconds they have no
  reason to.
- **The payoff is out of our control.** If Jamie's reaction is a quiet
  "ah, would you stop", a video built to that moment falls flat, and a
  held payoff that underdelivers makes people feel tricked.
- **One take, in chaos.** Bag drop is crowded and loud; the friend can miss
  it, the phone can shake, Jamie can turn the wrong way. No reshoot.
- **It's not advice.** Every winner on the grid is advice. Founder posts
  topped out around 2.2K before the advice format.
- **It can cost Jamie's race** if it eats into his last 30 minutes.

### 2. Assumption-ripper: what we're taking for granted
- **"He has no idea" is the stake.** Is it enough? It's only big if there's
  a reason it matters: his first marathon, you said you wouldn't run, you
  were injured, he's been training alone. **The real reason has to be in
  line two, and only Luke knows it.**
- **The reaction is the payoff.** Maybe running it together, or the finish,
  is the bigger moment. We won't know until the footage exists.
- **Bag drop is the right place.** It's the safe place, not the best shot.
  Running up beside him at 5K would look better, but it risks both races
  and depends on your pacing against his. Safe wins (P146).
- **Viewers know what a bib means.** Some will think you swapped onto
  someone's bib (against race rules). Say "my own entry".
- **Viewers know what Engine Room is.** They don't. Five words fix it.
- **Landscape is fine for the friend.** It isn't: cropping loses the face.
  Film vertical.

### 3. Expansionist: the upside we're missing
- **It's a two-video story.** Mon: the reveal. Tue: "how we ran it at every
  10K", the advice sequel, with characters people now know. People follow
  people (account split plan).
- **Stories are safe once he knows.** From the reveal on, post race-day
  stories: the Dublin audience is in the same corrals, and old reels
  resurface in the window.
- **Jamie as a collaborator** puts it on a third grid.
- **The enjoy video, done for real:** whoever is at 32K for you is in shot.
- **A share prompt that fits the format:** "Send this to the person you'd
  do this to."
- **A comment prompt:** "Who would you surprise?" Comments are our weak spot.
- **The bar, if it's real:** eaten on camera 30 minutes before the start,
  the window it's built for.
- **Pin it** as the founder introduction for every new viewer.

### 4. Outsider: no context
- "Who's Jamie? What's Engine Room? Why is it a surprise that you're
  running? Isn't everyone running?"
- "Did you take someone else's place?"
- "What's a bag drop?" (jargon in a marker)
- "Why should I care what his face does?"

### 5. Executor: the end result only
- **The one asset that matters is a usable reveal clip, vertical, with
  Jamie's face in it.** Everything else is optional.
- **Tonight:** film the 2 lines (5 minutes); text the friend the exact
  spot, time and angle; charge both phones; clear storage.
- **Tomorrow:** reveal 30 to 40 minutes before the gun; keep rolling 20
  seconds; get his answer on camera.
- **The edit decides after the footage** (rule below). Keep it 35 to 50
  seconds.
- **Measure:** follows (the reason to make a story), shares, emails.
- **If the reveal is missed:** it becomes "we both ran Dublin", with the
  race footage, and the sequel still runs.

## The edit rule (decided after the footage)
- **Strong reaction:** hold it to the end, 35 to 45 seconds. The last frame
  is his face.
- **Muted reaction:** put it in the middle, then end on the two of you
  running or finishing together, 45 to 50 seconds.
- **Missed:** "we both ran Dublin", race footage only.
