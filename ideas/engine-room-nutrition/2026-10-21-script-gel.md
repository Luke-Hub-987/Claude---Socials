## Script (about 345 words, about 1:43)
If you're running a marathon in the next few weeks and gels are a struggle after 20K, try drinking your next one instead of eating it. It's the same gel, in the water you were going to drink anyway.

You know the moment. 20K into a long run, the next gel's due, it's thick and sickly sweet, and you're hacking it down between breaths. 10 minutes later it's sloshing with every step and you're walking, watching your pace go.

Now picture race day, at 38K. You're sipping something that tastes like a sports drink, your legs are still turning over, and you're picking people off one lamppost at a time.

It sounds odd, but there's a simple reason it sits better. And if gels go down fine for you now, save this for the day they don't.

Your first 30g gel goes in at 20 minutes, then one every 30 minutes, which is 60g of carbs an hour. Land each one on a water station, give or take 5 minutes. When it's due, you take it. No "I'll wait till I feel like it".

Want your whole race planned like this? Email us your race and goal time through the link in the bio and we'll send you your plan, free. Back to the station.

Grab a bottle and take 3 sips first, because a full bottle has no room for the gel. Squeeze the whole gel in, cap on, and shake it.

Then sip it over the next 2K, and finish it before your next gel is due. That bottle is your drink for that half hour, so no sports drink on top. If the bottle's not gone, the carbs aren't either.

So why does the same gel sit so much better in a bottle?

Your stomach always gets the gel and the water together. Eat it, and it lands as a thick, sweet lump with a few sips chasing it. Drink it, and it arrives already mixed, a mouthful at a time. Same 30g, nothing to hack down, and at 38K you're still sipping, and still running.

## Caption (final)
Can't face another gel after 20K? Try drinking your next one instead of eating it. It's the same gel, in the water you were going to drink anyway. If gels go down fine for you, keep doing what works and save this for the day they don't.

Your first 30g gel goes in at 20 minutes, then one every 30 minutes, which is 60g of carbs an hour: 0:20, 0:50, 1:20, 1:50, 2:20, 2:50, 3:20 and 3:50. Land each one on a water station, give or take 5 minutes. Using 40g gels? One every 40 minutes.

At the station, grab a bottle and take 3 sips first, because a full bottle has no room for the gel. Squeeze the whole gel in, cap on, and shake it. Then sip it over the next 2K and finish it before your next gel is due. That bottle is your drink for that half hour, so no sports drink on top. If the bottle's not gone, the carbs aren't either.

Why it works: your stomach was always getting the gel and the water together. Eaten, it lands as a thick, sweet lump with a few sips chasing it. Drunk, it arrives already mixed. Same 30g, nothing to hack down.

Rather break it up? Swap every other gel for 4 or 5 jelly sweets (about 5g each, so 20 to 25g), or if it's the taste more than the texture, alternate two flavours you've trained with. Jelly sweets are chewed, so keep them to the first half.

Race hands out cups, not bottles? Take the gel about 100 metres before the station, then 3 or 4 good sips of plain water from the cup. Try the bottle method once on an easy run this week first, and use only gels you've already had on a long run.

What to carry, with one spare: 7 gels for a 3:30, 8 for 3:45 or 4:00, 9 for 4:30, 10 for 5:00.

Want your whole race planned like this? Email us your race and goal time through the link in the bio and we'll send your plan, free.
