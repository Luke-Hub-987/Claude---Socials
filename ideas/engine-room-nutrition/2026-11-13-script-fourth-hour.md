## Caption
Everyone says your carbs run out at 90 minutes. That's for hard efforts, around half-marathon pace or faster. At 4-hour marathon pace, most runners carry about 3 hours, so the fourth hour has to come from your pocket.

Why about 3 hours: you store about 500 g of carbs, about 400 g in your muscles and 100 g in your liver (Hearris and others, Nutrients 2018). Muscle can't hand its glycogen back to the blood, so what's in your arms stays in your arms; only your legs and liver fuel your legs. A 4-hour runner burns roughly 110 to 140 g of carbs an hour, so the stores alone run short at about 3 hours, around 32K (Rapoport's model, PLoS Computational Biology 2010). In the lab, cyclists given no carbs tired at about 3 hours and lasted about an hour longer when they were fed (Coyle 1986). It depends on your weight, pace and carb load, so treat it as "about".

The clock for a 4:00 marathon with 30 g gels: first gel at 20 minutes, then 0:50, 1:20, 1:50, 2:20, 2:50 and 3:20. None in the last 20 minutes. That's 7 gels, about 60 g an hour, so carry 8 in case you drop one.

Smaller gels (20 to 25 g, like most Maurten, GU and High5 gels): 0:20, then every 20 minutes to 3:20, so 10 gels, carry 11. 40 g gels: 0:20, 1:00, 1:40, 2:20 and 3:00, so 5 gels, carry 6. Either way it's about 60 g an hour or a little more.

For 3:30: 6 gels, the last at 2:50, carry 7. For 4:30: 8 gels, the last at 3:50, carry 9. If you're passing a station, grab a bottle and take a few sips with your gel, never sports drink, and never two gels at once.

The gels don't wait for 3:02 to work: from the first one, they're burned alongside what you stored, which is why the tank still has something in it at 32K.
