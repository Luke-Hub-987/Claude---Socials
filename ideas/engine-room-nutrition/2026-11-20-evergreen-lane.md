# The evergreen lane

## 1. What the fan told Luke, and what our numbers already say
The fan finds the marathon content useful but isn't racing, isn't entering
next year, and so has no reason to watch on any given day. Our own data agrees
with him, which we hadn't read this way:

| Reel (Nov) | Tied to a race date? | Views | Skip |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4-HOUR MARATHONS DIE IN HOUR 4 | No (any 4-hour marathon, ever) | 19.7K | 26.8% |
| NEVER DECIDE TO WALK AT 30K | No | 7.2K at 16h | 29.5% |
| ORANGES WON'T SAVE YOUR LEGS | No (any Sunday league match) | 2.5K (trial) | 33.4% |
| 8 SPRING MARATHONS, 1 CHANGE | Yes (spring races) | 2.6K | 49.7% |
| THE 10% RULE (dates in the caption) | Partly | 2.8K | 42.4% |

The November winners had **no deadline**. What they had was a moment the
viewer already fears or recognises (hour 4, the 30K voice, the 75th minute)
and a number. The gate's G5 ("a stake within about 4 weeks") was built from
September and October, when everyone watching had Dublin coming. In the off
season it pushes us towards the narrow, dated videos that did worst.

## 2. Why "nerves of steel" works (the deeper reading)
"3 habits to create nerves of steel (progressively more brutal)" got about 24
times the likes of "how to develop nerves of steel", the same video.
1. **The reward is who you become, and it never expires.** Nobody needs a
   date to want nerves of steel. The stake is the viewer's picture of
   themselves.
2. **Everyone has felt the opposite.** Nerves are universal, so nobody
   swipes away thinking "not for me" (our "they already know it's their
   problem", P220).
3. **A count with an escalation is a promise.** 3 things, each harder: they
   stay for the hardest one (we proved this on the 30K reel: no step at
   0:45).
4. **Habits are something to do today.** It hands over a method (P127).
5. **Sending it says something about the sender.** Identity content gets
   shared as a signal ("this is me", "this is you").

## 3. Why TED-Ed works
"Why is saffron so expensive?" and "How to estimate enormous numbers" hold
people with no stake at all except the itch.
1. **A familiar thing made strange.** You've seen saffron and never asked
   why it costs what it does. The gap opens on something already in your
   life (Loewenstein's information gap: curiosity is the feeling of a
   missing piece you now know is missing).
2. **An astonishing number early.** The number makes the gap feel big.
3. **A mechanism, shown step by step.** Each step closes part of the gap
   and opens the next (our stage structure, P176).
4. **You leave seeing the world differently.** The reward is a new lens,
   not a deadline.

## 4. What Ogilvy and Sabri Suby add (Luke said "Sabra Copper": I've read that as Sabri Suby, Sell Like Crazy; tell me if it's someone else)
- **Ogilvy warned against curiosity alone.** He called headlines that only
  tease "blind" and said the headline must promise the reader something in
  their self-interest. So a TED-Ed title alone isn't enough for us: every
  curious question has to end in something they use on their next run. "Why
  saffron is expensive" is fine for TED-Ed; for us it's "why you hit the
  wall when you carry fuel for 27 marathons" *and here's what that means for
  your next long run*.
- **Ogilvy's specifics.** Facts, not adjectives; the number in the headline;
  news where there is some.
- **Suby: only about 3% are ready now.** Most of any audience isn't buying
  (or racing) yet. You don't sell to the 97%; you give them something so
  useful they hand over their email, then you stay with them until their
  moment comes. That's exactly the fan: not racing now, worth capturing now,
  ready in spring.
- **Schwartz's awareness levels (under both).** A runner with no race is at
  most "problem aware". Content for them names a problem they recognise in
  their everyday running (the long run that falls apart, the parkrun fade,
  the hungry evening), not a race problem they don't have yet.

## 5. The model: two lanes
| | Lane A: evergreen (most reels, November to early January) | Lane B: the event (close to the date) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Every runner, racing or not; footballers; anyone curious about the body | Runners with that race booked |
| Stake | Who they become (identity), curiosity that ends in something they use, their weekly run | The race date |
| Title moves | Nerves of steel (count + escalation + identity), TED-Ed why / how, the myth killed | The course, the clock, the deadline |
| Specific how? | A number, a body moment, a worked example (70kg runner, 4-hour pace) | Their race, their goal time |
| The email | A tool they use any week (their fuel tank, their long-run clock), comment word | Their race plan |
| When | Now | Valencia race week (from about 30 Nov); spring blocks from late December |

Lane A still obeys everything that worked: the title they recognise in a
second, the moment they've felt, numbers to keep (P224), the gap held to the
last stage. Only the stake changes.

## 7. What changes in the gate (P225)
- **G5, amended for Lane A:** the stake can be a deadline inside about 4
  weeks, a result only we have, **or** (Lane A, trial reels first) who they
  become, or a curious question that ends in something they use on their
  next run. Trivia with nothing to use still fails.
- **G6 stays for Lane B.** For Lane A the subject can be the body (a body
  moment they've felt), still never the gut, sleep or kit alone.
- C8 in `gate/contradictions.md` is partly settled by this.

## 8. The council
- **Contrarian:** curiosity videos get views from people who'll never buy a
  marathon bar. Answer: every Lane A video ends in something a runner uses,
  and the email offer is a running tool, so the list stays runners.
- **Assumption-ripper:** we assumed urgency is the only stake because
  autumn taught us that. November's own winners had none.
- **Expansionist:** Lane A travels further (non-racers, footballers,
  hikers), grows the list through the off season, and gives the podcast and
  long-form videos a bigger pool to draw from.
- **Outsider:** "why does a marathon burn a day of food?" is the first of
  these I'd watch without being a runner.
- **Executor:** two Lane A trials next week, one identity (nerves-of-steel
  shape) and one TED-Ed question, with facts sourced first; Valencia race
  week from 30 Nov; judge Lane A at 24 hours against the trial base rates.
- **Chairman verdict:** run Lane A as the default until the spring blocks
  start, trial first; keep Lane B for race weeks. Amend G5 now so the gate
  stops pushing the off-season content towards dates nobody has.

## 9. Interest, not need (Luke, 21 Nov, P228)
The first Lane A pick showed the line: WHY SO MANY MARATHONERS FINISH AT 3:59
(interesting to anyone who has seen a finish clock) over YOU WEREN'T EMPTY AT
41K (useful only to a runner in a race). Off season, the title earns the watch
by being interesting today; the use comes after. The hook names the obvious
answer and sets it aside ("you'd think it's X, but there's a deeper reason"),
and the aha or myth bust is the first sentence after the hook.
