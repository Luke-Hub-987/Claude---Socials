## The Zero to One secret

Most runners think carb loading is one big plate of pasta the night
before. It is about twice their usual carbs, every day for two days, in
grams they can count, and the way to get it in is to swap and drink,
not to eat more food. The piece almost nobody knows, held for the last
stage: **the liver loses a lot of its store overnight, so race
breakfast is the last part of the load.**

## Caption (prose, P118: grams first)

How to actually carb load for a marathon. Most runners load on one big plate of pasta the night before, but a proper load is about twice your usual carbs, every day for the two days before your race.

Start 48 hours out. Aim for 8 to 10g of carbs per kg of body weight a day (the sports guidelines go up to 12). For a 70kg runner that is about 560 to 700g a day, roughly the carbs in a dozen plain bagels, spread over three meals and three snacks. If your race is on a Sunday, that means Friday and Saturday.

By the first evening you will feel full. Keep your plates the same size and swap: less meat, cheese, butter and oil, more white rice, pasta, bread, potatoes and jam. Go low fibre for these two days. The day before, drink some of it: a big glass of orange juice or a 500ml sports drink is about 30g each. Expect to weigh a kilo or two more on race morning: your muscles store carbs with water, and none of it is fat.

Race morning, three hours before the start, eat about 2g of carbs per kg (about 140g at 70kg: two plain bagels with jam and a glass of juice), because your liver uses up a lot of its store overnight. First gel at 20 minutes: about 60 to 75g of carbs an hour from there.

Want it worked out for you? Email us your race and goal time through the link in the bio and we will send you a free personalised plan, carb load included.

Practise your race breakfast before a long run first. If you have diabetes or any medical condition, check with your doctor before changing how you eat.

## ⚑ Science, checked

| # | Claim in the audio | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Load over the two days before; 8 to 10 g per kg a day | Carb loading guidance: 10 to 12 g/kg/day for 36 to 48 h before events over 90 minutes (Burke et al., 2011, J Sports Sci, the IOC consensus). 8 to 10 is the practical target already in the 7 days caption; the caption gives the range up to 12. Self-reported loading in London Marathon runners was mostly well below this (Atkinson et al., 2011, IJSNEM), the basis for "a guess is usually far too low" (said without the statistic, P107) |
| 2 | 70 kg: about 600 g a day, a dozen bagels | 70 x 8.5 = 595 g. A large plain bagel (~100 g) has about 48 to 55 g of carbs; 12 bagels is about 580 to 660 g. "About", and "as many carbs as", so it is a picture of the amount, not a menu (P115) |
| 3 | Swap fat, protein and fibre for carbs, plates the same size | Standard sports-dietitian practice for loading (Australian Institute of Sport carb loading guidance: compact, low-fibre carbs; reduce fat to make room). Low fibre matches 7 days |
| 4 | Juice or sports drink about 30 g | 330 ml orange juice ~28 to 30 g; 500 ml of a 6% sports drink ~30 g. Liquid carbs as a way to hit the target is in the same AIS guidance. Pinch of salt matches 24 hours and 7 days |
| 5 | Liver glycogen falls overnight; it maintains blood glucose; ~2 g/kg about 3 h before | Liver glycogen falls substantially after an overnight fast (Nilsson and Hultman, 1973; Taylor et al., 1996, MRS). Pre-event meal 1 to 4 g/kg, 1 to 4 h before (Thomas, Erdman and Burke, 2016, ACSM/AND/DC position). Three hours matches the 3 hours video; first gel at 20 min matches every race-day video |

**Not claimed:** how much faster loading makes you (P107), how long the
load lasts in minutes, anything about Luke, anything about medication.
