## The script patterns (with the line from the pack)

### 1. The first 3 seconds: eight openers that work
| Opener | The line | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| **Call-out with a number** | "If you're a 3:20 and 3:30 marathoner and can't get faster" | "If you're stuck between 4:05 and 4:15..." |
| **The myth said as if true, then killed** | "Carbs will make you fat, and if you want to lose weight, you need to avoid them. That is the stupidest thing I can say." | "Bank time while you feel good. That's the worst advice in the marathon." |
| **Blunt diagnosis** | "You're not getting better because your long run nutrition is [bad]." | "Your long runs aren't the problem. What you carry on them is." |
| **A warning** | "Be warned, because actually doing this will put a massive strain on your nervous system." | "Fair warning: the last one is going to hurt your ego." |
| **Escalation with a twist** | "Fair warning, the more subtle they get, the more powerful they get." / "the more uncomfortable they get, the calmer you get." | "Each one's more brutal, and only the first one can be stopped." |
| **Doubling down** | "These are three cold behaviours that you should do. And yes, I said should do." | "3 lazy habits of sub-4 runners. And yes, lazy." |
| **Forbidden knowledge** | "I need you to promise me that the information I'm about to give you, you are not going to use maliciously." | Not ours (it's a bit). Keep in the bank |
| **One thing** | "There's one line you need in every single ad..." | "There's one number on your watch at 23K that decides the last 10K." |

### 2. The value starts at 0:05 to 0:07
Nerves of steel: item 1 at 0:06. Photographic memory: item 1 at 0:06. Commanding
presence: 0:06. Their hook is **15 to 25 words**: the title said aloud, one
escalation or warning line, then "first things first". Our P198 is 40 to 55
words. **The tightest ones run 51 to 65 seconds: 3 items, about 15 seconds
each.**

### 3. Every item has a name, an authority, an exact instruction and what changes
"First is Kim's game. This is the exact drill elite military snipers used to forge
borderline inhuman recall. Stare at a random group of objects for exactly 30
seconds. Cover them up, and force yourself to list every single one. Do this
daily and you will start catching micro details that average people are
completely blind to."
- **Name:** Kim's game, the two-second pause, strategic silence, outrun the critic.
- **Borrowed authority, the more extreme the better:** military snipers, an ex-FBI
  agent, the FBI's lead hostage negotiator, a Harvard study, an NHL goalie who
  stopped 34 shots against the champions.
- **An exact instruction with a number:** 30 seconds, two seconds.
- **What changes, as an identity:** "the one who was in charge instead of the one
  trying to please".

### 4. Physical, violent verbs
"violently kicked all the way back to box one", "ruthlessly attack", "aggressively
dump every single detail", "you will destroy your competition", "forge borderline
inhuman recall". The verbs carry the energy; the nouns stay plain.

### 5. A contrast pair they'd repeat
"You respond when you are ready, not when you are triggered." "You're the coach on
the sideline instead of the player panicking on the pitch." "You look like the one
who was in charge instead of the one who was trying to please." "Your body isn't
storing fat because you ate a bagel. Your body is storing fat because you're
overeating."

### 6. Scenes they've lived, in tiny detail
"rubbing your neck, touching your face, or fiddling with your watch"; "still
drinking beers on a Saturday... brunch on a Sunday with their mimosas"; "pitching
with a timer ticking down while someone interrupts you".

### 7. The exact words said aloud
"We can take Friday off?" against "We can take Friday off." "Ian, you've done this
100 times." "For guys who are sick of smelling like their girlfriend's body wash."

### 8. The reductio: if it were true, this would happen
"If carbs made you fat, every elite marathoner eating 500 to 800 grams of carbs,
even myself, would be morbidly obese."

### 9. The honest cost
"You'll struggle with this because you will be perceived as cold, as unbothered,
maybe even as arrogant." The cost makes it believable.

### 10. Proof from today
"I just got back from my morning run." "Today I ran 28 miles." "168 beats per
minute... down to 124." "22 marathons, 16 ultras... I made many mistakes."

### 11. Who they are comes last, as an identity
"Hi, my name is Kian, and my goal is to make you the sharpest and calmest version
of yourself. So if you want more videos like this, hit the follow." "Drop a follow
to stay on the intelligent side of your algorithm." The intro is at the end, never
the start (our row 30).

### 12. The ask is a comment they can answer in one word
"Save this video, comment your Marathon PR, and I'll tell you what you need to
focus on."

## What isn't ours
- Swearing ("shit", "sucks") and "stupidest": the brand list (playbook rule 5).
- Famous faces in the photo row (unlicensed; our collages lost, row 5).
- "Promise me you won't use this maliciously": a bit, and it would ring false on
  pacing advice.
- Jargon names (Leitner system, MQL, Andromeda): P157 says no method names a
  runner wouldn't use. Plain names a runner would say are fine (the Whisper,
  the 30-day check).
- Claims without a source ("studies have shown", "a Harvard study"): we name the
  study or we don't say it (FACTS).

## Ours: the rules to add (for the gate chat to decide)
1. **Open on the myth said as if true, then kill it,** or on a call-out with a
   goal time. Never on us.
2. **The hook is 20 to 30 words; the first item starts by 0:07** (trial against
   P198's 40 to 55).
3. **A 60-second format to trial: 3 named items, about 15 seconds each:** name,
   who uses it (the more extreme the better, and true), the exact instruction
   with a number, what changes, as an identity.
4. **One contrast pair per script** they'd repeat on a club run ("Tired isn't
   injured"; "the runner passing at 35K, not the one being passed").
5. **One scene in tiny detail per item** (the gel wrapper stuck to your hand, the
   watch you keep checking).
6. **The exact words said aloud** (what the runner says to themselves at 28K).
7. **The honest cost** (people will pass you at 10K, and it'll sting).
8. **Proof from today** in the first 15 seconds (Luke's run this morning, his
   3:44:13).
9. **The identity follow at the end:** "Follow if you want to be the one passing
   people at 35K."
10. **The top band crosses out the myth** (struck through in red) and writes the
    truth under it, one per item.
11. **One word in the title in colour** (red or yellow), the feeling word.

## Council (P150)
- **Contrarian:** these are self-improvement creators selling an identity
  (cold, commanding, steel); runners want a time. Copy the structure, not the
  swagger.
- **Assumption-ripper:** "Is the 60-second format really better, or are those
  creators just bigger?" We don't know. It's a trial: one 60-second 3-item reel
  as a trial reel to strangers, against our 1:30 average watch of 16s.
- **Expansionist:** a numbered list of named techniques is the save magnet we've
  been missing (their saves 30% to 64% of likes, ours under 3% of views). Saves
  are what's been weakest on every post since 6 Nov.
- **Outsider:** "progressively more brutal" and the crossed-out "Calm down" are
  the two things I'd remember a day later.
