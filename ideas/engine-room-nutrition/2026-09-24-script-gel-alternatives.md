# What to take instead of gels in a marathon

## Thursday, and I had this wrong

I said Saturday earlier today on the logic that Saturday evening is
when people lay their fuel out. **That is when they arrange it, not
when they get it.**

The constraint on this video is that the viewer has to **buy or
prepare something** before their long run. Thursday gives them two
days and an open shop. Saturday evening gives them nothing.

**Thursday.**

*Hook. The collision (P87):* "If gels wreck your stomach, here is the

*Super hook, dream outcome:* "Ninety seconds: why it happens, four

**[ONE. WHY IT HAPPENS]**
"Sugar pulls water towards it. Put something that concentrated into a
stomach that already has very little blood going to it ⚑ and it sits
there, dragging fluid in with it. That is the cramping and that is the
nausea.

Which is why the fix is usually the same carbs arriving more dilute,
rather than fewer carbs."

**[TWO. THE FOUR. Card held here, in the middle]**
"Four options, and they are on screen.

**Carb drink mix** first, because it solves the problem instead of
dodging it. Forty to eighty grams in a five hundred mil bottle ⚑,
sipped across an hour, arriving at a concentration your stomach can
deal with.

**Chews**, about twenty five grams a pack ⚑, chewed slowly with water.

**Jelly sweets.** About five grams each ⚑, so a dozen is your hour.
Cheap, and it is the same sugar.

**Flat cola** late on, about thirty five grams a can ⚑.

Any of those, with water, gets you to sixty an hour."

## ⚑ Before filming

1. **Every gram figure needs a label checked.** Gels run 20 to 40g,
   drink mixes vary enormously, sweets vary by brand. Say "about"
   every time and put ranges on the card.
2. **The concentration mechanism** ⚑. The direction is well
   established: a concentrated carb solution taken without enough
   fluid sits in the stomach and causes distress. **Never say the word
   osmolality and never put a number on it** (P81).
3. **"A couple of weeks" for gut adaptation** ⚑, the same flag as the
   gels count script. Keep it loose.
4. **Never name a competitor brand as bad.** Name categories. If he
   names products at all, name them neutrally.
5. **The fibre half of the original question is a separate video.**
   Low fibre in the day or two before a race ⚑ belongs in race week.
   Bolting it on here doubles the length.
6. **One boundary line.** If someone's gut reacts to everything, that
   is a doctor, not a video. It does not need a beat, it needs a
   sentence.
7. **The failed-batch count** ⚑[N].
