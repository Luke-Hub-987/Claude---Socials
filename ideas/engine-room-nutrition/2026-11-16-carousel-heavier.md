## Caption
Same runner, 70kg on Monday and 71.5kg on race morning, but faster on race morning. That extra weight is the fuel you packed.

Every gram of carbs your muscles store holds about 3 grams of water with it (2.7 to 4 g in a 2016 study), so the scales go up in race week. About 1 to 2 kg is typical, but that's a coaching estimate built on the water ratio, because no study has weighed runners through a load, so your number may differ.

Heavier also wins, because sub-elite marathon runners who ate more than 7 g of carbs per kilo the day before ran significantly faster (Atkinson, 2011). Loading puts off fatigue in any event over about 90 minutes (Hawley, 1997), so the extra weight pays for itself.

The load for a Sunday race runs 36 to 48 hours, so it starts on Friday morning. Eat 8 to 10 g of carbs per kilo of body weight a day on Friday and Saturday, which is 480 to 600 g at 60 kg, 560 to 700 g at 70 kg, or 640 to 800 g at 80 kg. Spread it over every meal and snack, but keep it white (bagels, white rice, white pasta, crumpets, juice), because fibre and fat leave no room for carbs. Saturday's last meal is a normal plate at about 5pm, not a giant pasta dinner, so breakfast 3 hours before the start tops up what drains overnight.

So when the scales go up on Saturday, don't panic and don't cut carbs, because that's the tank filling.
