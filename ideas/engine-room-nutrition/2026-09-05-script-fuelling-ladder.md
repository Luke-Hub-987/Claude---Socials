# Script — the 5 levels of fuelling a long run

## On-screen text

> **The 5 levels of fuelling a long run**
> *(most runners never leave level 2)*

One sentence, seven words, one parenthetical (HF6). Plain enough for
someone who has never run, and the parenthetical is the self-scoring
hook: everybody wants to know which level they are on, and the answer
is already an accusation.

Held roughly 10 seconds, top of frame, one font.

## Why the ladder carries this content

The five levels are a **progression**, not a list, so the loop only
closes at level 5. The viewer places themselves at level 2 within
fifteen seconds and then cannot leave, because the rest of the video is
the way out. That is a different mechanism from the 10/10 video, which
is the point of rotating devices.

**Note on P42:** this leans closer to how-to than anything else we have
put on ERN, where how-tos sit at 439-645 views. The ladder framing and
the level-2 accusation are what keep it a diagnosis rather than a
lesson. Treat it as a deliberate test of that rule and check the
retention curve against the 1,271-2,158 band.

**Where the product sits:** level 3 opens with a low-fibre carb bar
thirty minutes before heading out. That is ERN's exact window, appearing
as a rung on a ladder rather than a pitch. Say it once, do not sell it.

## Script — v3, cut to 3 levels (~260 words)

Luke's own rewrite is the base. Kept from it: the 12K gate, "wiped for
two days with aches and pains", the people who run 20K and are fine
afterwards, "they don't have a secret hack", "you cannot just run 100Ks

Changed: the hook now leads with the pain and the cost of staying

**[ON SCREEN, held ~10s]**
"The 3 levels of fuelling a long run (most runners never leave level 2)"

**[VERBAL HOOK — pain, cost of staying, dream outcome, loop]**
"If your runs above twelve K leave you wiped for two days, with aches
and pains and nothing in your legs, and you have quietly decided that
eating mid run does not agree with you, that is where you will still be
in three years, blaming your stomach for it. Those people we all know
who run twenty K and look completely fine afterwards do not have a
secret hack. There is a three level scale for this, they are two levels
up, and the jump out of level two is one change."

**[AHA 1 — the frame, not the fix]**
"Level one is running fasted to force your body to burn fat, and fat
burns in the flame of carbohydrate, so you finish with nothing left for
the end. Level two is a gel an hour, started at twenty K because you
felt fine until then, and by that point blood flow has moved away from
your stomach, so it sits there like a brick and you blame the gel
instead of the timing."

**[ON SCREEN ONLY, not spoken]**
"2 accountants. ⚑[N] failed batches. Still pre-launch."

**[AHA 2 — the mechanism]**
"Here is why level two has a ceiling you cannot run a hundred K a week
out of. Carbs get in through two doors. Glucose goes through the first
and caps out around sixty grams an hour ⚑. Fructose goes through the
second, and that is worth another thirty to sixty on top ⚑. That is the
whole reason a pro can push ninety to a hundred grams an hour and you

**[LEVEL 3 — over the b-roll]**
"So level three is getting the first door fully open, on a clock. A low

twenty minutes from twenty minutes in. Sixty grams an hour, every hour,
and the last five K stops being damage limitation. That is the sauce."

**[PAYOFF]**
"Nobody gets out of level two by trying harder. You move one level at a
time, on long runs, and the only level that costs you anything is the
one you are on right now."

**[CTA]**

## Verify before filming (P39) — this script is claim-dense

**Solid, well supported in the literature:**
1. SGLT1 saturating around 60g of glucose per hour.
2. Adding fructose via GLUT5 raising the ceiling to roughly 90g/hour.
3. The ~1:0.8 glucose to fructose ratio (this is Maurten's own basis).
4. SGLT1 being sodium dependent.

**Softened deliberately, and why:**
5. **"120-150g of sugar per hour at long-run effort."** Higher than the
   commonly cited oxidation ceilings. The script says "far too little,
   far too late" instead of quoting the figure. Put a number back only
   with a source.
6. **"Where 80% of runners get stuck."** No source, and a made-up
   percentage is exactly the failure P39 exists to stop. The OST says
   "most runners", which is an opinion he can defend.
7. **"Cannibalises lean muscle."** Overstated for one fasted long run.
   Replaced with "you finish with nothing left for the end."
8. **"Recovery drops from 48 hours to 12."** No source, cut entirely.
9. **"Complete immunity to the wall."** Not defensible, and it is the
   line that would draw the comments. Replaced with "the last five
   kilometres feel like the first."

**Still needs a number from Luke:** ⚑[N], the failed-batch count.
