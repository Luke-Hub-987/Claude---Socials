# Why FINISHER → RACER → HUNTER would have failed

## The second it's on screen: what the viewer does
1. **Reads the arc and places themselves in under a second.** "I'm a racer."
   That's the only question the title asks, and they've answered it. The gap
   closes at 0:01, and the stages about the other two runners are about
   somebody else.
2. **Reads the bracket: the hunter passes you.** So the answer is "be the
   hunter", and every runner can guess how: start slow, finish strong. It's the
   most repeated advice in running. No belief is broken, so there's nothing to
   resolve: "oh, okay, the negative split again". Swipe.
3. **Feels ranked, and ranked low.** Most of our audience are first or second
   marathoners around 4 hours: finishers and racers. The title puts them at the
   bottom of a ladder with Luke at the top. A status threat doesn't make people
   curious; it makes them defensive. The swipe is self-protection.

## Three failures already on our record (my miss: I wrote it against them)
- **Row 10, sorting viewers.** "Who hits the wall" sorted runners into groups
  and got about 7.5K; PLAN-winners lists sorting under "what never worked". The
  arc is a sorting video in a better coat.
- **Row 1, the answer up front.** The bracket names the winner and what he does.
- **Row 26, advice they already know.** The hunter's method is our gel clock and
  hold-back-then-pass, both told to these followers this month.
- And P158: the viewer is the hero, Luke the guide. The arc makes Luke the hero
  (he's the hunter) and the viewer the one being passed.

## Why the arc worked for its creator and wouldn't for us
The athlete → entrepreneur → artist arc is **three stages that are all
admirable.** Wherever you place yourself, you're somebody, and the next stage
is a promotion. "Obsessive ambition & playfulness is rare" works the same way:
**almost everyone believes they're the rare one** (the better-than-average
effect), so it flatters the viewer and they send it to a friend as praise or a
dig. FINISHER is not admirable in that arc; it's the bottom rung. Same device,
opposite feeling.

## Ogilvy and Briar on it
- **Ogilvy:** a headline promises a benefit or gives news; "the consumer isn't a
  moron". There's no news here. The benefit (be the hunter) is a slogan, and
  the reader knows the method already.
- **Briar:** the line-one gap has to be one specific question a stranger can't
  guess. "What does the third one do?" is guessable. The turn has to be
  something they didn't believe; there isn't one.

## The psychology under every winner (proximity to races set aside)
| Lever | What it does in the head | Where it won |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The enemy is outside the viewer** | It isn't your fault: it's the advice you were given, the myth, your body. Relief plus a target. Nobody defends against a video that's on their side | The wall "actually starts" earlier than you were told (184K); ACTUALLY carb load (95K); the 90 minutes is "complete shite" (15K, skip 27.0%); "EVEN THE FIT ONES" (your fitness didn't fail you, your fuel did) |
| **2. A belief broken in the title** | The brain can't leave a contradiction it hasn't resolved; the swipe stops while it checks | Weigh more on race morning, and that's good (151K); your first K should be the slowest (77.8K); stop doing zone 2 (309K); "Cold" behaviours you SHOULD do |
| **3. A moment they're afraid of, in their own race** | Loss is felt twice as hard as gain; a picture of your own race going wrong is a threat you have to deal with | The wall (4 of our top 9); DIE in hour 4 |
| **4. A number that makes it theirs** | "That's me": the 4-hour, the sub-4, the 7 days | 4-HOUR MARATHONS DIE / IN HOUR 4; the goal-time reels |
| **5. A promise of a peak** | You stay for the worst one, the last one, the reveal | (EACH ONE MORE BRUTAL); progressively more brutal (29.7K vs 1,227); (EVEN THE FIT ONES) |

**What the arc had:** lever 4 a little (35K), lever 5 a little (stage 3).
**What it lacked:** 1 (the enemy was the viewer's character), 2 (nothing
broken), 3 (being passed is embarrassment, not danger).

## What a pro might not think of
- **Self-placement ends curiosity.** Any title the viewer can answer about
  themselves in a second ("which one are you?") gets answered, not watched. The
  arc only works when you can't place yourself without the definitions, or when
  every place is good.
- **The comments the "Rare" post got (2,302) came from "comment 'training'"**, a
  lead magnet, not from people saying which one they are. Our one-word comment
  ask would have got one-word comments from the few who stayed.
- **The fourth hour blamed the body; the arc blamed the runner.** That's the
  biggest difference between our best post and this one, and the scorecard
  couldn't see it.

## The scorecard change (the ideas chat owns it)
The arc scored 9 of 10 and would still have flopped. Two tests become
**must-pass, whatever the total**:
- **Test 4, the fight, must pass, as "a belief they hold, broken".** Every
  winner did it; the arc didn't.
- **New must-pass: the enemy is outside the viewer** (the advice, the myth, the
  body, the industry), never their character or their rank.
Plus one question before scoring anything: **can the viewer answer the title
about themselves in one second?** If yes, it's dead.

# Tue 17 Nov instead: £250 SHOES MAKE SOME / 4-HOUR RUNNERS SLOWER / (I PASSED THEM IN DUBLIN)
SLOWER in red. 10/10, both must-passes pass.

## The five levers
1. **Enemy outside:** the shoe marketing that sells a £250 fix. Our own line
   already (gel alternatives, 24 Sep): "Same idea as the carbon plates. Built
   for the sharp end, sold to the rest of us." So it doesn't contradict us; it's
   ours.
2. **Belief broken:** paying more makes you faster. The study says that at
   4-hour pace the gain mostly disappears, and some runners get worse.
3. **A moment they fear:** being passed at 35K in the most expensive shoes in
   the race, and the money (loss aversion twice).
4. **Their number:** 4-hour runners, £250.
5. **The peak:** some get slower; and Luke passed them (the proof in brackets).
Plus a real date (P200): the Black Friday sales, before they buy.

## The facts
| Claim | Source / status |
|---|---|
| Super shoes improve economy about 2.7 to 4.2% at 13 to 18 km/h | Speed-controlled Vaporfly studies (Hoogkamer and others 2018 and later) |
| At slower paces the gain shrank: about 1.4% at 8:00 a mile, about 0.9% (not significant) at 9:40 a mile; some runners were less economical in them | Joubert and others, 16 runners (2023). 4-hour pace is 9:09 a mile, between the two. **Gate chat: confirm the paper and the numbers before the audio says them** |
| The other side (honest cost) | Kipp and others: at slower speeds, an economy gain can turn into a bigger percentage of finish time. So the audio says "for some runners, almost nothing", never "they don't work" |
| Luke passed a lot of runners in super shoes in Dublin (3:44:13 chip) | FACTS, Luke's own race |
| **Needs Luke:** what he wore in Dublin and what it cost | The honest version of the proof |

## The truth problem (the one that would hurt the brand)
- **The study measured oxygen cost, not race times**, in 16 runners; at the
  slower pace the gain wasn't significant, and "some were worse" is one small
  lab sample. MAKE...SLOWER claims a race result the study never measured.
  Commenters who know the Kipp paper (a small economy gain can be a bigger share
  of a slower runner's time) would take it apart, and our edge is that our
  science holds.
- **The payoff contradicts the title (row 32's shape).** The runners Luke passed
  in Dublin slowed because they went out too fast, which is the video's own
  fix. So the shoes didn't make them slower; the pacing did. The title blames
  the shoes and the last stage blames the pacing.

## TAM, uniqueness, memorability
- **TAM:** narrower than any winner. The wall, fuel and the first 10K touch every
  marathoner; this touches the ones buying or defending one product, this week.
- **Unique:** no. Every running channel has made it. Only the Dublin bracket is
  ours, and it's an anecdote.
- **Opinionated:** yes, but on the wrong thing for us: a carb bar brand picking a
  fight with shoes, then landing on known advice (hold back, the gel clock:
  row 26).
- **Unforgettable:** "The shoes buy you seconds. The first 10K costs you
  minutes" is decent; the title isn't quotable because of SOME.
- **Not dull:** the price stops people for a second; nothing holds them after.

## What the scorecard got wrong, and the fix
- **Test 7 (only us)** passed on the bracket. New wording: **fails if the topic is
  already a common video on other running accounts**, whatever bracket is added.
- **Test 4 (a belief broken)** passed on "shoes make you faster". New wording:
  **the belief has to be one they hold confidently, not one they already doubt.**
- **New check: no hedge word in the title** (SOME, MAY, CAN, MOST). If the title
  needs one to be true, the claim isn't strong enough. Find a stronger true claim
  or a different topic.
- **New check: the title's cause and the payoff's cause are the same thing.**
