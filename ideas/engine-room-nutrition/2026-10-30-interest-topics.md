# Wider than the marathon: interest topics with stakes

## 1. The answer: yes, go wider, one circle out

Luke's right. The marathon is home, but for the next 3 months most of the
audience has no race, and "marathon" in every title talks to a smaller
crowd every week. **P166:** until the spring races are 6 weeks out, half
the reels can be about running in general, as long as each has a stake
someone feels today and a bridge to the list or the bar.

What our data actually says against it, and why it doesn't stop us: the
fuel and training videos that flopped (1.6K to 5.3K) had plain titles and
nothing at stake ("how to train your gut"). The ones in your PDFs work
because of the stake and the curiosity, not the subject. So we keep every
rule that made the winners (a stake, the answer held back, a picture in
line one, no stats in the audio, a method with numbers) and drop only
"marathon in every title" for these slots.

**How far out:** one circle, not three (Briar's core, casual and new).

| Circle | Who | What they feel now | Bridge to the money |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Core** | Marathon runners | The next race, months off | The free plan |
| **Casual (the new focus)** | Anyone who runs: parkrun on Saturday, a winter 10K, a spring half | Not getting faster, losing fitness, a flat run | The plan for their next race (any distance, to confirm), and what they eat before they run (the bar's slot) |
| New (not on this account) | Gym, Hyrox, GAA | Different worries | The team-sport plan on the strategy branch (P163), not this feed |

GAA and Hyrox stay off this account: Instagram learns who an account is
for from who watches it, and mixing in team sport would show the reels to
the wrong people.

**Every one of these is tested as a trial reel first.** Trial reels go only
to people who don't follow us, so a flop costs the profile nothing, and
a winner gets shared to the profile.

## 3. Getting them onto the list and towards the bar

1. **One CTA, wider.** "Email us your next race and goal, any distance,
   through the link in the bio, and we'll send you a free plan for it."
   Same form, no extra step (P154). **Can you and Jamie do plans for 5K, 10K
   and the half?** If not, the broad reels keep "if you've got a marathon
   this spring" in the CTA only.
2. **The fuel topics (#4, #5) are the bar's buyers.** Someone who watches a
   video about what to eat before a morning run is closer to buying a
   pre-run bar than someone who watched a marathon pacing video. If #4 or
   #5 wins, a second free guide ("what to eat before you run, by the time
   you run") is the lead magnet that fits the bar best, on the same form.
3. **Trust comes from being right in public, often.** Every interest reel
   ends on a method they can try on their next run, so the second time
   they see us, they already know it worked. That's the trust that makes
   the email feel safe to give.
4. **The bar still never appears in a reel.** It lives in stories, Lives
   and emails (the creative strategy, section 5).

## LLM Council (P150)

- **Contrarian:** Our own grid says fuel and training as the subject did
  1.6K to 5.3K. "Running hacks" is a crowded format with big accounts
  already doing it, and "why you're not getting faster" is the most
  common running video there is. Going wide could make us one more
  generic running account and lose what made the wall work: a famous fear
  on a clock.
- **Assumption-ripper:** Those flops had plain titles and no stake, so
  they don't prove broad topics fail; they prove dull ones do. We're also
  assuming the email needs a marathon: anyone with a race of any distance
  wants a plan, and anyone who eats before a run is a bar customer.
  Luke's assumption to check: "interest topics" can't mean interesting to
  anyone; it has to be runners, or the follows are worthless for the bar.
- **Expansionist:** parkrun gives the Irish and UK audience a race every
  Saturday morning, all winter, so the race clock never actually stops.
  And #4 and #5 point straight at the bar's 30 minutes. If one of them
  wins, it's the first time the content and the product meet.
- **Outsider:** "I don't have a marathon, I'd never watch 'how to pace a
  marathon'. 'Legal hacks that feel like cheating' I'd watch to the end,
  and I'd send it to my running mate."
- **Executor:** Film #1 and #2 this week, both from facts we already have.
  Trial reels first, so it costs nothing if they flop. Luke sends the
  science for #3 and #4 by Thursday. Decide by Fri 13 on the numbers:
  if the interest trials beat the marathon reels on skip and emails per
  1,000 views, Wednesday stays wide through January.
- **Blind cross-review:** The "generic running account" worry holds for
  #2 more than #1, so #2 runs only as a title test, not as the Wednesday
  reel. Everyone backs keeping it to runners (no GAA or gym). The parkrun
  point and the fuel point are the two that change the plan: they keep a
  race clock and put the bar in reach. "Trial reels first" answers the
  Contrarian's data point without arguing with it.
- **Chairman:** **Go one circle wider: runners of any distance, not just
  marathoners, with Wednesday and the weekend trial for interest topics
  with stakes and Monday staying on the marathon for emails. Start Wed 4
  with LEGAL RUNNING HACKS / THAT FEEL LIKE CHEATING (facts ready), test
  #2's two titles Friday, and get the science for the fuel topics, which
  are the ones closest to the bar. Judge it on Fri 13.**
