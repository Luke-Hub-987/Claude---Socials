## The Zero to One secret

The next three weeks can still make you faster, and you do it by
running less. Every runner's instinct in the taper is to panic about
losing fitness and add miles; the gain comes from taking them away. And
the last long run has a different job from every one before it: it is a
rehearsal for race morning.

**[NAMED CONCEPT, PAIN, DREAM, SAVE PROMPT]**
"This is the taper. Panic about losing fitness and squeeze in extra miles, and you carry three weeks of tiredness to the start line. Get it right and you stand there on legs that feel lighter than they have in months. There are four stages, so save this."

**[1. 3 WEEKS OUT: DRESS REHEARSAL]**
"For most plans, three weeks out is your last long run, and it is a dress rehearsal. Get up at the time you will on race day, eat the same breakfast, wear the shoes, socks and kit you will race in, and take your gels at the same times. If anything chafes, upsets your stomach or lets you down, you still have time to change it."

**[2. 2 WEEKS OUT: SHORTER RUNS, SAME PACE]**
"Two weeks out, make your runs shorter and keep the pace. Cut about a quarter of your weekly distance by trimming each run, and keep the same days and some marathon pace work. Your legs stay sharp while months of tiredness drain away." ⚑1

**[3. 10 DAYS OUT: LAST BIG SESSION]**
"Ten days out is your last big session. Make it one you know well, like a few K at your marathon pace. After this, the fitness you have is the fitness you race with, so if you miss a run, let it go, and do not try to catch up." ⚑2

**[REHOOK]**
"And the last one is where tapers usually go wrong."

**[4. THE LAST 10 DAYS: SAVE THE ENERGY]**
"The fourth is the energy. As the miles drop, you will probably feel restless and want to test yourself. That energy is what you race with, so keep it. Skip the extra runs, the gym and the long days on your feet, and go to bed a little earlier." ⚑3
