# Where we go from here: creative topics, the brand, and the list

## 1. What went wrong with the last list (my miss)

I scored "timed for now" as a point, so two topics with no feeling in them
(a watch reading long, picking a goal) came out on top because the date
fitted. Timing can't rescue a topic nobody feels. **P165, the "who cares"
test:** before a topic is scored, would a runner whose race is 5 months away
stop scrolling for it? It passes if it's a fear they still carry from the
last race, a picture of race day they want, or a number they'll use on every
long run from now to the start line. Trivia (why the watch says 42.7) and
admin (pick a goal) fail, whatever the date.

And the gap to spring isn't the problem it looks like. On Mon 26 Oct, 11
people signed up with no new reel: old race-day videos kept working after
the races were over. Runners watch race-day content to picture their own
race, months out. **The pacing videos you like work in any month for that
reason.** What changes in the gap is the job of each post, not the race
clock.

## 2. The through line (Alex Garcia)

One sentence every video answers to, and the CTA sits right on it:

**"The exact plan for your marathon, K by K, so it doesn't fall apart."**

The free personalised plan is that sentence as a product, so every video
that keeps to it is one step from the email (Briar: stay close to the
money). Videos that don't fit it (the watch, the goal) are the ones that
felt like nothing.

## 5. The brand: guide, not hero (P158)

**The tops.** Never a "new merch" post: it's the bar-photo problem again.
Three uses, in this order:
1. **A uniform.** You and Jamie wear them in every reel, so the same top,
   the same blue title and the same opening line become how people know us
   before they read anything (Briar: "how to use clothes like a top 1%
   creator").
2. **A reward, not a sale.** Anyone who used our plan and sends their race
   result (with permission to share it) gets a top. That gives you proof
   stories and people wearing the name at parkruns and spring races.
3. **Run clubs.** Bring them to an Irish run club or parkrun and film
   runners answering one question ("What's your spring marathon goal?").
   It's a new format (interviews rank high on Briar's list), real people,
   real faces in the video (never on the cover), and every runner you meet
   is a plan sign-up.

**Proof (the Island A to B).** The strongest trust we can earn is a
runner's own before and after, in their words (the shape: "walked at 32K
last time, ran the whole way with the plan"; only real results, never made
up). The viewer is the hero, we're the guide. Ask on
Sunday's story; with permission, these become reels and emails.

**Sell in stories and Lives, never in reels (SNL).** Reels stay advice.
Stories run the daily questions (the stories plan), and once a month a Live
with Jamie: "Bring your spring marathon goal, we'll plan your first 10K
live." Every answer gets the plan link. The bar is talked about where the
warm people are.

**Honesty as a brand voice (the Hormozi amplifier).** In emails and
stories, admit the downside that proves the upside (the shape: "it's
plain on purpose, because of when you eat it"; only true things about the
bar, from you). Never in reels.

## 6. What a professional would look at that we're not

1. **The gap is the testing season.** Spring runners try their fuel on
   long runs from January to March ("nothing new on race day"). That's when
   they pick what they'll use, so that's when we should be selling, not
   race week. Your PDF's race-pack note (gels, chews and electrolytes to try)
   fits this exactly. **Is it a real plan?** If so, it's the product for
   January.
2. **Per-video attribution with no extra step.** Swap "link in bio" for
   "comment PLAN" with an auto-DM (a tool like ManyChat).
   It's no more taps than going to the bio, every reel gets its own word, so
   you'd know sign-ups per video, and the comments help the reel too. It
   swaps a step, it doesn't add one (P154), but you said no to the last
   idea that touched the sign-up, so I'd test it on one reel and drop it if
   that day's sign-ups fall below the no-post baseline.
3. **A small paid test behind the best email reel.** €5 to €10 a day
   behind a goal-time video, to Irish and UK runners, for 2 weeks. It gives
   the one clean number we've never had: what an email costs. That decides
   whether ads are worth it at launch.
4. **Post every reel to YouTube Shorts too.** Same file, no extra work.
   People search YouTube for "how to pace a marathon" in any month, and a
   Short keeps getting found long after Instagram stops showing it.
5. **Somewhere for people to belong between races.** A free Strava club
   for spring marathon runners: Sunday long runs, a weekly check-in on
   stories. Irish and UK runners already live on Strava, and it keeps
   people with us for 5 months without a reel having to do it.
6. **Repost the winners.** Most of today's followers never saw the wall,
   carb load or how hard. Trial reels show them to non-followers; repost
   one every 2 to 4 weeks with a new frame one (the weekend slot above).
   Carb load goes back up 2 weeks before the first spring races.
7. **Collabs beyond us two.** The collab with your account already works.
   Do the same with Irish and UK run clubs, physios and mid-size running
   creators: one collab post a month.
8. **What's in the emails decides the sales.** The welcome flow built
   around the race date is already written on the strategy branch (P162).
   It's ready to go live; nothing here needs to change for it.
9. **A launch date.** A list with no date cools. A founding offer with a
   date (or a race pack for testers in January) turns "interested" into
   "bought".
10. **Judge formats on emails per 1,000 views each month,** not views.
    Log sign-ups per day against posting days (P154), and once a month
    compare the Mon format, the Wed format and the tests.
