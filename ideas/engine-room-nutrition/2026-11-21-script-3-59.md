## Script

"You've seen so many marathoners finish at 3:59, but it's not luck. In 9.5 million finish times, runners near a round number at 40K sped up, so the minute was in their legs. Here's how to find it on purpose."

**[THE ROUND NUMBER]**

"Finish times bunch up just under round numbers like 3:30 and 4:00. In the minute just under 3 hours, about 50% more finished than in the minute just over it. Pacers and qualifying times don't explain it, because the runners did it themselves. Save this and screenshot the clock card, so you know your number."

**[THE LAST 2.2K]**

"The minute shows up in the last 2.2K, and only when the clock is close. Runners near 4-hour pace at 40K sped up when the round number was in reach, nearly 4 hours in. Your legs said empty, but the clock said go."

"Send this to whoever you're running your next marathon with."

**[YOUR 40K CHECK]**

"So look at the clock at the 40K sign. On a sub-4, 3:47 there means it's still yours, so go. That's about 12 and a half minutes of running left at sub-4 pace. The other round numbers are on the card. But if you're 10 minutes off, no sprint finds that, so run your own race home."

"And you don't need a marathon to use any of this."

**[YOUR NEXT RUN]**

"Pick a round number for your next long run or parkrun, like 25 minutes or 2 hours. Check the clock 2K from the end, and if the round number's in reach, go after it. Do it every week, so the clock check is a habit long before you reach a 40K sign."

"There's more your legs are hiding, so follow for the next one."

## Caption
So many marathoners finish at 3:59 because round numbers pull runners home. In a study of about 9.5 million marathon finishes (Allen, Dechow, Pope and Wu, Management Science, 2017), finish times bunch up just under 3:00, 3:30, 4:00 and the other round numbers. In the minute just under 3 hours, 51.4% more runners finished than in the minute just over. Pacers, qualifying times and other runners don't explain it: runners close to a round number speed up over the last 2.2K.

Sub-4, 3:36, 3:41, 3:47, 3:53. Sub-4:30, 4:03, 4:09, 4:15, 4:22. At or under it, the round number is yours, so go. Well over it, run your own race home; no sprint finds 10 minutes.

No marathon booked? Pick a round number for your next long run or parkrun and check the clock 2K from the end.
