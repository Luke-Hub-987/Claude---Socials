## Script

"Never decide to walk at 30K of your marathon until you've waited 10 minutes. That voice saying just walk this hill isn't weakness. Cyclists who rode 3 hours without carbs saw their blood sugar fall by almost half, and the effort felt harder."

**[THE 30K VOICE]**

"There are 3 things to do the moment it starts, and each one is harder than the last. First, know the voice when it comes: you'll get going again shortly, I'll never do this again, why am I doing this. That's a fuel gauge reading low, so treat it as your cue to act. Save this so the 3 steps are with you when it starts."

**[THE GEL THAT'S DUE]**

"Second, take the gel that's due, even if you're sick of the texture. On a 4-hour marathon, that's the 2:50 gel, right as the voice starts. Wash it down with a few sips of water if a station's close. The gel you least want at 30K is the one your head needs most."

"Send this to the friend who walked at 30K and blamed their head."

**[THE 10 MINUTES]**

"Third, and the hardest, make no decision about walking for 10 minutes. The sugar starts reaching your blood within minutes. So keep running, 15 to 20 seconds a K slower if you need to, with shorter steps. Your watch already shows the race time, so after the 2:50 gel, decide nothing before 3:00."

"The last 10K is where you find out whose voice it was."

**[THE LAST 10K]**

"If it was fuel, you'll be counting down the 10K signs instead of arguing with yourself. Your last gel on a 4-hour clock is the 3:20 one. But if you're dizzy, shivering or weaving across the road, that's more than fuel, so stop and find the medical tent."

"Follow if you want to run the last 10K with a quiet head."

## Caption
Never decide to walk at 30K until you've waited 10 minutes. When the voice starts ("just walk this hill", "you'll get going again shortly", "I'll never do this again", "why am I doing this"), treat it as a fuel gauge, not a verdict. Take the gel that's due even if you're sick of the texture, with 3 or 4 sips if you're passing a station. Keep running, 15 to 20 seconds a K slower if you need it, with shorter steps, and make no decision about walking for 10 minutes (on a 4:00 clock: the 2:50 gel, no decision before 3:00).

The clock (30 g gels): first at 20 minutes, then every 30, none in the last 20. For a 4:00 marathon that's 0:20, 0:50, 1:20, 1:50, 2:20, 2:50 and 3:20, so a gel is due right around 30K. With 20 to 25 g gels, one every 20 minutes; with 40 g gels, every 40.

Your 30K, the gel due nearest it, and your last gel: 3:30 marathon, 30K at about 2:29, the 2:20 gel, last gel 2:50. 3:45, 30K at about 2:40, the 2:50 gel, last gel 3:20. 4:00, 30K at about 2:51, the 2:50 gel, last gel 3:20. 4:30, 30K at about 3:12, the 3:20 gel, last gel 3:50.

Why it isn't weakness: when 6 men cycled for 3 hours without carbs (Nybo and others, 2003), their blood sugar fell from 5.2 to 2.9 mmol/L, the effort felt harder, and their brains took up less glucose. With glucose drinks, blood sugar held steady. 3 hours is about 31.6K at 4-hour pace. It's a small study of cyclists, so it's the reason behind the voice, not a promise.

The 10 minutes is a rule for decisions, not a lab number: sugar starts reaching your blood within minutes, so give it time before you trust the voice.

If you're dizzy, shivering or weaving, that's more than fuel. Go to the medical tent.
