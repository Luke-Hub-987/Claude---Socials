## Script

"The voice telling you to walk at 30K isn't weakness. Cyclists who rode about that long without carbs, 3 hours, saw their blood sugar fall by almost half. As less of it reached their brains, every turn of the pedals felt harder."

**[THE 30K VOICE]**

"Around 30K, a voice starts saying why am I doing this, or just walk a bit. That voice is a fuel gauge reading low, so treat it as your cue to act. Save this so the plan's with you when you hear it."

**[THE GEL THAT'S DUE]**

"A gel is due around now if you've been on the clock since 20 minutes. For a 4-hour marathon, that's the 2:50 gel, a minute before you get there. It's the one you'll least want, but take it anyway, even if you're sick of the texture. Wash it down with 3 or 4 sips if you're passing a station. The gel you least want at 30K is the one your head needs most."

"Send this to the friend who walked at 30K and blamed their head."

**[THE 10-MINUTE RULE]**

"After that gel, you make no decision about walking for 10 minutes. The sugar starts reaching your blood within minutes, so give it time before you listen to that voice again. Hold your pace, and shorten your steps if your legs feel heavy."

"The last 10K is where you find out whose voice it was."

**[THE LAST 10K]**

"If it was fuel, the voice fades, and you're counting down the signs instead of arguing with yourself. Keep the clock going, and for a 4-hour marathon, your last gel is the 3:20 one. But if you're dizzy, shivering or weaving across the road, that's more than fuel, so go to the medical tent."

"Follow if you want to run the last 10K with a quiet head."

## Caption
The plan for the 30K voice: when it starts ("why am I doing this", "just walk a bit"), treat it as a fuel gauge, not a verdict. Take the gel that's due even if you're sick of the texture, with 3 or 4 sips if you're passing a station. Hold your pace, shorten your steps, and make no decision about walking for 10 minutes.

The clock (30 g gels): first at 20 minutes, then every 30, none in the last 20. For a 4:00 marathon that's 0:20, 0:50, 1:20, 1:50, 2:20, 2:50 and 3:20, so a gel is due right around 30K. With 20 to 25 g gels, one every 20 minutes; with 40 g gels, every 40.

Your 30K, the gel due nearest it, and your last gel: 3:30 marathon, 30K at about 2:29, the 2:20 gel, last gel 2:50. 3:45, 30K at about 2:40, the 2:50 gel, last gel 3:20. 4:00, 30K at about 2:51, the 2:50 gel, last gel 3:20. 4:30, 30K at about 3:12, the 3:20 gel, last gel 3:50.

Why it isn't weakness: when 6 men cycled for 3 hours without carbs (Nybo and others, 2003), their blood sugar fell from 5.2 to 2.9 mmol/L, the effort felt harder, and their brains took up less glucose. With glucose drinks, blood sugar held steady. 3 hours is about 31.6K at 4-hour pace. It's a small study of cyclists, so it's the reason behind the voice, not a promise.

The 10 minutes is a rule for decisions, not a lab number: sugar starts reaching your blood within minutes, so give it time before you trust the voice.

If you're dizzy, shivering or weaving, that's more than fuel. Go to the medical tent.
