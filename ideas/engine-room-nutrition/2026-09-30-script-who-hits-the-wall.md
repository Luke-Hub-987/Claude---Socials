## The secret (Thiel)

**You would think getting fitter makes the wall less likely. In four
million marathon results, runners hit it far more often in the years
just before a PB.** Chasing the time is the risk. And the wall is not
a fast runner's problem: the slower the finish, up to about five hours,
the more people hit it.

| Runner | Opens on the unexpected | In-race action |

| 1. PB chasers | The share jumps from under a quarter to over a third | Race the pace your long runs proved; back off at 10K if it feels like work |

| 3. Fast starters | It rarely feels fast at the time | First 5K five to ten seconds a K slower than goal |
| 4. 3 to 5 hour runners | The slower the finish, the more likely, the one nobody expects | Carbs at twenty minutes, then every twenty |

## ⚑ Science, checked against the full paper

**Smyth B, Lawlor A (2021) "How recreational marathon runners hit the
wall: A large-scale data analysis of late-race pacing collapse in the
marathon." PLOS ONE.** Over 4 million race records, 270 marathons.
Hitting the wall = slowing at least 25% for at least 5K after 20K.

| # | Claim | In the paper |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Over a third in the years before a PB, under a quarter earlier | "36% of all runners hit the wall in the 3 years before a recent PB compared with just 23% in earlier years." Explanation offered: "may also be explained by risk-taking behaviour and sub-optimal pacing decisions when runners are chasing a PB". Hence "the researchers think" |
| 2 | Around 30K, about 10K long, about half an hour | Slowdown begins at 29.6K (men) and 29.3K (women), lasts 10.7K and 9.6K; finish-time cost relative to PB 31.5 min (men), 33.2 min (women) |
| 3 | 28% of men, 17% of women; men overestimate | "28% of male runners hit the wall compared with only 17% of female runners"; the paper cites prior work that "male runners were found to consistently overestimate their marathon abilities, leading to more aggressive and risky pacing strategies", and that women pace more consistently. Hence "the research links that to" |
| 4 | A fast start is a strong predictor | Cited prior work: fast starts "increased the likelihood that a runner would go on to hit the wall later", and "aggressive pacing to be a strong predictor of subsequent slowing". "Five to ten seconds a K" matches the first half video |
| 5 | The slower the finish, up to about five hours, the more hit it | "The proportion of runners hitting the wall increases steadily with recent PB times between 3 and 5 to 5.5 hours." **"You are out there longer, so start your carbs early" is our reasoning**, said as a reason, and it matches the wall video's "every twenty minutes" |

**Not claimed:** an overall percentage for everyone (the paper reports
men and women separately), anything about Luke hitting the wall, any
grams per hour.
