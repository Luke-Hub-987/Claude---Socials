# What to take instead of gels in a marathon

## Why this one matters more than a normal idea

**It came from a real person asking.** Every other topic on the board
was chosen by us from data. This one has a demand signal that no
amount of analysis produces: someone went out of their way to ask.

It is also **ERN's exact commercial territory**. The bar is a gel
alternative, so this is the most natural product mention the account
will ever get, and it arrives as the answer to a question the viewer
asked rather than a pitch.

## OST

> **What to take instead of gels in a marathon**
> *(and still hit 60g an hour)*

- **Nine words, 42 characters.** Two clean lines, well clear of the
  play button (P89).
- **"Instead of gels" is the exact query.** People type it. The
  phrasing is lifted from how the question was actually asked.
- **Instruction verb**, and **marathon in line one**, which is where
  every big number on this account has come from (P86).
- **The whisper kills the objection and withholds the answer.** The
  first thing anyone thinks is "yes but then I will under fuel", and
  "still hit 60g an hour" answers it while saying nothing about what
  to take (P80).
- **Specificity as credibility.** A real number in the whisper reads
  as someone who has actually worked it out (P73, Cashvertising).

**Alternative, wider gate, less credible:** *What to take instead of
gels in a marathon* *(when your stomach says no)*.

**Rejected:** any version leading with "if gels make you sick". It
gates to people who already know they have the problem and loses
everyone who has not connected their bad races to their fuelling yet.

## The collision for the hook (P87)

This is the strongest part and it reframes the whole question:

> **A gel is about twenty five grams of sugar in a mouthful of
> liquid.** The same twenty five grams in a bottle of water goes down
> fine for most people. ⚑

**The problem is usually the concentration rather than the gel**, and
a viewer who has spent three years assuming their stomach is broken
has never heard that. It is a real mechanism (P81), it makes the rest
of the video obvious once stated, and it sets up the alternatives as
"the same carbs arriving more dilute" rather than a list of swaps.

Phrase it positively on delivery. The banned construction is very
close to the surface on this one.

## The save card

> **60g AN HOUR WITHOUT GELS** ⚑
> Carb drink mix — 40 to 80g per 500ml bottle
> Chews — about 25g a pack
> Jelly sweets — about 5g each, so a dozen is 60g
> Flat cola — about 35g a can
> A bar — about 30g
> **Practise it on a long run. Never debut it on race day.**

A table with grams on it is exactly the card that took saves from 34
to 90 (P84).

## Beats

1. **Why gels do this to people.** Concentration, the mouthful of
   liquid, and what happens when it arrives without water. This is the
   news and it is the reason most people never fixed it.
2. **The alternatives, with the grams.** Drink mix first, because it
   is the one that solves the concentration problem rather than
   dodging it, and it is the honest answer to what he was asked.
   Then chews, sweets, cola, solid food.
3. **The catch nobody mentions.** Course drinks will not have your mix
   in them, so anything liquid means carrying it or planning your
   bottles, and everything here has to be tested on a long run first.
   Ties to the deadline and to the long run videos.

**The bar line, and it has never fitted this well:** "and if what you
actually want is something solid that is not another gel, that is the
entire reason we are making a bar."

## ⚑ Before filming

- **Every gram figure needs the label checked.** Gels vary from 20 to
  40g, drink mixes vary enormously, and sweets vary by brand. Say
  "about" everywhere and put the ranges on the card.
- **The concentration mechanism** ⚑. The direction is well
  established: a concentrated carb solution taken without enough fluid
  sits in the stomach and causes distress. **Do not put a number on
  osmolality and do not say the word.** Jargon is not the mechanism
  (P81).
- **The fibre half of his question** is a different video. Low fibre
  in the day or two before a race is the standard advice ⚑ and it
  belongs in the race week run. **Do not bolt it on here**, it doubles
  the length.
- **Never name a competitor brand as bad.** Name categories, and if he
  names products, name them neutrally as things that exist.
- **This is not medical advice.** If someone's gut reacts to
  everything, that is a doctor, and one line says so.
- **The failed-batch count** ⚑[N].
