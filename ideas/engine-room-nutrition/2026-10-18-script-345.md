## Script v1 (~344 words, ~1825 characters, about 1:40)

"If you're running a marathon in the next few weeks, you should let the three forty-five pacer get away from you at the start, and that's your race going to plan."

"Sit on that flag from the gun and you can be walking by thirty-two K. Do this instead, and you'll go past that same flag before the finish."

"There's a gap between you and that pacer that your watch can't show you, and it changes how you should start. Save this."

**[THE METHOD: FOUR NUMBERS]**
"Here's the method first. Write four numbers on the back of your hand, or make them your phone's lock screen: fifty-four, one forty-six, two thirty-nine and three thirty-two. They're the times you want to see at ten, twenty, thirty and forty K."

**[0-10K: 5:20 A K, 54 MIN]**
"For the first ten K, run five twenty a K on your watch. You should get all ten words of "I am running a marathon today and I feel good" out in one breath, in your normal voice. Check the clock at the ten K sign. Around fifty-four minutes is perfect."

**[10-30K: 5:13 A K, 1:46, 2:39]**
"From ten to thirty, drop to five thirteen. It's a few seconds quicker than real three forty-five pace on purpose, because your watch adds distance on every bend. At twenty, you want around one forty-six, still ten words but a bit breathy. At thirty, around two thirty-nine, and about five words before you need a breath."

"Which brings me back to that gap."

**[30K TO THE LINE: 5:13, THEN 5:03]**
"The pacer crossed the start mat before you did, maybe a minute before. So when you finally draw level with that flag, you're already a minute ahead of three forty-five on your own chip. Chase it early and you've banked time you'll pay back at thirty-two K. Hold five thirteen to forty, about three thirty-two at that sign, and you'll catch them anyway. Then run the last two K at five o three, and you finish around three forty-three."

"Want a pace band for your own goal? Email us your race and goal time through the link in the bio and we'll send you one, free."

## The pace band (same method and GPS buffer as sub-4)
Real 3:45 pace is 5:20 a K. The watch paces are a few seconds quick
because a GPS watch reads about 1% long in a race.

| Sign | Watch pace | Time at the sign (watch 1% long) | Watch spot on | Talk test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10K | 5:20 | 53:52 | 53:20 | 10 words, one breath, normal voice |
| 20K | 5:13 | 1:46:33 | 1:45:30 | 10 words, a bit breathy |
| 30K | 5:13 | 2:39:14 | 2:37:40 | about 5 words |
| 40K | 5:13 | 3:31:55 | 3:29:50 | 2 or 3 words |
| Finish | 5:03 last 2K | 3:43:07 (3:44:13 if 1.5% long) | 3:40:55 | |

**Mat gap check:** the plan runs about 2 minutes under 3:45 by chip. Start
a minute behind the pacer and you draw level around 32 to 35K.

## Caption
How to pace a 3:45 marathon at every 10K. Write four numbers on your hand or your lock screen: 54:00, 1:46, 2:39 and 3:32. Those are the times to see at the 10K, 20K, 30K and 40K signs.

Run 5:20 a K on your watch for the first 10K, then 5:13 a K to 40K, then 5:03 for the last 2K. The watch paces are a few seconds quicker than real 3:45 pace (5:20 a K) because a GPS watch reads about 1% long in a race, so trust the clock at each sign more than the pace on your wrist. That plan finishes around 3:43.

Effort check with the sentence "I am running a marathon today and I feel good": all ten words in one breath to 10K, ten slightly breathy to 20K, about five by 30K, two or three at the end.

Don't chase the 3:45 pacer at the start. They crossed the start mat before you, so drawing level early means you're already ahead of 3:45 on your own chip. Run your numbers and you'll pass them late.

Other goals: sub-4 is on the page already; for 3:30, 4:30 or anything else, email us your race and goal time through the link in the bio and we'll send your pace band, free.
