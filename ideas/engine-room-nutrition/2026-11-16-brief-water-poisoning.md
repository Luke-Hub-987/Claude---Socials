# WATER IS POISONING / 4-HOUR MARATHONERS / (1 IN 8 AT BOSTON)
OST score 10/10 (OST-SCORECARD.md). Red word: POISONING.

## Why this, after the fourth hour
- **The fourth hour's formula, a new twist.** It's the same viewer (4-hour
  marathoners), on race day, with a myth everyone's heard ("drink at every
  station") killed with proof in line one. The twist is new: not too few
  carbs, too much water. It isn't a sequel (row 31): the myth, the line one,
  the method and the picture are all different.
- **The proof is a hospital study, not an opinion.** Almond and others, *NEJM*
  2005, 2002 Boston Marathon: of 488 finishers who gave blood, **13% (1 in
  8) had hyponatremia** (blood sodium diluted, 135 mmol/L or less) and 0.6%
  were critical (120 or less). **Finishing over 4 hours: odds 7.4 times
  those of runners under 3:30.** **Gaining weight during the race: odds 4.2
  times.** **Sports drinks did no better than water**: the volume drunk was
  the cause. (PubMed 15829535; Harvard Gazette, Apr 2005.)
- **It's ours.** The 4-hour runner is the person our best reel was about. And
  it pays off the carousel (GAIN 1.5KG BEFORE YOUR MARATHON) with the
  opposite rule for race day, which is the repeat line.
- **The Outsider stops:** water poisoning runners is a fact non-runners
  repeat.

## Line one (myth, kill, proof, in Luke's words)
"Everyone tells you to drink at every water station so you don't dry out, but
if you're running a marathon in about 4 hours, that advice is how runners end
up in the medical tent."
**Gap test:** a stranger asks "how can water put me in the tent?" Answered by
stage 1 (the Boston blood tests), and the fix (how much, then) by the last
stage. Nothing earlier gives it away.

## Pain and dream (one line each)
- **Pain:** you drank at every station like you were told, and at 35K you're
  sick, puffy, with a headache, and slower than the runner who barely drank.
- **Dream:** you'll know before race day exactly how much you need, from one
  weigh-in on a long run.

## The method (stages, one clock: race day)
1. **Boston, 2002:** 1 in 8 finishers had diluted blood; the 4-hour runners
   were the worst hit (7 times the odds of the 3:30s), because a slower runner
   passes more stations and sweats less per station.
2. **The sports drink doesn't save you:** in the study it did no better than
   water. The amount was the problem, not the salt.
3. **The test:** weigh yourself before and after your next long run. Heavier
   at the end means you drank more than you sweated (ACSM 2007: don't drink
   more than you lose in sweat). Lighter by a kilo or so is normal.
4. **Race day:** drink when you're thirsty, not because a table is there; the
   gels on the clock are your fuel (the fourth hour's gel clock), with sips.
**Repeat line:** "Gain weight before your marathon. Never during it."
**Honest cost:** on a hot day you'll need more; this is for the races our
viewers run (Ireland, the UK, Europe, autumn and spring).
