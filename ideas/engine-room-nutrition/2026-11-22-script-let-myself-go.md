"This was me, and you've said it too: 'I've let myself go'. Diets never stuck, until I picked up these 10 habits. I've since dropped almost 10 kilos and run marathons and ultras, but each habit gets more niche, so stay for number 10."

**[1/10: 66 DAYS, NOT 21]**

"Give it 66 days, not 21. In a study of 96 people, a new habit took about 66 days to stick, and missing one day didn't break it. The 21-day idea came from a plastic surgeon. Save this, then screenshot the list for your fridge."

**[2/10: WHEN AND WHERE]**

"Write down when and where you'll run, like Tuesday at 7am, the canal. In one study, 91% who wrote it down exercised, against about a third who didn't."

"Copy the NHS beginner week, so run 1 minute and walk 90 seconds."

**[4/10: TALK PACE]**

"Run slow enough to say a full sentence without gasping for air, so it feels easy."

**[5/10: 3 A WEEK]**

"Run 3 days a week with rest days between, so your legs catch up."

"Do 10 minutes of squats and slow calf raises in the kitchen, twice a week, slow on the way down. In sport, strength training cut injuries to about a third."

## Caption
"I've let myself go." I said it too, and diets never stuck until these 10 habits. I've since dropped almost 10 kilos and run marathons and ultras. They're what worked for me, not a weight-loss promise, so here they are, in order.

1. Give it about 66 days, not 21. In a study of 96 people, a new daily habit took 66 days on average to become automatic, from 18 to over 200, and missing one day didn't set people back (Lally and others, 2010). The 21-day idea came from a plastic surgeon. 2. Write down when and where you'll run. In one study, 91% of people who named when and where exercised, against about a third who didn't (Milne, Orbell and Sheeran, 2002). 3. Copy the NHS Couch to 5K first week: a brisk 5-minute walk, then 1 minute running and 90 seconds walking, about 20 minutes, 3 times a week. 4. Run slow enough to say a full sentence without gasping for air. 5. 3 runs a week, a rest day between. 6. 10 minutes of squats and slow calf raises (slow on the way down) in the kitchen, twice a week; in sport, strength training cut injuries to about a third (Lauersen and others, 2014). 7. Something with carbs before you run, like toast with jam at 5pm before a 7pm run: 8 minutes of jogging won't empty your legs, but a long gap since lunch leaves you flat. A proper dinner after. 8. If a week felt hard, repeat it. 9. Aim for a parkrun: a volunteer tail walker always finishes last, so you can't. 10. Get your free parkrun barcode (parkrun is free, you just register) and put the Saturday after week 9 in your calendar.

Comment START and I'll email you the full list and your first run.

## LLM Council (P150)
- **Contrarian:** ten items in 75 seconds is a lot; if habit 1 doesn't land, the list is a wall of advice they half-know (row 26). Habits 4, 5 and 8 are the most familiar.
- **Assumption-ripper:** we assume "I let myself go" is said by this viewer and not only by people who never ran. It is (Luke's restarter, P236), but the script must never sound like it's for marathoners. It doesn't: no marathon, no goal time.
- **Expansionist:** the list is the email. The full list plus "your first run" is the comment-word lead magnet, and number 10 (book a parkrun for week 10) gives the email a date to follow up on.
- **Outsider:** "why is a nutrition brand telling me about habits?" Habit 7 answers it in one line, without a product.
- **Executor:** film as a 10-card list filling in (Rory's on-screen list), Luke to camera, one take. The list card is the save.
- **Blind cross-review:** the Contrarian's point changed the order: the two surprising facts (66 days, 91%) lead, the familiar ones sit in the middle, the tail walker and the booked parkrun close. The Outsider's point holds: habit 7 stays plain, no bar.
