If you're running a marathon in the next few weeks, you'll probably sleep badly the night before, and it won't cost you your race.

It's 2am. You're staring at the ceiling, doing the maths: if I drop off now, I'll get 4 hours. You check your phone, your heart's going, and by the time the alarm goes off, you're sure the race is gone.

Get the 2 nights before right, and you lie there calm, eyes shut, knowing the sleep that counts is already in the bank, and you run like nothing happened.

And there's one thing to do at 3am that changes the whole night. Save this, and send it to whoever's racing with you.

The night that counts is 2 nights before. For a Sunday race, that's Friday. Bed 30 minutes earlier than normal, phone out of the room, and no alarm if you can get away with it.

By 6pm, everything's laid out. Bib pinned on, gels in your pocket, laces double-knotted, watch on charge. Set 2 alarms, so you're not waking every hour to check the first one.

Then lights out at your normal time, not 8 o'clock. Go to bed early and you just lie awake longer, running the race in your head.

So what do you do at 3am?

Don't look at the clock, and don't pick up your phone. Eyes shut, lie still, and pick a word, say MARATHON. Think of things that start with each letter. M, map, milk, mountain. A, apple, anchor. Your head can't run the race and do that at the same time. And even if you never drop off, lying there resting still counts. When the alarm goes, get up and go. You're ready.
