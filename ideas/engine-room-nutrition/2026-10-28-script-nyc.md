## Script (314 words, 1610 characters, about 1:35)

If you're running New York, the loudest mile of the whole race is the one most likely to wreck it. And you get there straight off the quietest.

Go with the noise there, and you end up walking up Fifth Avenue in front of the biggest crowd you'll ever see.

Hold it, and you run into Central Park passing people.

And there's one bridge where your watch can stop making sense. What you do on it decides the rest of your race. Send this to whoever's running New York with you.

**[MILES 0-2: THE BRIDGE]**
You start straight up a bridge. For a 4 hour marathon, that's 9:30 a mile. You should get all 10 words of "I am running a marathon today and I feel good" out in one breath. Don't try to win it back on the way down.

Want this worked out for your goal? Email us your race and goal time through the link in the bio, it's free. Right, Brooklyn.

**[MILES 2-13: BROOKLYN]**
Brooklyn is long and mostly flat. Settle at 9:10 a mile, and not a second quicker. If you're about 45 seconds behind at halfway, that's perfect.

Now the bridge I told you about, and the mile after it.

**[MILES 15-20: THE QUIET BRIDGE, THE LOUD AVENUE]**
Around mile 15 you go onto the lower level of a bridge. No crowd, just breathing, and it climbs. Your watch can lose signal under there, so forget the pace and go by your breathing. If you can still get about 5 words out, you're fine, even at 9:35. Then you come off it onto First Avenue, and it's the loudest mile you'll ever run. Run 8:50, and no faster, however loud it gets.

**[MILE 20 TO THE LINE: FIFTH AVENUE]**
From about mile 22, Fifth Avenue drags uphill for 2 miles. Shorter, quicker steps, around 9:15. Then into Central Park, and if you've still got 5 words, go. That's where you pass everyone who loved First Avenue a bit too much.
