If you've said 'I don't need gels', the first 2 hours of a 4-hour marathon agree with you, and that's the lie. By 2 hours about half your fuel is gone. Here's how the next 2 hours go, and the fix.

By 2 hours you've burned about half the carbs you store, but you feel completely fine. That's with no carbs at all, so no gels, no drink mix and no sweets. At this pace you burn well over 100 grams an hour, but you only start with about 500. Save this and screenshot the gel clock, so you've got your times.

About 3 hours in, around 32K, the stores in your legs run short. You're trying just as hard, but the pace on your watch keeps sliding. In one lab study, trained cyclists with no carbs were done at about 3 hours, but fed carbs, they lasted about 4.

Your head goes next, because blood sugar drops too. In a 3-hour study with no sugar, it fell by almost half, so the same effort felt harder. The first 2 hours feel free, but the last hour sends the bill.

So the fix has to start while you still feel great.

Take your first gel at 20 minutes, then one every 30 minutes, with a few sips of water. With 30 gram gels that's 60 grams an hour, but drink mix or sweets count too. The sugar burns alongside your stores, so there's still some left when you reach 32K. On a 4-hour marathon your last one goes in at 3:20. If you miss one, let it go, and never take 2 at once.
