## Where I'm pushing back, and why

1. **Our only motion data says no on the main post.** P160: four motion reels
   had a median around 11K, against about 29K for reels with a real photo on
   top. Those were motion tops, not TED-Ed storytelling, so it's not proof
   this fails. It is the reason this runs as a trial against B (P160, P174)
   and not on the profile until it wins.
2. **The face is the hook on a reel, but not on TED-Ed.** TED-Ed is 4 to 5
   minutes of narration on YouTube, watched by people who chose to watch.
   Ours is a stranger deciding in 1 second. Our best November frame one was
   Luke alone with the title (fourth hour, skip 27%). So **the first 15
   seconds never cut away** (P159). Our drop-off is at 0:05 to 0:08 and a
   step at about 0:45. Animation can help the 0:45 step. It can't fix the
   front, so it doesn't try.
3. **20 seconds of one picture is a slideshow.** TED-Ed changes the shot
   every 2 to 4 seconds inside a sequence, and the shots join through a
   morph or a match cut, not a hard cut. A 17-second cutaway here is 5 or 6
   shots, never one slow scene.
4. **Every cutaway has to show what the words can't.** Animate the
   mechanism or the number: bunching you can see, a clock that changes a
   runner's legs. The asks and the opinion stay on Luke's face, because
   "save this", "send this" and "3:47 means go" come from a person, not a
   drawing. Decoration is cut.
5. **The keep comes first.** The gated script puts the clock card on screen
   from the save line to the end (P224, G34). Every cutaway hands back to the
   card or ends on it, and the 40K stage is the card itself, animated in the
   band with Luke underneath.
6. **This departs from the 16:9 rule.** Cutaways are full screen, 1080 x
   1920, because a 16:9 animation in the top band is a third of a phone
   screen and TED-Ed detail disappears at that size. Cards and covers stay
   16:9 in the band as before. The scene code takes a size parameter, so the
   same scenes can be rendered at 16:9 later for the YouTube long-form (the
   2027 framework video). That's the upside: whatever we build here gets
   reused there.

## The look: TED-Ed, in our colours

- **Paper:** warm cream (#f3ead8) with a faint grain, the same as the tank
  demo. It's "explainer mode" and reads as different from Luke's video,
  which is the point.
- **Ink:** near-black (#2a2420) outlines, flat fills, no gradients, no 3D.
- **Palette:** ERN blue #1f7ae0 for the runner we follow, gold #f5b52e for
  the clock and anything to keep, and red #d9473f **only for 3:59 and the
  one number per shot that matters.** Other runners are muted (#9fb0c6,
  #c9bfae).
- **Characters:** the canvas runner from the tank demo and the enjoy engine,
  with simple faces (an eye dot, an eyebrow that moves). Crowds are the same
  runner in 6 skin tones and 6 tops. No real people, no race branding, no
  logos.
- **Type:** Caveat hand lettering for labels and the spoken key line;
  Barlow Condensed 800 for clocks and numbers. 3 to 5 words on screen at
  once, ever.
- **Motion:** a slow camera push on every shot; things draw on rather than
  pop in; morphs between shots (bars become runners, a road becomes a
  calendar). Easing on everything, nothing linear.
- **Safe zone:** nothing that matters in the bottom 30% of the screen
  (Luke's subtitles and the Instagram buttons sit there) or the top 10%.
- **Sound (optional, the editor's choice):** a pencil scratch on draw-ons, a
  soft whoosh on morphs, one tick per clock second in stage 2. No music
  under Luke's voice.

### Cutaway 1, THE ROUND NUMBER (about 16 s)

1. A big race clock ticks from 2:58:50 to 2:59:59. A trickle of little
   runners crosses a finish line, and each one drops into a column labelled
   **2:59**. Lettering: "the minute under 3 hours".
2. The clock flips to 3:00:00 and the column beside it, **3:00**, fills
   more slowly. The 2:59 column ends about half again as tall. Lettering:
   **"~50% MORE"** (gold). This is the one bar pair drawn to scale (51.4%).
3. The camera pulls back. The two columns are part of a whole spread of
   finish times, with spikes just under **3:30** and **4:00** too. Shape
   only: no axis numbers beyond the round times, no invented heights.
4. A pacer with a balloon jogs in and the balloon is crossed out: "NOT THE
   PACERS".
5. A generic "qualifying time" ticket stamps in and is crossed out: "NOT
   QUALIFYING". No real marathon's name or logo.
6. The little runners sprint into the 2:59 column themselves; one turns to
   camera. Lettering: "they did it themselves". The paper folds to the
   clock card as we cut back to Luke for "save this".

### Cutaway 2, THE LAST 2.2K (about 15 s)

1. A road from a **40K** sign to the finish, with **2.2K** in hand
   lettering along it. Our blue runner, at about 4-hour pace, shoulders
   down.
2. The finish comes into sight, about 400 m out, and a race clock rises over the gantry: **3:57:40**. The runner's
   eyebrow lifts, and the camera pushes in on the clock.
3. Speed lines. The runner's stride opens and they pass two muted runners.
   The clock ticks up as they close.
4. They cross at **3:59:4x** (the 3:59 in red). Lettering: "nearly 4 hours
   in".
5. The repeat line as a split frame. Left: the runner's legs as two
   sandbags, the word **EMPTY**. Right: the clock, the word **GO** in gold.
   The words are Luke's spoken line, so the picture doesn't claim a feeling
   the study measured (G22: the study measured times, not feelings). Cut
   back to Luke for "send this".

### Cutaway 3, YOUR NEXT RUN (about 18 s)

1. A park loop with no brand and no logo. A runner's watch shows a target
   of **25:00**, and a hand writes "round number" next to it.
2. The same runner on a road; the watch now reads **2:00:00** as the long
   run goal (the script's two examples).
3. A marker on the path: **2K TO GO**. A watch glance: at 25:00 pace that's
   **15:00** at 3K; the watch reads 14:52. Lettering: "in reach".
4. The runner lifts and crosses at 24:5x. A small tick.
5. A row of weekly calendar squares fills with ticks, one per week.
   Lettering: "every week".
6. The road of weekly runs stretches into the distance and ends at a
   **40K** sign with the gold clock card behind it. This match cut hands
   back to Luke and the card for "follow".

Maths for shot 3: 25:00 for 5K is 5:00 a K, so 3K takes 15:00 and 2K is
left. 14:52 is under it, so the round number is in reach. That's the same
logic as the card, carried down to a parkrun.

## Production: how it gets built and what Luke gets

- **Built in code**, like the tank demo: canvas scenes rendered with
  Playwright and joined with ffmpeg, 30 fps, 1080 x 1920. The scenes read a
  size parameter, so a 16:9 render for YouTube is a flag, not a rebuild.
- **The kit this reel starts** (and later reels reuse): runner and crowd,
  race clock, K sign and gantry, the bunching chart, the watch, crossed-out
  stamps, calendar ticks, paper grain, draw-on lettering. The tank demo
  adds the fuel tank. Each later reel should only need its own 2 or 3 new
  props.
- **Timing to Luke's voice:** I render each cutaway at the script estimate
  with 1.5 s of hold at both ends, plus a shot list with the word that
  starts each shot. His editor trims the holds to the recording. If Luke
  sends the recorded audio first, I time every shot to his words instead,
  which is better.
- **What he gets:** 3 cutaway MP4s (9:16), the animated clock card for
  stage 3 (16:9, for the band), the B version's stills (already done:
  `motion/three-59/`), and one contact sheet of every shot to check before
  editing.
- **Time:** the three cutaways plus the animated card are a few hours of
  work. The first storyboard frames (one still per shot) come before any
  animation, so Luke can kill a shot before it's built.

## The test

- **A** (cutaways) and **B** (band only) as IG trial reels, same audio,
  same caption, posted the same day an hour apart, A first. ERN TikTok gets
  B, as gated.
- **Control:** the first 15 s are identical, so skip rate should match. If
  it doesn't, the difference is the audience, not the format, and the
  test is void.
- **Read at 24 h:** average watch time, retention at 0:30 and 0:45 (the
  step), saves and shares.
- **Keep A** if its average watch time is at least 3 s longer and its
  retention at 0:45 is higher, with saves no lower than B. **Drop A** if
  it's level or worse. **One more round** if it wins on watch but loses on
  saves, with the card on screen during more of the cutaways.
- Whatever happens, a win here allows only more trials (P160). Two wins in
  a row before it goes on the profile.

## LLM Council (P150)

- **Contrarian:** four of four motion reels sat under the photo median.
  This could cost a trial slot and a day's build for nothing.
- **Assumption-ripper:** "TED-Ed works, so it'll work for us" carries a
  YouTube format over to reels. The thing to copy is the explanation by
  picture, not the length or the narration. And Luke's "20 seconds" is a
  feel, not a rule: the shots inside it are what matter.

  in Ireland or the UK has.
- **Outsider:** why does the bunching need a drawing? Because "50% more"
  heard once is a number, and a column filling up beside a smaller one is
  something you see and remember.
