## Script

"Your 'healthy' snack before a match or run, the protein bar off the shop shelf, gets a 3 out of 10. It can sit heavy once you start, so I'm rating 5 snacks, up to the one that gets a 10."

**[PASTRY: 2/10]**

"Turn the pack over, because the label tells you. You want the carbs bigger than everything else on it put together. The sports nutrition guidelines say heavy, fatty food can upset your stomach close to a game. On a sausage roll, the fat is nearly as big as the carbs, so pastry gets a 2. Save this, and screenshot this card for the next time you're at the shelf, because all 5 scores are on it."

**[PROTEIN BAR: 3/10]**

"This protein bar has just 11 grams of carbs. Against that, there's 16 grams of protein and 8 of fibre. It's a great snack after you play, but a poor one an hour before. Protein after the match, but carbs before it, so it gets a 3."

**[JELLY SWEETS: 7/10]**

"Jelly sweets are nearly all carbs, with no fat or fibre. 6 of them come to about 30 grams. They won't carry a whole game alone, so they lose points. But they're perfect after the warm-up and again at half-time, so keep a bag for those 2 moments."

**[TOAST AND JAM: 9/10]**

"2 slices of toast with jam give you about 40 grams of carbs. White bread keeps the fibre low, and jam adds quick carbs without the fat. Eat it an hour or so before you start, so it's a 9."

"So what beats toast and jam?"

**[BANANA: 10/10]**

"A banana, because it's about 25 grams of carbs with hardly any fat or protein. Most shops and petrol stations have them. Add it to the toast and you're at about 65 grams. It won't feel like an athlete's snack, but it tops up the meal you had earlier, so eat it on the way."

"Your team's group chat argues about this every week, so send this to them and settle it."

## Caption
I went round the shop and rated 5 snacks for the hour or two before a match or a run, off the label. The rule is the label test: carbs should be the biggest number, with fat, protein and fibre small. The 2016 sports nutrition position (the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, Dietitians of Canada and the American College of Sports Medicine) says choices high in fat, protein or fibre may need to be avoided before exercise to lower the risk of stomach trouble, and recommends 1 to 4 g of carbs per kilo, 1 to 4 hours before anything longer than an hour.

The scores: pastry 2, protein bar 3 (11 g carbs, 16 g protein, 8 g fibre, 7 g fat on this one), jelly sweets 7 (about 5 g each, for after the warm-up and half-time), toast and jam 9 (about 40 g for 2 slices), banana 10 (about 25 g). Your top-up an hour or so before, at about 1 g of carbs per kilo: 60 kg, a banana and 2 slices of toast with jam (about 65 g); 75 kg, the same plus 2 jelly babies (about 75 g); 90 kg, 2 bananas and 2 slices (about 90 g). Then 6 jelly sweets after the warm-up and again at half-time if it's a match. The snack tops up the meal; the carb-rich meal 3 to 4 hours before still does most of the work (UEFA's expert group, 2021).
