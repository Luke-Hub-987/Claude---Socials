## The Zero to One secrets (unexpected value, usable today)

1. **You get faster by running less, at the same pace.** Most runners
   cut the miles and the speed together, or stop running, and arrive
   flat.
2. **Eat the same when you run less.** The instinct is to cut food as
   the miles drop; these are the weeks the body repairs months of
   training.

## Script (about 330 words, about 1:45; clarity and numbers first, P108)

**[ON SCREEN, held ~10s]**
"How to taper for a marathon"

**[HOOK]**
"If you are running a marathon in the next few weeks, you can still get faster in the next three weeks, and it all comes down to what you cut." ⚑1

**[PAIN, DREAM]**
"Cut the wrong thing and you stand on the start line with heavy, flat legs after months of training. Cut the right thing and you start on legs that feel lighter than they have in months."

**[NAMED CONCEPT, THE EXAMPLE, SAVE PROMPT: the loop stays open]**
"This is the taper. Say your biggest week is sixty K over four runs. Here is exactly what to cut, week by week, so save this."

**[1. 3 WEEKS OUT: 48K, DRESS REHEARSAL]**
"Three weeks out, run about forty-eight K. Twenty-two of that is your last long run, as a dress rehearsal: five K easy, fifteen K at marathon pace and two K easy, in your race kit, after your race breakfast, with gels at your race day times. The rest is three easy runs of eight or nine K." ⚑2

**[2. 2 WEEKS OUT: 40K, SAME PACE]**
"Two weeks out, about forty K. A sixteen K long run, two easy eights, and a thirty-five minute run with three one K stretches at marathon pace. Your runs get shorter, and they never get slower." ⚑3

**[3. RACE WEEK: 30K, STAY SHARP]**
"Race week, about thirty K, still four runs. Two easy runs of ten K, a twenty-five minute run with three one K stretches at marathon pace, and two days out, twenty minutes easy with four fifteen-second pick-ups. That is the rule: cut the miles, keep the pace and keep the same number of runs. If your biggest week is bigger or smaller, cut by the same amounts: a fifth, then a third, then half." ⚑4 ⚑1

**[REHOOK]**
"And the last one is the thing runners cut that they should not."

**[4. ALL 3 WEEKS: KEEP EATING]**
"The fourth is food. When the miles drop, it feels natural to eat less, but your body is repairing months of training. Keep a carb like rice, pasta or potatoes and a protein like chicken, eggs or yoghurt in every meal, and save any diet for after the race." ⚑5

**[CTA, one sentence]**
"Save this for the next three weeks, and the free race week guide and first go at the bar are in the bio."

## The worked example (for the caption and the file)

| Week | Distance | Runs (four a week) |
|---|---|---|
| Biggest week | 60K | Your plan |
| 3 weeks out | ~48K (a fifth off) | 22K rehearsal (5 easy, 15 at marathon pace, 2 easy) + 3 easy runs of 8 to 9K |
| 2 weeks out | ~40K (a third off) | 16K long run + 2 x 8K easy + 35 min with 3 x 1K at marathon pace |
| Race week | ~30K (half), not counting the race | 2 x 10K easy + 25 min with 3 x 1K at marathon pace + 20 min easy with 4 x 15 s pick-ups (2 days out) |

Race week matches the 7 days video (half the distance, the 25 minute
run with three 1K stretches). The 15K at marathon pace is Luke's
session; it is a big run three weeks out, and it is the one the
rehearsal exists for.

## Luke's science, checked

**Kept:** keep the intensity, cut the volume, keep the frequency (the
core of the taper research); the 5 easy / 15 at marathon pace / 2 easy
long run; 35 minutes with 3 x 1K at marathon pace two weeks out; short
marathon pace pick-ups in race week; do not cut calories; stopping
completely can leave legs feeling flat.

**Changed or cut:**

1. **"Physiological window closed", "cellular fatigue", "reveal aerobic
   capacity": cut as wording.** Said plainly: run less at the same
   pace and you can still get faster.
2. **"Shuts down your central nervous system", "motor pathways",
   "tendon spring tension": cut.** Central nervous system claims and
   the tendon spring idea were cut from earlier videos for the same
   reason. Said as "your legs can feel heavy and flat".
3. **Percentages became fractions, and the steps were matched to the
   7 days video.** That video told people to run "about half your
   usual weekly distance" in race week. Luke's 60 to 70% for race week
   and 40 to 50% two weeks out would contradict it, so: about a fifth,
   about a third, about half. That lands in the 40 to 60% total cut
   the research supports.
4. **Cardiac drift under 5%: cut.** A statistic (P107), needs a chest
   strap, and the goal pace video (25.3K) already did the pace test.
   The long run here is a dress rehearsal instead, which is new.
5. **The "taper meltdown", phantom aches, scratchy throat and cortisol:
   moved to video 2**, and the throat/cortisol link gets vetted there.
6. **The 7-5-3-2 race week protocol: left out.** The 7 days video
   already posted it.
7. **Static stretching and foam rolling ("coiled spring"): cut.** The
   spring claim was cut before; "nothing new in the taper" covers it.
8. **Electrolytes and "plasma up by 8%": cut** (a statistic, cut once
   before). **Sleep:** saved for video 2.
9. **"Immune reboot": cut.** Said as "your body is using these weeks to
   repair months of training".
