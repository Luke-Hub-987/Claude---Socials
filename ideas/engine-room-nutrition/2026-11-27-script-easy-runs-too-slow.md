## Script

"You speed up on your easy run when a runner comes the other way, so you don't look too slow. You come home tired, but 3 paces keep your legs fresh for the long run. Here's the speed limit for your goal time."

**[FIRST 10 MINUTES]**

"Your easy run is 30 minutes, split into thirds. For a sub-4, the first third goes between 7:00 and 7:05 a K. That's slow enough to talk in full sentences, and it should feel almost too easy. Someone will pass you, but they're not running your week, so let them go. Grab a screenshot of the card now and save this, because your goal's paces are on it."

**[NEXT 10 MINUTES]**

"Once your legs are warm, the middle third goes between 6:30 and 6:35, and you can still talk. If you can't finish a sentence, you're too quick, so back off. When the road climbs, run by your breathing and let the watch go slower. It looks slow from the footpath, but it's the pace that builds you, so hold it until 20 minutes."

"So where does easy end?"

**[THE SPEED LIMIT]**

"Never run quicker than 6:05 a K, even on the days you feel great. That limit keeps your easy days easy, so your long run gets your best legs. If your watch says 5:50, ease off until it reads 6:05 or slower. Stay there until your 30 minutes are up, however good you feel. It costs you the feeling of looking quick on a Tuesday, but that's a feeling, not fitness. The card has the limit for every goal from 3:00 to 4:30."

"Send this to the friend who gets passed on every easy run and doesn't care, because they've got it right."

## Caption
Easy runs go wrong the same way: a runner comes the other way, you feel slow, you speed up, and a run meant to be easy comes home as a tempo. An easy run has a speed limit, and the voice saying "I look too slow" is what breaks it.

Your 30-minute easy run in 3 paces a K (first 10 minutes, next 10, then never quicker than the last number). 3:00 marathon: 5:20-5:25, 4:55-5:00, never quicker than 4:40. 3:30: 6:10-6:15, 5:45-5:50, never quicker than 5:20. 3:45: 6:35-6:40, 6:05-6:10, never quicker than 5:45. 4:00: 7:00-7:05, 6:30-6:35, never quicker than 6:05 (in miles, about 11:20, 10:30, never quicker than 9:47). 4:30: 7:50-7:55, 7:15-7:20, never quicker than 6:50. These come from Jack Daniels' easy-pace range for your marathon time, worked out, not a study of these runners. Hills, heat and wind push the pace slower; go by the talk test when they do: full sentences means easy.
