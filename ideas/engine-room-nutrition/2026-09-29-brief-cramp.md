# Where marathon cramp actually comes from

## Why this one

- **It carries both halves of P97:** a secret as a question in the
  OST (reach), and a four-stage fix inside (saves).
- **Same shape as the outlier**, word for word: "Where the marathon
  wall actually starts" / "Where marathon cramp actually comes from".
- **It is contested**, which is the one lever we have not pulled yet.
  Comments have been 0 or 1 on every video (P93).
- Cramp is the next biggest fear after the wall, the runner has felt
  it or seen it, and it decides whether they finish the last 10K.

## The secret (P94)

**Most runners think cramp is salt running out. The best study of it
found the runners who cramped were no saltier and no more dehydrated
than the ones who did not. What they had done was run faster than they
planned.** ⚑

## OST

> **Where marathon cramp actually comes from**

Markers only: **0-10K / 10-30K / 30K-FINISH / WHEN IT HITS**

## Hook (draft)

"If you are running a marathon in the next few weeks, the cramp that
stops you at thirty K has less to do with salt than you have been told,
and you set it up in the first ten K."

## Beats

1. **0-10K.** The study: 210 Ironman triathletes, crampers were no more
   dehydrated and had the same sodium in their blood. They had raced
   faster than they planned. ⚑ The first ten K is where that happens.
2. **10-30K.** Cramp hits the one muscle doing the most work, usually a
   calf or hamstring, which is odd if the cause were salt in your whole
   body. The protection is the long runs at race pace (thread to the
   goal pace video).
3. **30K-FINISH.** The other big predictor was having cramped before.
   If you have, pace is the lever you control.
4. **WHEN IT HITS.** Stop and gently stretch that muscle. In a lab,
   pickle juice stopped cramps faster than water before any of it could
   have reached the blood ⚑, which points at a reflex rather than salt
   levels.

**Save prompt at beat one. No thread line at the end, one-sentence CTA
(P97).** Keep electrolytes in the plan: the claim is that salt is not
the main cause, never that it is useless.

## ⚑ Science to confirm before scripting

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Crampers not more dehydrated, no lower serum sodium; faster running and a history of cramp predicted it | Schwellnus et al., 2011, Br J Sports Med, 210 Ironman triathletes |
| Earlier marathon data found no blood electrolyte difference between crampers and non-crampers | Maughan, 1986 |
| Pickle juice relieved electrically induced cramps faster than water, with no change in blood electrolytes | Miller et al., 2010, Med Sci Sports Exerc. Small study, lab cramps in dehydrated men, not a marathon |
