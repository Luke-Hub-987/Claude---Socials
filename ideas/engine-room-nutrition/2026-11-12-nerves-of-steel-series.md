# Race day execution / match day execution: nerves of steel, with fuel underneath

## The idea in one line
Take the self-improvement titles that win (nerves of steel, cold, calmest in the
room) and point them at the two moments our audience gets nervous: the start
pen and the last 20 minutes of a match. Every one lands on the same truth:
**nerves of steel run on sugar.** The head techniques work until the tank's
empty, so the fuel is what holds the rest up. That's the carb bar's reason to
exist, without a single line of selling.

## The carbs bridge, said true
- When the tank runs low, it isn't only the legs: the brain runs on glucose, so
  mood, decisions and skill go with it. That's the Tunnel in Friday's wall post
  (vision narrows, everything irritates you).
- **To vet before any script uses it** (names I'll confirm, not quotes):
  carbohydrate during a match and skill late in the game (Russell & Kingsley's
  review of football, 2014); carbohydrate the brain senses before it's digested
  (the mouth-rinse studies, Carter and others, 2004); low blood sugar and
  decision-making. Krustrup 2006 (fibres empty after a match) is already vetted.

## The mental techniques (to vet; don't copy one creator's set)
Cian's three were anxiety reappraisal, self-distancing and stress inoculation.
Two of them are well-known research (saying "I'm excited" instead of "calm down",
Brooks 2014; talking to yourself by name, Kross and others 2014), but **all three
as one set would be his video in running kit.** Ours mixes at most two of those
with our own: the lamppost rule (FACTS), the clock at the 10K signs (C24), and the
gel at 20 minutes (C22).
