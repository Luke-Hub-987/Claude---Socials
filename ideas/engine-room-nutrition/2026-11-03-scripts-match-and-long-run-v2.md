**Markers:** BEFORE KICK-OFF / HALF-TIME / THE LAST 15
**Top photo (frame one):** one player, hands on knees or on shorts, late

**The gap:** "while you're standing still" (when?), held by "2 places" in

"If you play a match every week, the legs you lose in the last 15 minutes get decided while you're standing still."

"Get it wrong and at 80 minutes the ball's 5 yards away, you're telling your legs to go, and nothing happens."

"Get it right and you're the one still getting to that ball in the 85th minute while everyone else is walking."

"Marathon runners call this the wall. In a match it hits in the last 15 minutes, and it gets set up in 2 places before you feel it, so save this."

**[BEFORE KICK-OFF]**

"The first place is your breakfast table. 3 hours before kick-off, have a plain meal you've eaten before training, like porridge, or a bagel with a banana. Then 30 minutes before, something small and solid with a bit of salt in it, instead of a can of something sweet in the car park."

"If you'd like a match-day plan built around your kick-off time, comment MATCH and we'll make one."

**[HALF-TIME]**

"The second place is where you're standing still, at half-time. You're stood there with a bottle of water and a slice of orange, feeling grand, and that's the trap. What you take in those few minutes is what's in your legs at 75. So take 20 to 25 grams of carbs with your water, what's in one gel, even if you feel fresh."

"And the last 15 minutes is where you find out if it worked."

**[THE LAST 15]**

"When the legs start to go, take shorter, quicker steps getting back into position. Big strides on tired legs burn what's left, and short ones save it for the one sprint that matters."

"Send this to the team WhatsApp."

## Script 2: the long run (T4 v2)

**OST:** **HOW HARD YOUR LONG RUN / SHOULD FEEL** (21 and 11 characters)
**Markers:** THE FIRST 20 MINUTES / THE MIDDLE / THE LAST 20 MINUTES
**Top photo (frame one):** one runner on their own, late in a long run,
slowing or walking on a quiet road, daylight, no bib.
**The gap:** "one sentence... tells you before it happens" (which sentence,
and how does it tell me?), half shut at stage 1 (the sentence), held by
"the number you're listening for comes near the end", fully shut in the
last stage (5 words).

"If you're training for a marathon, there's one sentence you can say out loud on your long run that tells you the last 20 minutes are going to fall apart, before they do."

"Get it wrong and you're walking the last 2K home with your watch still running and nothing left in your legs."

"Get it right and you run the last K past your own front door, feeling like you could go again."

"We call it the 10-word test, and the number you're listening for comes near the end, so save this."

**[THE FIRST 20 MINUTES]**

"Say this out loud, even if you feel daft: I am running a marathon today and I feel good. That's 10 words. For the first 20 minutes, all 10 should come out in one breath, in your normal voice. If they don't, slow down until they do, even if your watch says you're crawling."

"If you want a plan built for your next race, email us your race and goal time through the link in the bio."

**[THE MIDDLE]**

"If the run's longer than 90 minutes, take your first gel at 20 minutes, the same as race day, and say the sentence again every time you take one. If the 10 words come out a bit breathy now, that's fine. That's the run doing its job, so hold the pace and leave the watch alone."

"And the last 20 minutes is where you hear the number that matters."

**[THE LAST 20 MINUTES]**

"If you're down to 5 words before the last 20 minutes have even started, that's the fall-apart coming early. Slow down until the 10 come back, and you'll run that last K past your front door instead of walking it."

"Send this to the mate who always goes off too fast on the long run."
