## The facts (vetted 16 Nov; the gate chat adds them to FACTS)
| Claim | Source / status |
|---|---|
| High-intensity running was **35 to 45% lower in the last 15 minutes than the first 15**, whatever the standard or position. Top-class (international) and moderate professionals | Mohr, Krustrup and Bangsbo, *J Sports Sci* 2003;21:519-28, doi 10.1080/0264041031000071182. **Not Premier League: say "top professionals"** |
| After a match, **about 47% of leg muscle fibres were empty or almost empty of glycogen**; sprints were slower after the game | Krustrup and others, *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 2006 (31 Danish fourth-division players). Already vetted in the 10 Nov brief. Audio: "about half the fibres in your legs", never "you run out of fuel" (the sprint drop didn't track total glycogen). Fourth division is close to a good Sunday league side, which helps |
| Players covered **about 33% more high-intensity running** after a high-carb diet than a low-carb one | Balsom and others, *Int J Sports Med* 1999;20:48-52. 6 players, 4-a-side, 90 minutes: small; say "in one study" |
| A 12% carb drink before each half and at half-time gave **faster dribbling in the last 30 minutes** than water or a placebo, in players who'd eaten | Harper, Stevenson, Rollo and Russell, *J Sci Med Sport* 2017. 15 players. **Gate chat: the volumes, before any grams go in the audio** |
| Premier League players' **pre-match meal held under 1.5 g of carbs per kg**, below the guidelines; they ate more carbs on match day overall (6.4 g/kg) | Anderson, Morton and others, *IJSNEM* 2017 (6 players, food diaries). Use: "even Premier League players under-eat the pre-match meal" |
| Wenger's first Arsenal away trip (Blackburn, 1996): he'd taken the chocolate away, the players chanted "we want our Mars bars", and at half-time the dressing room went silent; physio Gary Lewin told him "they're hungry" | Wenger's own telling (ESPN, Arsenal.com, IBTimes). The scores differ between accounts, so no score in the audio. **The story is the half-time gap, never "Wenger was wrong"**: his diet changes are why he's loved |
| GAA players ate too little carb, short **by up to 61% on the day before matches**; a high-carb 48 hours improved repeated sprints and jumps in a simulated Gaelic football match | Ó Catháin and others, *Sports* 2020; O'Brien and others, *Nutrients* 2021. For the GAA version, not this one |
| More goals go in after the 75th minute than in any other 15 minutes | Sources disagree (15% to 26% depending on season and stoppage time). **Not used** until one clean source |

## The method (one clock; the gate chat sets the grams)
1. **The night before:** a proper carb dinner. One short stage: they half-know it (row 26). The proof: about 33% more high-intensity running in one study.
2. **The pre-match meal, 3 to 4 hours before kick-off:** the new bit. Even Premier League players under-eat it. Grams per kilo from FACTS.
3. **Half-time:** the orange slice is the picture (every player's had one; a couple of slices is a few grams). Carbs at half-time even when you feel fine: the 12% drink study, the dribbling in the last 30. Wenger's "they're hungry" is the turn inside this stage.
4. **The last 15:** what it buys, as a scene (the run in behind at 85 minutes).

**Honest cost (P206):** carbs don't make you fitter, and a big half-time drink
can slosh: try it at training first.
