## Three checks, at all times (v4)

1. **Watch pace, every K:** the number to hold. Set a few seconds
   quicker than real four-hour pace, because a GPS watch reads long in
   a race (tangents, first 10 minutes). Run these on the watch and the
   real pace lands on plan.
2. **The time on the watch at each 10K sign:** the truth, since the
   signs are real distance. Within about 30 seconds means on plan.
3. **The talk test (ten words), every stage:** effort, the same scale
   as the how hard video.

| Sign | Watch pace to here | Time at the sign (watch 1% long, typical) | If the watch is spot on | Talk test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10K | 5:42 | about 57:30 (57:35) | 57:00 | 10 words, one breath, normal voice |
| 20K | 5:35 | about 1:54 (1:53:58) | 1:52:50 | 10 words, a bit breathy |
| 30K | 5:35 | about 2:50 (2:50:22) | 2:48:40 | about 5 words, then a breath |
| 40K | 5:35 | about 3:47 (3:46:46) | 3:44:30 | 2 or 3 words |
| Finish | 5:25 for the last 2K | **about 3:59 (3:58:47)** | 3:56:23 | 1 or 2 words, controlled |

Even with the watch reading 1.5% long, the finish is 3:59:59: inside
four. Within Luke's ranges: 5:42 (his 5:42-5:45), 5:35 (his 5:36-5:40,
one second quicker for the GPS buffer), 5:25 (his 5:25-5:30).

## The Zero to One secret

Two opposite mistakes stop sub-4: running it all at the easy zone 2
effort everyone now trains at, or going out with the four-hour pacer to
bank time. The race starts slower than four-hour pace and is run on
three numbers at once: watch pace, sign time and talk test.

## Caption

How to pace a sub-4 marathon at every 10K. Three checks, all race: the pace on your watch every K, the time on your watch at each 10K sign, and the talk test ("I am running a marathon today and I feel good", ten words). The watch paces are a few seconds quicker than four-hour pace on purpose, because GPS adds distance in a race. Start your watch on the start mat. Within about 30 seconds of the sign times means you are on plan, and if the watch pace and the sign time disagree, trust the sign.

0 to 10K: 5:42 per K on your watch, about 57:30 at the 10K sign. All ten words in one breath, normal voice. Almost too easy, and behind four-hour pace on purpose.

10 to 20K: 5:35 per K, about 1:54 at 20K. Ten words, a bit breathy. Gel every 20 minutes with water.

20 to 30K: 5:35 per K, about 2:50 at 30K. About five words, then a breath. Shorter, quicker steps. Trained with caffeine gels? Take one at 22K.

30 to 40K: 5:35 per K, about 3:47 at 40K. Two or three words between breaths. It feels like a 9, and that is the plan. Signpost to signpost.

Last 2K: 5:25 per K, elbows back, finish around 3:58.

Easy zone 2 the whole way gets you a 4:15. Going out with the 4-hour pacer gets you passed by them.

## Luke's science (14 Oct), checked

**Update, 14 Oct (Luke): heart rate taken out of the audio, markers and
caption.** It depends on each runner's max, so it confused more than it
helped. Pace, the time on the watch and the ten-word talk test are the
measures; "easy zone two" stays only as the mistake in the pain line.

**Kept:** sub-4 is not raced at an easy zone 2 effort; around 80-85% of
max (about 150-160 bpm for a max near 190) is the race; 5:42-5:45 for
the first 10K (we use 5:45); 5:38 locked through 40K; heart rate
climbing at the same pace (about 150, 152-156, 156-160, 160-165); the
effort rising to an 8 or 9 at the same pace; shorter, quicker steps
from 20K; a caffeine gel around 22K for anyone who has trained with
them (as the wall video put it); signpost to signpost; no all-out 2K
sprint; 5:25-5:30 for the last 2K, elbows driving back.

**Changed, and why:**
1. **"Zone 3" is said as beats and percentages, never as a zone
   number.** On Garmin's default zones (percent of max), 80-85% is
   zone 4, not zone 3; other watches set zones differently. Saying
   "zone 3" would send half the audience to the wrong number. "Zone
   two" stays in the pain line only as "your watch's easy zone two",
   which is too slow for sub-4 on any watch.
2. **Gels every 20 minutes, not 25-30:** every earlier video says 20.
3. **Finish around 3:58, not 3:57:** checked to the second, the plan
   runs 3:58:30 (57:30, 1:53:50, 2:50:10, 3:46:30, then 5:28 for the
   last 2.2K). The fastest end of every range gets 3:57:14.
4. **Cut:** "burns glycogen you can never get back", tendon bounce,
   caffeine "blocking fatigue receptors", "brain-governor", cadence
   175+ and +3-5 steps a minute, 200-metre targets, 3:3 breathing (the
   ten-word talk test does that job, as in the how hard video).
