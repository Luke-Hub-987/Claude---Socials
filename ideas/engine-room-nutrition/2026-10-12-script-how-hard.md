# How hard a marathon should feel at every 10K

## OST (triple-checked, 13 Oct)

> **How hard a marathon should feel**
> **at every 10K**

**No heart rate or zones on screen.** Three reasons from our data:
1. **Jargon in the title cost us before** ("taper": 43.1% skip, P116).
   Every title that worked is plain words a non-runner understands.
2. **Zone numbers mean different things on different watches.** Luke's
   notes put the first 10K in "zone 2" (68-75% of max); a watch on
   standard zones calls the same heart rate zone 3. A zone on screen
   would be wrong for half the audience.
3. **Effort and the talk test work for everyone,** with or without a
   watch (the biggest audience, Briar's TAM). Heart rate goes in the
   audio as one simple number and in the caption.

**Why this title:** the wall's shape ("every 10K", the race clock behind
147K) on the question pace can't answer; "Marathon" in line one; nine
plain words; the answer (a score per 10K) withheld. Considered: "What a
marathon should feel like at every 10K" ("how hard" is sharper and
promises a number); "What heart rate to run a marathon at" (jargon,
zones disagree); "How your marathon should feel..." ("a marathon"
matches every winner).

**Markers, the stage, words per breath and heart rate (P111; Luke 13 Oct: a heart rate for every stage):**
0-10K: 10 WORDS, UNDER 80% HR / 10-20K: 10 WORDS, AROUND 80% HR /
20-30K: 5 WORDS, 80-85% HR / 30K TO THE LINE: 2-3 WORDS, 85-92% HR

## The Zero to One secret (Luke's, 13 Oct)

The same pace feels four completely different ways across a marathon.
Everyone tries to make 35K feel like 5K, and that is how they blow up:
by chasing an easy feeling late, or by running early at a pace that
already feels like work. A nine out of ten at 35K is the plan.

## Script (about 330 words, about 1:45)

**[ON SCREEN, held ~10s]**
"How hard a marathon should feel at every 10K"

**[HOOK]**
"If you are running a marathon in the next few weeks, the pace that feels easy at five K should feel like a nine out of ten by thirty-five, and that is your race going to plan." ⚑1

**[PAIN, DREAM]**
"Run the first ten K at a pace that already feels like work, and you are walking by thirty-two K. Get the effort right, and you are still on your pace at forty."

**[NAMED CONCEPT, SAVE PROMPT]**
"Runners call it the talk test, and you use it with your heart rate. Say this sentence: I am running a marathon today and I feel good. Here is how many of those ten words you should get out, and your heart rate, at every 10K, so save this."

**[1. 0-10K: 10 WORDS, UNDER 80% HR]**
"The first ten K should feel easy. You can say all ten words in one breath, in your normal voice, and your heart rate stays under eighty percent of your max, under one fifty-two if your max is one ninety. If either one is off, slow down." ⚑2

**[2. 10-20K: 10 WORDS, AROUND 80% HR]**
"Ten to twenty, all ten words still come out in one breath, just a bit breathy, at around eighty percent, about one fifty-two. On hills or into wind, keep the effort and let the pace slip, and take a gel every twenty minutes." ⚑3

**[3. 20-30K: 5 WORDS, 80-85% HR]**
"Twenty to thirty, you get about five words, I am running a marathon, then a breath, and your heart rate creeps up to eighty to eighty-five percent, up to one sixty-two. That is normal at the same pace, so hold it and shorten your step." ⚑4

**[REHOOK]**
"And the last one is where a nine out of ten is the plan."

**[4. 30K TO THE LINE: 2-3 WORDS, 85-92% HR]**
"From thirty, the same pace feels like an eight, then a nine, with two or three words between breaths and your heart rate at eighty-five to ninety-two percent, up to about one seventy-five. That is the race going right. Run lamppost to lamppost, and save your sprint for the last eight hundred metres." ⚑5

**[CTA, one sentence]**
"Save this for race day, and the free race week guide and first go at the bar are in the bio."

## The worked example (caption)

How hard a marathon should feel at every 10K: the talk test and your heart rate, together.
Test sentence: "I am running a marathon today and I feel good" (10 words). Heart rate as % of your max (example: max 190).
- 0-10K: all 10 words in one breath, normal voice. Heart rate under 80% (under 152). Either one off? Too fast.
- 10-20K: all 10 words, a bit breathy. Around 80% (about 152). Hills or wind: keep the effort, let pace slip. Gel every 20 min.
- 20-30K: about 5 words, then a breath. 80-85% (152-162) at the same pace is normal. Hold pace, shorter steps.
- 30K to the line: 2 or 3 words between breaths. 85-92% (162-175). That's the plan. Lamppost to lamppost; sprint only in the last 800m.
Still saying all 10 words at 35K? That's your signal to push.
Wrist heart rate can jump around early on; if it disagrees with your breathing, trust your breathing.
Dizzy, chest pain or confused at any point: stop and go to the medical team.

## Luke's science (13 Oct), checked: kept simple

**Kept:** the core secret (the same pace feels four different ways;
don't try to make 35K feel like 5K); the "too slow" rule (if it feels
like work early, you are too fast); RPE climbing 4, 5, 6, then 8 to 9;
heart rate drifting up at the same pace; letting pace slip on a hill
or in wind to hold effort; shorter, quicker steps from 20K; waypoint
running (as the lamppost rule in quit); hold the sprint until the last
800 metres and drive the arms.

**Changed or cut, and why:**
1. **Zone numbers: cut from screen and audio.** Luke's zone 2 (68-75%)
   is zone 3 on standard watch zones; one percentage in the audio
   ("under about eighty percent") and the caption instead.
2. **68-75% of max for the first 10K: loosened to "under about 80%".**
   68-75% is well below marathon effort for most recreational runners
   and would contradict "five to ten seconds a K slower than goal" in
   earlier videos.
3. **"Short sentences, prefer silence" at 10-20K: changed to "a full
   sentence if you have to".** The first half and second half videos
   say full sentences at halfway (consistency, Luke 10 Oct).
4. **Gels every 25-30 minutes: kept at every 20**, as every earlier
   video.
5. **Statistics cut** (P107): 2% above threshold, 3-6 bpm, over 50%
   blood flow, 3-5 steps per minute, 200 metres, 45 minutes.
6. **Mechanism jargon cut:** type I/II fibres, spinal reflex cramping,
   tendon spring tension, Golgi tendon organs, "brain governor",
   "central nervous system emergency brake" (banned since the wall).
7. **"Elite runners do not..." cut:** a generalisation about a group.
8. **3:3 and 2:2 breathing: cut for simplicity**; the talk test does
   the same job with nothing to count.
9. **Caffeine gel at 20-25K: cut.** Not needed to keep it simple, and
   stimulants sit near the medication line (P106).
10. **Mouth rinse: cut here.** Already in out of the wall and quit; no
    need to repeat it in a video about effort.
