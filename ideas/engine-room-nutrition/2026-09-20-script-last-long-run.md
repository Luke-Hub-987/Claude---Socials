**[HOOK STACK]**

*Hook and gate, "you" first:* "You have two or three long runs left

*Super hook, pain twisted:* "Every one you run as just another long run
is a rehearsal you gave away, and then the first time you test your
fuel, your kit and your stomach for real is thirty kilometres into the
race you trained sixteen weeks for."

*Super hook, dream outcome and the cost:* "Three things to rehearse on
Sunday. They take no extra effort, about forty seconds to explain, and
they decide whether race morning feels like something you have already
done."

**[ONE. THE FUEL, AND WHEN YOU PRACTISE IT]**
"Take exactly what you plan to take on the day, at the exact timings
and the exact amounts. For most people that is somewhere around
seventy five to ninety grams of carbs an hour ⚑, so a gel or a bar
every twenty minutes.

Here is the part almost nobody does. The rehearsal that counts is the
last ten kilometres of the run. Early on, your stomach has plenty of
blood going to it and sugar goes down easily. Once you are past twenty
K, your body has pulled most of that blood away ⚑, out to your skin to
cool you and into your legs to keep you moving, and your stomach
slows right down. That is the exact state it will be in at thirty five
K on race day. So if the only time you have ever practised eating is
the first hour, you have practised the easy bit.

Your gut does adapt to the load, over something like ten to fourteen
days of doing it ⚑, which is why this weekend is the one that counts.
And if part of your plan is real food rather than another gel, that
gets rehearsed too."

**[ON SCREEN ONLY, not spoken]**
"2 accountants. ⚑[N] failed batches. Still pre-launch."

**[TWO. THE MORNING]**
"Set race morning's alarm. Eat race morning's breakfast, the same
amount, and leave the same gap before you start running. You are
testing the clock here: the three hours, the coffee, the toilet, the
drive in. A breakfast that works perfectly at nine can behave
completely differently at half five."

**[THREE. THE KIT, AND WHY IT ONLY SHOWS UP LATE]**
"Everything you will wear and carry goes on. Shoes, socks, shorts,
vest, belt, the lube, the number pinned where you are going to pin it.

And the reason this has to happen on a long run is that chafing is a
distance problem. By about twenty five K, dried sweat has left a crust
of salt sitting on your skin ⚑, and with soaked fabric moving over it,
a seam you could not even feel at five K will have taken the skin off
you by thirty. Find a hot spot on Sunday and you have three weeks ⚑ to
fix the tape, the lube or the kit. Find it on race day and you have
twelve K left to get through with it."

**[CLOSE]**
"Do those three and race morning is the second time you have done it.
Leave them and race day is the first time for all three at once, in
the one place you cannot change anything."

**[THREAD]**

## ⚑ Before filming

1. **"Two or three long runs left"** and **"three weeks"** both depend
   on his race date. Keep them loose unless he pegs them to a week.
2. **75 to 90 grams an hour** ⚑. Consistent with the gels script,
   which used 60 to 90. Pick one range and use it on both.
3. **"Most of that blood pulled away"** ⚑. The effect is real and well
   documented. The size of it is where the risk sits, so say "most of
   it" out loud rather than a percentage. The 80% figure is at the
   upper end of what is measured and a hard number invites a
   correction, which is the cardiac-drift lesson.
4. **The salt crust** ⚑. Chafing getting worse with distance, sweat
   and salt is not in dispute. "Salt acts like sandpaper" is a
   mechanism rather than a measured finding, so it is delivered as
   description, and the word sandpaper has been left out for that
   reason.
5. **The 10 to 14 day gut adaptation window** ⚑, same flag as the gels
   script, and it is what makes this weekend a deadline.
6. **The failed-batch count** ⚑[N].

## What changed in v2, and why

Beats one and three were correct and neither was **news**. "Practise
your fuel" and "nothing new on race day" are the two most repeated
instructions in the sport, so a viewer who already knows them has no
reason to stay.

Both now carry a mechanism, which is what makes an old instruction new
(P81):

- **Fuel:** the rehearsal that counts is the last ten K, because the
  blood has left your stomach by then. This also converts the beat
  into a save, since it cannot be acted on from memory.
- **Kit:** chafing is a distance problem because of what sweat leaves
  behind, which is why five K tells you nothing.

The pacing discipline for the first five K was the third candidate. It
is strong, and we already have a video on it doing 4,291, so it was
moved into the thread where it sends traffic to that asset instead of
competing with it.

Jargon dropped on the way in: splanchnic vasoconstriction, zone 2,
anaerobic oxygen deficit. The mechanisms stayed, the words went.
