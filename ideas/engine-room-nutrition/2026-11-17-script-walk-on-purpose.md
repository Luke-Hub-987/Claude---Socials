## Caption
Walking in a marathon feels like failing, but it doesn't have to be. In ultras I walk on purpose from the start, and a marathon study found the same thing works over 42.2K.

The study (Hottenrott and others, Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport, 2016): 42 non-elite runners were split at random. Half ran the whole marathon, half ran and walked, with a minute's walk about every 2.5K (every 1.5 miles). The run/walk group finished in 4:14:25 on average and the run-only group in 4:07:40, a gap the study found wasn't significant, and the walkers reported less muscle pain and fatigue afterwards. So the honest cost is about 7 minutes on average, and the pay-off is legs that still work late on.

How to do it: first walk at 2.5K, while you're fresh. Walk 1 minute on your watch, at the side of the road, fast. Start running again at 50 seconds. Repeat about every 2.5K. Your gels stay on the clock (first at 20 minutes, then every 30), walk or no walk; if one falls due within a couple of minutes of a walk, take it walking, with 3 or 4 sips of water if you're passing a station.
