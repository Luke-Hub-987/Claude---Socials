This was me, and you've said it too: 'I've let myself go'. Diets never stuck, until I picked up these 10 habits. I've since dropped almost 10 kilos and run marathons and ultras, but each habit gets more niche, so stay for number 10.

Give it 66 days, not 21. In a study of 96 people, a new habit took about 66 days to stick, and missing one day didn't break it. The 21-day idea came from a plastic surgeon. Save this, then screenshot the list for your fridge.

Write down when and where you'll run, like Tuesday at 7am, the canal. In one study, 91% who wrote it down exercised, against about a third who didn't.

Copy the NHS beginner week, so run 1 minute and walk 90 seconds.

Run slow enough to say a full sentence without gasping for air, so it feels easy.

Run 3 days a week with rest days between, so your legs catch up.

Do 10 minutes of squats and slow calf raises in the kitchen, twice a week, slow on the way down. In sport, strength training cut injuries to about a third.

Eat some carbs before you run, like toast with jam at 5pm before a 7pm run. 8 minutes of jogging won't empty your legs, but a long gap since lunch leaves you flat, so dinner's the refill.

If a week felt hard, repeat it, because nobody's marking you.

Aim for a parkrun, where a volunteer tail walker finishes behind everyone, so you can't come last. You didn't lose it in a month, so you won't get it back in one.
