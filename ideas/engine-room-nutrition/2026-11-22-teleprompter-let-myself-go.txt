You've probably said 'I let myself go', but it wasn't laziness. It was work and maybe kids, for 10 busy years. Getting fit again comes down to 10 small habits, but the first one surprises nearly everyone who restarts after 30.

Give it 66 days, not 21. In a study of 96 people, a new habit took about 66 days to stick. The 21-day idea came from a plastic surgeon, not a habit study. Save this, then screenshot the list so it can go on your fridge.

Write down when and where you'll run, like Tuesday at 7am, the canal. In one study, 91% of people who wrote it down exercised, against about a third who didn't.

Copy the NHS beginner plan's first week, so run for 1 minute, then walk for 90 seconds.

Run slow enough to say a full sentence, because the restart is meant to feel easy.

Run 3 days a week with a rest day between, so your legs catch up with your lungs.

Do 10 minutes of squats and calf raises in the kitchen, twice a week. In sport, strength training cut injuries to about a third.

Eat some carbs before you run, like toast with jam at 5pm before a 7pm run, so dinner after is the refill.

If a week felt hard, repeat it, because nobody's marking you.

Go to a parkrun, where you can't come last, because a volunteer tail walker finishes behind everyone. You didn't lose it in a month, so you won't get it back in one.

The last one makes it real, so book a parkrun for the week after the plan's 9 weeks end. Put it in your calendar now, before the doubt comes back.
