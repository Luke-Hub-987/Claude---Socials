## Behind the curtain: 7 things the top 5 share

1. **A famous fear or a countdown, never a niche.** 4 of the top 9 are the
   wall (median 69K). The rest are time slices everyone lives (7 days, the
   first 10 minutes) or the thing everyone does (carb loading). Goal times
   sit in the middle (median 29K); trivia, kit and training sit at the
   bottom.
2. **The answer is somewhere you'd never look.** "Where X actually starts",
   "the hidden wall", "ACTUALLY carb-load": each title promises that what
   you believe is wrong. That's the counter-position in four words.
3. **The audience's race was 1 to 4 weeks away.** Every top-5 reel went up
   between late September and mid-October, with Dublin, Amsterdam,
   Frankfurt and New York coming. They kept growing for weeks (7 days: 31K
   at 14 hours, 151K now; the hidden wall 41.5K to 87.9K) because every
   week more viewers' races got closer. That's the biggest reason November
   is quieter (P171), and we can't change it. We can only aim at the next
   thing they're counting down to.
4. **The car.** 16 reels filmed in the car: median 44K. 10 filmed in the
   house: median 22K. Some of that is timing (the house videos are mostly
   later), but the car is also the set Alex Garcia tells you to lock: same
   shot, same place, so the feed knows it's you. The last two posts were in
   the house.
5. **Running kit on camera.** In the car Luke wears a bright running zip
   top in almost every winner. In the house it's a shirt or a hoodie. The
   new ERN tops belong here.
6. **A real photo of one person or one thing on top.** A runner bent
   double, a man eating, feet on a scale, a splits screen. Not a motion
   graphic (enjoy, goal gone: the bottom two), not a busy map.
7. **Short titles in normal case, with one word in capitals.** All top 5
   are written normally (Where the marathon wall actually starts), three
   with one word in capitals (ACTUALLY, WITHOUT, FIRST). The all-capitals
   titles have a median of 29K against 34K for normal case. It's not proof
   (most all-capitals titles were goal times), but our current rule is
   all capitals, and the evidence leans the other way. **Test it with
   trial reels.**

Also: every winner used the gate "If you're running a marathon in the next
few weeks". It was true then. For spring topics the true version is "If
you've got a marathon this spring," (a season, not a date, P153).
