# 1,000 true fans for ERN: what Seth said, what our numbers say, the plan

## In one paragraph

Seth's point is to stop working for the platform's numbers and build a small
group of people who would miss you if you disappeared and who tell others
because it helps *them*. Our Klaviyo numbers say we're further from that than the
views make it look. **590 people on the list, but no email has had more than 8
clicks, and our best reel ever (18K views, 15 Nov) brought about 2 sign-ups a
day.** We have an audience, not fans. The plan below keeps the reels as the shop
window and adds what Seth says actually builds fans: a named smallest audience,
the founders talking to 20 of them, a numbered founding list and a reason to
tell a friend that's built into the offer.

**Honest reading:** we probably have fewer than 10 true fans today, not 590. And
views and emails have come apart: the best video of the autumn brought no more
sign-ups than a quiet day. One confounder: since 12 Nov the email ask moved from
the body to the last line or the pinned comment (P204, P235), so the drop could
partly be our own CTA change and not only the season. We can't separate the two
from these numbers.

**One note on Huel:** I haven't checked Huel's early history in this chat, so
nothing below leans on it. Their fans had a product to buy from the first day.
Ours don't yet, which makes the founding list (step 3) matter more for us than
it did for them.

## The plan

### Step 1: One sentence on who ERN is for (Luke and Jamie, this week)

My recommendation, built from who our best reels served (fourth hour, gel
clock, hand plan, wrist numbers):

> **ERN is for amateur marathoners between 3:30 and 4:30, training for a spring
> race, who've hit the wall or can't stomach gels and want a plan they can carry
> on race day. It isn't for elites, for people chasing grams-per-hour lab
> numbers, or for anyone who just wants the cheapest gel.**

Seth would call that specific enough to fail, which is the point. It's also why I
question two of the recent rules (see the council): the non-marathon slot (P234)
and the "fit again" series for anyone who used to feel fit (P236). Both widen
the audience at the moment Seth says to narrow it.

### Step 2: The founders sit with 20 of them (23 Nov to 13 Dec)

This is our version of Superhuman's hour with each user, and it's the step I'd
keep if we kept only one.

- **The next email asks for one reply:** "What did you eat in the 2 hours
  before your last long run? Send a photo or one line." It's behaviour, not
  opinion, so it's the kind of answer Seth trusts.
- **The first 20 who reply get a 15-minute call** with Luke or Jamie (10 each,
  about 2.5 hours each in total). On the call we build their race-day fuel plan
  with them and watch where they hesitate, what scares them and what they've
  already tried. Seth's questions: which part scares you? What would you get rid
  of if someone did it for you?
- **Notes go in `research/fan-calls/` with no names or emails** (initials and a
  goal time only), written as what they did, not what they said they'd do.
- What it delivers: the real problem in their words for the scripts, the first
  20 people who'd miss us, and product research on the bar that costs nothing.

### Step 3: The founding list, numbered and limited (by 20 Dec, blocked on Luke)

Decware's public waiting list, done honestly:

- "The first batch is [X] bars. The first [N] people on the founding list get
  them, in order. You're #[n]."
- **Blocked on Luke:** the real first-batch size and a date range we can stand
  behind. If the batch isn't genuinely limited, we don't do this. Fake scarcity
  is the hype Seth says the internet undid, and it breaks the trust we're
  building.
- **Status:** founding members are named on the launch page (with their OK), get
  the first batch and get a say in the final flavour, which we can offer because
  of the Rice Crisp Graveyard series.

### Step 4: Build the reason to tell a friend into the offer (with step 3)

Carmine's only seats tables of 6 or more. Our version:

- **Bring a training partner and you both move up 25 places.** Running is done in
  pairs and clubs, so the referral follows how they already train.
- **The race-day card:** the four numbers we already hand over (hand plan, wrist
  numbers; saves 2.6% to 2.7%, our best) as a small printed card with our mark on
  it, posted to founding members. It sits on a wrist at a start line, so it's
  our version of the Patagonia logo. Test on 50 people first, because it costs
  postage.

### Step 5: The clipboard (from January, when the spring blocks start)

The heating engineer's 40 neighbours' numbers. Ours is a page of named results
from runners who used our plan ("planned 4:30, ran 4:20:51"), each one with
their OK. We have 16 results and 10 PBs already. Every new result goes on it.
It's proof a stranger can check, which no gel brand has.

### Step 6: Count people, not views (the weekly loop, from 30 Nov)

The Monday results loop (`ideas/weekly-loop-prompt.md`) adds these four lines at
the top, above the reel stats:

| Metric | Today | 31 Jan target |
|---|---|---|
| New emails this week | about 18 | 30 |
| Replies to the week's email | not tracked | 20 |
| Fan calls done (running total) | 0 | 20 |
| **True fans** (replied, came on a call, or sent a result) | **under 10** | **100** |

100 by the end of January, not 1,000. At the current sign-up rate the whole list
reaches about 1,000 around April, and a true fan takes more than a sign-up.
1,000 is the target for launch.

### What doesn't change

The reels and the gate stay. They're the shop window that brings strangers in,
and the gate is why the fourth hour worked. Seth isn't saying don't make content.
He's saying don't mistake the shop window for the shop.

## LLM Council (P150): should we rebuild around 1,000 true fans?

**Contrarian (why it fails):** Seth is selling to freelancers and authors with a
product to buy. We don't have one: no launch date, a slow food scientist. A
founding list for a bar that might not exist for six months goes cold, and the
people we called in December forget us by June. Second, true fans of *what*? Our
content is about fuel during the race (gels at 16.2K, 30g every 30 minutes),
but `audience.md` says the bar is for the 30 minutes before. We could build
1,000 fans of our gel plan who have no reason to buy a pre-race bar.

**Assumption-ripper:** Three assumptions to test. (1) "Views don't turn into
fans": the November drop lines up with our own CTA moving out of the body on
12 Nov, so test that before blaming views. (2) "Luke's comment point": Seth
actually says don't read the one-star reviews, so the lesson is to listen to the
people it's for, not to read everything. (3) "Huel did this": Huel had a product
to sell from day one, so the comparison only holds once the bar is real. And
the assumption in our own system: 236 principles tuned to skip rate and OST
scores are, in Seth's words, learning to win the algorithm's high school.

**Expansionist (the upside we're missing):** The calls are the cheapest
product research we'll ever get, and they make the podcast. "Listener fuel
call of the week" is a ready-made segment for DEAD LEGS, with permission. And
the founding list could fund the first run: if 300 people pre-pay for a box,
the minimum order with a manufacturer stops being a risk. That's how a
pre-launch brand uses true fans.

**Outsider (no context):** "Do you know the names of ten people who'd be gutted
if you stopped? If not, why are you writing title number 237?" And "why does a
bar company make a coffee video?"

**Executor (only the end result):** By Fri 27 Nov: the one-sentence audience
agreed, and the next email asks for the reply. By Sun 13 Dec: 20 calls done,
notes in `research/fan-calls/`. By Sun 20 Dec: founding list live if Luke has a
real batch size, otherwise a "first to know" list with no numbers. Weekly loop
counts true fans from Mon 30 Nov. It costs about 5 founder hours and one email.

**Blind cross-review:**
- The Contrarian's product-fit point is the strongest in the room and nobody
  answers it. It decides whether step 3 is a pre-order or a mailing list. It
  goes to Luke as a question.
- The Assumption-ripper's CTA confounder is right and cheap to test. Put the
  email ask back in one reel's body as an A/B before deciding views don't
  convert.
- The Expansionist's pre-pay idea is the biggest upside but depends on the
  Contrarian's question and a real batch size. Park it until both are answered.
- The Outsider's coffee question lands. The non-marathon slot (P234) and the
  broad "again" series (P236) widen the audience, and Seth says narrow it. That's
  a real conflict with two of Luke's rules from last week, so it's his call, not
  ours.
- The Executor's dates hold. The 5-hour cost is small next to what the reels
  take each week.

**Chairman's verdict:** **Do steps 1, 2 and 6 now. They're cheap, they need no
product, and they start the count Seth says matters. Hold steps 3 and 4 until
Luke answers two questions: is the bar for before or during the race, and what's
the real first-batch size?** Keep the reels and the gate as they are, but test
the email ask back in the body once to check whether views have really stopped
turning into sign-ups. Put P234 and P236 to Luke as a conflict with this plan,
not as changes.
