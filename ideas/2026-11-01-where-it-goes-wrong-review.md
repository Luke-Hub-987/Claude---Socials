## Why (ledger first, P148)
At 0:02 the viewer has seen the photo and the title and heard "If you've got
a marathon this spring". That's where it went.

1. **The gate told most people it wasn't for them yet (new ledger row 24).**
   "This spring" is true, but it says "months away". Our winners said "in
   the next few weeks" when that was true. Two days earlier, the email reel
   opened with Luke's own line, "If you have a marathon or any race coming
   up", and got **33.3%**. Same week, same audience, one sentence different.
   This is the strongest single cause.
2. **The photo was a mood, not a problem (new ledger row 25).** A runner in
   silhouette on a wet road at sunset reads as a motivational stock clip. Our
   best frames showed the problem: a runner bent double, cramp, a man eating
   for carb load, a splits screen, a real email. Nothing in this picture says
   something is about to go wrong. (It also doesn't clearly read as downhill.)
3. **The burned-in subtitles misheard the numbers.** On screen: "It probably
   won't go wrong at 30" and "Four k where your legs go". The title says
   where it goes wrong, the subtitle says a wrong number. Subtitles also ran
   from 0:00 (the plan said from 0:03, P159). A repeat of P159: my miss for
   not putting a subtitle proof-read on the checklist.
4. **What didn't save it:** the car, the running top and the normal-case
   title. My top-reels analysis (31 Oct) treated those as drivers. They look
   like hygiene: fine to keep, but they don't stop a scroll on their own. The
   calendar, the gate and the problem in frame one do.
5. **The wall family again.** For followers who saw the wall (184K), "where
   it actually goes wrong" can read as a rerun. A smaller factor than 1 and 2.
