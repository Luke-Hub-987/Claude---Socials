## Eight findings

### 1. The audience comes for the race and skips the fuel

| Family | Videos | Views |
|---|---|---|
| **The wall** | 2 | **68K, 19K** |
| Race-day clock and race outcome | 5 | 9K to 21K |
| Race mechanics and problems | 2 | 10K, 9.5K |
| **Fuel as the topic** | 4 | **1.6K to 4.4K** |

**Every fuel-titled video is in the bottom four.** The wall video is
mostly fuel inside (eat at 20 minutes, blood leaving the gut, sugar
running down), and it did 68K. **Fuel works as the advice inside a race
video. It fails as the title.** For a nutrition brand that is the most
important finding in the set: the product lives inside the race.

### 2. The creative test is the outlier, and what it tested was the audience size

Luke's creative brief on 27 September produced "What happens to your
body at every 10K", which became the wall video. **Its declared test
was upstream broadening: a title that passes the "my mother" test**
(Galloway's New audience). It reached **55,449 viewers**, three times
anything else. Every other video was written for someone already in a
block. **Broadening worked, and every video since went back to core.**

| Video | "Would a non-runner want to watch?" | Views |
|---|---|---|
| Where the marathon wall actually starts | **Yes**, everyone knows the wall | 68K |
| How to get out of the marathon wall | Partly | 19K |
| Where marathon cramp actually starts | Partly | 9.5K |
| How many gels you actually need | No | 3.8K |

### 3. The secret title works, and the "how to" costs skip

Same package, same asset: "Where... actually starts" 25.5% skip,
"How to get out of..." 36.2%. Cramp brought it back to 33.0%. **A
secret as a question is the strongest OST shape we have** (P97).

### 6. Steps get saved, secrets get shared, contested claims get comments

- **Save** is highest on step-by-step instructions for one moment:
  3 hours 3.7%, out of the wall 3.1%, cramp 2.8%, 30 minutes 2.5%.
- **Share** is highest on a single overturned fact: 42K tangents 0.8%.
- **Comments** come from a claim people want to argue with: wall 7,
  first half ("banking time") 6, 3 hours 5, cramp 4, goal pace 4.

**The wall video had all three**, which is why the best next video
needs a secret, four steps and something to argue about.

### 7. TikTok rewards the clock and completion, not the Instagram winner

TikTok's best is the 3 hours video (5,090 views, 20 follows), and its
best completion is the wall (9.4%). The wall itself did only 654 there.
**TikTok follows come from clock-moment instructions**; Instagram
reach comes from the broad secret.

### 8. Follows: still unread from the ERN side

Every recent read says 0 because it is opened from the personal
account. The 30 minutes video, read from ERN, showed 12. **Follow
performance for the last five videos is unknown** until someone opens
them from the ERN account.
