## Step 1 — Five advisors

### 1. The Contrarian (what will fail)

- **The hook is vaguer than the result deserves.** "Over 1 hour" when
  you have the actual times. "3:58 to 2:54" stops a thumb; "over 1
  hour" is a claim anyone could make.
- **Without the method, it reads as an ad.** Brag, stat, stat, offer.
  Nothing a reader can take away if they don't click. Your own rules
  say the reader is the hero, and here they're an audience for your
  result.
- **"Nearly 600" appears twice in four paragraphs.** The second one
  makes the first look padded.
- **The 45-PBs sentence is the weakest line in the post.** "We advised
  over 45 runners to run their own marathon PBs" reads as though you
  told them to PB. Then a fragment ("Ranging all the way from...") that
  mixes categories: a first marathon isn't a time.

### 2. The Assumption-Ripper (challenge every assumption)

- **You're assuming the plan caused the PBs.** 45 people who also
  trained for months, and a first marathon is automatically a PB.
  Nobody will audit you, but anyone sharp will notice "first marathon"
  sitting in a PB list.
- **You're assuming "we" means something to the reader.** It doesn't.
  The friend is unnamed and the reader can't tell who's speaking.
- **You're assuming NYC runners read your LinkedIn.** Your own
  audience data: Greater Dublin 16-34%, London 8-20%. New York is six
  days out and a sliver of your followers.
- **You're assuming "personalised" scales.** Your best post did 65,693
  impressions and 175 link clicks. If this one hits, can you actually
  turn around 150 personalised plans in a week, and if not, what does
  a slow or generic reply do to the trust you just bought?

### 3. The Expansionist (the upside you're missing)

- **22,500 people ran Dublin yesterday.** A large share of your
  network was there or knows someone who was, and today every one of
  them is asking "what's next?" Speak to them directly. That's a far
  bigger, warmer CTA than New York.
- **Tag your friend.** Their whole network sees the post. Free reach,
  and it answers "who is we."
- **The 2:47 is your best asset and it's buried at the end of a
  fragment.** That's a story in itself. With permission, tag that
  runner too, and it's a series: "the 45," one per week.
- **The before-and-after finish photos do more than any line of copy.**
  Real times, real medals, two of you.

### 4. The Outsider (knows nothing, asks the obvious)

- **What's a PB?** I assume personal best, but your audience is
  accountants.
- **Why is it free? What's the catch?** A free personalised plan from
  a stranger is either generous or a sales funnel, and I can't tell
  which.
- **Why does the link go to a nutrition company?** I wanted a marathon
  plan and I've landed on a carb bar homepage. I don't know where to
  click, so I leave.
- **What does "built around your course" mean?** That's actually the
  most interesting phrase in the post, and you don't explain it.

### 5. The Executor (what to do Monday morning)

- **Short is the right call.** The method lives in the plan they get
  for free. Don't give it all away in the post.
- **Fix three things before posting:** real times in line one, the
  45-PBs sentence rewritten, and the duplicate 600 cut.
- **Link straight to the plan form, not the homepage.** Every extra
  click halves the conversion. Test it on a phone first.
- **Reply to every comment in the first hour.** That's what LinkedIn
  rewards with distribution.

## Step 3 — The Chairman's verdict

**Keep it short. Your instinct to cut was right.** The post doesn't
need the method, because the plan is the method and that's what
they're signing up for. But the short version currently spends its
few lines on vague claims where you have specific ones, repeats its
best stat, and sends people to the wrong page.

**Five changes, in order of impact:**

1. **Aim the CTA at yesterday's Dublin finishers.** They're your
   audience, they're thinking about their next race right now, and
   the window is about a week. New York is six days out and a sliver
   of your followers. Lead with Dublin and spring.
2. **Put the real times in line one, and name your friend.** Tag them.
3. **Rewrite the 45 line** so it's one clean sentence that ends on the
   2:47.
4. **One line of reason-to-believe:** what all of them had in common.
5. **Link straight to the plan form**, and say "PBs," not "PB's."

**Operational risk, before you post:** if this does the numbers your
best post did, you could get 150+ plan requests in a week. Decide now
what a fast, genuinely personal reply looks like at that volume, or
the post that builds trust becomes the one that spends it.
