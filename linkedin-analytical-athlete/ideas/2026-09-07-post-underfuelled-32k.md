## THE POST (v2)

10/10 signs you under-fuelled before a race (you find out at 32k).

There are two doors into your bloodstream for carbohydrate. You have almost certainly been using one.

The first door takes glucose. It jams at roughly 60 grams an hour, and it does not care how fit you are.

The second door only opens for fructose. It is worth another 30 to 60 grams an hour on top.

That is the whole reason a pro can push 90 grams an hour on the move, and you cannot get a single gel down at 25k.

Same stomach. Different door.

Here is how you find out it was you.

1. You started eating when you felt like you needed to. By then you were an hour behind.

2. Your carb loading was one big pasta dinner the night before.

3. You can name your weekly mileage to the kilometre. You have never counted your carbs per hour.

4. Your first gel went in at 20k, because you felt fine until then.

5. You have never turned a gel over to read which sugars are in it.

6. You tried your race fuelling for the first time on race morning.

7. You ate less in taper week, because you were training less.

8. Your long runs were fasted, to teach your body to burn fat.

9. Your pace fell off a cliff in the last 10k while your effort never changed.

10. You finished convinced your stomach cannot handle food on the move.

Nine of those are fixable this week. The tenth is a belief, and it goes when the other nine do.

What to change on your next long run:

Start eating at 20 minutes, on a clock, before anything tells you to.

Take 60 grams an hour, and make sure some of it is fructose, so the second door is open.

Then do it again the Saturday after that, until it is boring.

That is the whole change.
