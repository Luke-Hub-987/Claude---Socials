## THE POST

10/10 signs you under-fuelled before a race (you find out at 32k).

Your body has two separate doors for moving carbohydrate out of your stomach and into your blood.

The first door handles glucose. It jams at roughly 60 grams an hour, no matter how fit you are or how much you swallow.

The second door only opens for fructose. It is worth another 30 to 60 grams an hour on top.

Elite runners take 90 to 100 grams an hour because they use both doors. Most amateurs queue at one door with everything they own, then spend the last 10k wondering where their legs went.

Here is how you know it happened to you.

1. You started eating when you felt like you needed to. By then you were already an hour behind.

2. Your carb loading was one large pasta dinner the night before.

3. You can tell me your weekly mileage to the kilometre. You have never counted your carbs per hour.

4. Your first gel went in at 20k, because you felt fine until then.

5. Everything in your kit was glucose. Nothing was fructose.

6. You tried your race fuelling for the first time on race morning.

7. You ate less during taper week, because you were training less.

8. Your long runs were fasted, to teach your body to burn fat.

9. Your pace collapsed over the last 10k while your effort stayed exactly the same.

10. You finished convinced your stomach cannot handle food on the move.

Most people read that list and find six of them.

What to do on your next long run:

Start eating at 20 minutes. Not at 20k, and not when you feel like you need it.

Take 60 grams an hour, and make sure some of it is fructose, so the second door is open.

That is the whole change.
