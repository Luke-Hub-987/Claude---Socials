## Every [bracket] needs your real number

- **Your time off, and ideally both times.** "[4:0X] to [2:5X]" beats
  "1 hour X minutes" as a hook. The before and after times are the
  most specific, most unguessable thing in the post, and they're the
  reason anyone reads line two.
- **Your friend's name and their time off.** If they also took over an
  hour, say so, because two people doing it on the same day is the
  proof it's a method. If it was less, say the real figure. Don't round
  them up to match yours.
- **The 30+ figure.** Use the exact count. And check what it's counting:
  if some of those are first marathons, a first finish is technically a
  PB, but "[X] PBs and [Y] first marathons finished" is more credible
  and reads bigger, not smaller.
- **"Nearly 600."** Fine as written if the real figure is 570 or above.
  If it's lower, use the real number.
- **Points 1 to 4 have to be what you actually did.** I built them from
  the principles already in this project (goal pace, fuelling at 60g
  from minute 20, three key sessions, running the line). If any of
  them didn't apply to your Dublin block, swap in what did. A method
  post about a real PB is only as strong as its least true line.
- **The honest bit.** Only use it if it's true that your first
  marathon went badly. It's the most trust-building line in the post
  when it's true, and the most damaging when it isn't.

## Why this shape

- **The times are the hook.** Your best IG titles and LinkedIn posts
  all lead with something specific and unguessable. Here that's a real
  result, and an hour off a marathon pulls Core, Casual and New at
  once: runners want the method, someone who's done one marathon
  wonders if they could, and anyone at all knows an hour is a lot.
- **Paragraph 2 turns one result into a pattern.** One PB is a good
  day. 30+ PBs and 600 plans is a method. That's the line that makes
  the CTA land.
- **Numbered headers of a few words, bullets under each, contractions,
  around 230 words.** Per the Lara Acosta structure.
- **No career, no "two accountants."** Pure running, per the Pillar 1
  retirement.
