# The list-format library

## First, why "10/10 reasons... and the one that costs them" failed

Luke's verdict on it: not enough stakes.

The diagnosis is the **unit**. You cannot acquire a reason. A list of
reasons explains behaviour the viewer already has, so there is nothing
to take away and nothing lost by scrolling. Compare the units in the
lines that worked: **habits** (do them), **signs** (check yourself),
**things to buy** (spend or save), **quotes** (repeat them).

**Rule: the unit must be possessable.** Habits, signs, rules, swaps,
words, foods, prices, checks. Never reasons, factors, thoughts,
considerations.

## The four mechanisms underneath the format

Every alternative below is built by hitting these, not by copying the
words.

**1. The open loop (Zeigarnik).** Unfinished tasks are held in memory
better than finished ones, and a numbered set is an explicitly
unfinished task. The brain does not settle until it is closed. Bullet
lists compound it: several small completions inside one video.

**2. The information gap (Loewenstein).** Curiosity is the gap between
what you know and what you have just been told exists. Crucially,
**complete ignorance produces almost no curiosity** — partial knowledge
is what creates the pull. That is the mechanical reason for the
half-known keyword rule, and the reason invented jargon kills a hook.

**3. Processing fluency.** A numeral caps the perceived cost of
watching. "7 things" reads as a defined, structured, survivable
commitment. Density and vagueness read as work.

**4. Self-referential encoding.** Information processed in relation to
the self is attended to and remembered better than the same
information processed neutrally. That is exactly Luke's *"that is me"*
test, and it is why an identity in the tail beats a topic.

**And the one that creates the cost of scrolling: loss aversion.** A
framed loss moves people harder than an equivalent framed gain. So the
tail should name what they lose, not only what they gain. "£192" beats
"save money".

## Eleven formats that do the same job

Each names the mechanisms it fires and carries an ERN line built at
rung four (P60). ⚑ = a real number Luke confirms.

### 1. The receipt — an itemised bill
*Loss aversion, made literal and arithmetic.*
> **£4 a gel, 3 a long run, 16 long runs. £192 to not enjoy any of it.** ⚑

The viewer does the multiplication themselves, which is the Rolls-Royce
derived-conclusion move. Strongest line in the batch.

### 2. The glossary — words that decide an outcome
*Information gap at its sharpest: they have seen these words and never
looked them up.*
> **The 3 words on a gel packet that decide whether it stays down** ⚑

Possessable, usable in a shop tomorrow, and the cost of scrolling is
buying the wrong thing again.

### 3. The self-audit — they score themselves
*Self-referential encoding plus social comparison.*
> **7 things you do on every long run. Most runners are doing 4 of them wrong.** ⚑

Needs real grounding before it ships, or it becomes a false premise
(P54's carve-out on the fittest-runner line).

### 4. The tier list — categorisation shortcut
*Fluency, comparison, and an argument in the comments.*
> **Every gel in the shop, S to D, by what's still fine at 30k**

### 5. The autopsy — a named failure taken apart
*Information gap with a real corpse. Series numbering compounds.*
> **He took a gel at mile 20 that had never once caused a problem in training** *(Race Report Autopsy, Ep 1)*

### 6. The red flags — early warnings of a later disaster
*Time bomb plus self-incrimination.*
> **4 things at 7am that mean the last 10k is already gone** ⚑

### 7. The commandments — rules for a named person
*Fluency plus a gate. The gate is the whole value.*
> **4 rules for eating before a marathon if you're out there 4:30, not 2:30**

### 8. The elimination — a set cut down in front of them
*Open loop held to the final frame.*
> **9 race breakfasts, cut down to the 1 we'd take to a start line** ⚑

### 9. The archetype cast — recognition of people at the scene
*Pure self-referential encoding. Weak on stakes alone, so it must
carry a cost in the tail.*
> **The 5 people in every start line portaloo queue, and the one who has already lost 2 minutes**

### 10. The countdown from worst
*Retention: the loop only closes at the end.*
> **Every gel I've taken this year, worst to best, ending with the one I'd actually take again** ⚑

### 11. The threshold — one number that changes behaviour
*Small gap, single fact, high fluency.*
> **The exact minute your body stops caring what you ate that morning** ⚑

## The check before any list ships

1. **Is the unit possessable?** Could they take one and use it?
2. **Is the loop closed only at the end**, or does the OST give it away?
3. **Do they half-know the keyword?** No foothold, no curiosity.
4. **Is a loss named**, not just a gain?
5. **Would they say "that is me" in the first second?**
