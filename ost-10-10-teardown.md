# Part A — "10/10 uncomfortable habits to make you dangerously charismatic", word by word

Luke's own scroll-stopper. Eight elements, each doing a separate job.

| Element | Job | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **10** | Promises a **complete set**, not a sample. Round numbers are processed fastest, so the commitment feels defined and survivable. | Processing fluency |
| **/10** | **Turns the number into a score.** This is the move nearly everyone misses. "10 habits" is a listicle, which the feed is saturated with and skims. "10/10" is a **rating**, which implies a judge, a standard, and things that were rejected. | Categorisation shortcut; authority by implied filtering |
| **10/10 together** | Reads as **top marks**. One token does two jobs at once: how many, and how good. Compression, and a quality claim you never have to defend. | Fluency + implied curation |
| **uncomfortable** | The **price tag**. Prices the content as costly, which signals substance to an audience jaded by easy hacks. It also kills the "I've heard this before" objection, because uncomfortable means *not derivable*. | Costly signalling; the unguessability ingredient in P54 |
| **habits** | A **possessable, repeating unit**. Not tips (one-off), not reasons (unusable). Habits are ongoing, so the promise is identity change rather than information. | Self-referential encoding |
| **to make you** | An explicit **causal bridge**. The line stops being about a topic and becomes about a transformation in the viewer. | Self-reference; loss aversion is now available |
| **dangerously** | **Transgression** bolted onto a socially approved goal. Slightly forbidden reward against a stated cost. | Cost-plus-forbidden-reward tension |
| **charismatic** | An **identity, not a metric.** Universally wanted, unfalsifiable, so nobody argues with it or feels excluded by it. | Pre-existing desire (P54 ingredient 1) |

## The four mechanisms holding it up

1. **Open loop (Zeigarnik).** Unfinished sets are held in mind better
   than finished ones. A number is an explicitly unfinished task.
2. **Information gap (Loewenstein).** Curiosity needs *partial*
   knowledge. Total ignorance produces almost none. "Habits" and
   "charismatic" are both half-known, so the hole is small and sharp.
3. **Processing fluency.** The numeral caps the perceived cost of
   watching.
4. **Self-referential encoding.** Content processed about the self gets
   more attention and better recall. This is the "that is me" test.

Plus **loss aversion** on the tail: a named loss moves harder than the
same-sized gain.

Sources: [Zeigarnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeigarnik_effect) ·
[curiosity gap](https://www.writebetterarticles.com/learn/curiosity-gap) ·
[Loewenstein](https://psychologyfanatic.com/information-gap-theory/) ·
[self-referential encoding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-referential_encoding) ·
[tier lists as categorisation](https://en.thairath.co.th/plus/everydaylife/106105)

## The skeleton, and the rest of the family

```
[SCORE]  [PRICE]  [POSSESSABLE UNIT]  [CAUSAL BRIDGE]  [IDENTITY]
```

- "10/10 **famous** **quotes** **to completely alter** your **sense of
  reality**" — same skeleton, effort-to-outcome gap pushed to the
  extreme: ten sentences in, a worldview out.
- "10/10 **uncomfortable** **habits** **of** runners who **never get
  injured**" — Luke's own, 2,156. The bridge is "of", which swaps
  transformation for membership: these are the people you want to be.
- "**Be a 10/10 Athlete**" (`@brandon_trajano`) — the score moved onto
  the person.
- "10/10 **signs** your easy pace is **wrecking your half marathon**" —
  the unit changed to signs, so the viewer self-scores.

**The unit is the lever nobody touches.** Habits, quotes, signs, rules,
words, foods, prices. Change it and you get a new format without
leaving the shape that works.

# Part B — the version we own

"10/10" is not borrowed language in endurance. **Runners already score
effort out of ten every single day** — RPE, rate of perceived exertion,
where 10/10 is all-out. The scale is already in the ICP's mouth, which
is exactly the iceberg-vocabulary condition Briar describes.

So the device we own is the same score, **rated on the axis our niche
actually argues about**:

> **Everything scored out of 10 at race effort.**

Not taste. Not calories. **What it does to you when you are working
hard.** That axis belongs to ERN, it is the whole reason the product
exists, and it is instantly understandable to someone who has never
run.

It gives us:

- a **repeatable series** (series numbering is the strongest signal in
  the 82-title dataset: Ross Mackay's Ep 55-64 holds 9 of the top 25
  slots on a 1,492 baseline)
- an **argument in the comments**, because a score invites
  disagreement in a way a list never does
- a **scoreboard the product eventually appears on** without a single
  claim being made about it

# Part C — the lines for the long-run b-roll

Every one is checked against HF6 (4-9 word main clause, one
parenthetical with a concrete tail), the zero-context test, and the
possessable-unit rule.

**1. 10/10 foods that are impossible to eat while running**
*(and the 3 that actually work)* ⚑
New: understands it completely with zero running knowledge, and it is
funny before it is useful. Casual: has choked on a cereal bar and given
up. Core: this is the solids-versus-gels argument. **Unit:** foods.
**Price:** impossible. **Tail:** a concrete payoff, not a vibe.
*Lead option.*

**2. 10/10 uncomfortable habits of runners who never fall apart at the end**
*(none of them are training)* ⚑
The proven 2,156 skeleton, unchanged, with a fuelling payload. "Fall
apart at the end" is plain English, which is what "hit the wall" and
"bonk" are not. The parenthetical is the whole hook: it tells Core this
is not another training video.

**3. Every runner snack, scored out of 10 at race effort**
*(starting with the one we made this morning)*
Our device, episode 1. The b-roll is the episode.

**4. 10/10 snacks runners take on long runs**
*(ranked by what stays down)*

**5. 10/10 signs you have never eaten properly on a long run**
*(the last one is the giveaway)* ⚑
Unit changed to signs, so the viewer scores themselves as they watch.

**6. 10/10 things elite runners eat on the move that sound wrong** ⚑
Borrowed authority. Only ships with real sourced examples (P40, P39).

## Honesty check before filming

The ranking formats require that he has genuinely tried the items.
**"10/10" is a score, not a count**, so three or four real ones is
fine and padding to ten is not. Today's bar is the live shot; the rest
is real experience or it does not go in.
