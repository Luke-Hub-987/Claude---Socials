# Voice — The Analytical Athlete (LinkedIn, @luke-morrison-aca-cta)

Drafted from 10 real full-text posts pulled directly off LinkedIn (see
`swipe-file.md`), the highest-impression/engagement posts in the
6/10/2026-9/7/2026 export. **Status: draft** — Luke should confirm this
matches how he wants to sound before `/ideate-analytical-athlete` leans
on it hard, the same way `brands/personal/voice.md` was confirmed after
review.

Luke named two explicit reference points for this brand: **Ogilvy /
John Caples-style direct-response copywriting** ("people don't care
about us, give them valuable information in a format/impact they've
never seen before") and **Justin Welsh's post structure** (clear,
concise, one idea, ending in a direct CTA to the email list). Both show
up clearly in the real samples below.

## Voice signature

Direct-response, not social-media-casual. Every post opens on a single
sharp claim or contrast, states real numbers/research, then closes on
a because-you-read-this-far CTA to "The Analytical Athlete" newsletter.
No hedging, no throat-clearing, no "let's talk about." The persona is a
credible peer inside the same career (accountant/tax professional who
also races ultras) speaking to people in that career, not a fitness
influencer speaking to the public. See `audience.md` — this is load-
bearing for why the voice works: it earns attention through shared
professional identity, not through fitness-niche authority.

## The line-by-line "slippery slide" mechanic (Sugarman/Ogilvy, confirmed by Luke)

Per Luke's own framing: "the headline should make you want to read the
next line, the next line should make you want to read the next line,
so on and so forth. Long copy isn't boring, it's just not exciting or
interesting enough." Every top post below is built this way — short
declarative sentences, each one a small hook into the next, never a
paragraph the reader can skim past. Test any draft by reading it one
line at a time and asking "does this line alone earn the next one."

## Post structure (Justin Welsh model, confirmed pattern across samples)

1. **Opening line = the whole hook**, almost always a contrast or a
   number, never a scene-setter. ("Same intake. Same bonus. One leaves
   at 5:30 to train for their PB.")
2. **A short, plain-language mechanism or research citation** — a real
   number, a named study, a physiological fact — never vague ("113
   studies. 4,390 people." / "British Journal of Sports Medicine" /
   "2.9% better running economy").
3. **Reframe onto the reader's own life**, second person, present
   tense ("You already know this in your body.").
4. **Close with a direct CTA to the newsletter**, named explicitly
   ("The Analytical Athlete"), with a growing subscriber count as
   social proof ("Join 145+ working professionals") and a single link.
   This is the money line — never cut it, never bury it, never make it
   vague ("check out my page"). **Confirmed 2026-09-07: the link
   resolves to `engineroomnutrition.com/pages/fuel-1` — this funnels
   into Engine Room Nutrition's own list, not a separate product** (see
   `audience.md` and `swipe-file.md`). "The Analytical Athlete" is the
   name/voice of this content stream, the destination and eventual
   offer are ERN's.

## Visual device: the two-panel contrast image (confirmed real, see `swipe-file.md`)

"Same intake, same bonus" carries a custom two-panel, retro-styled
graphic mirroring its opening line's contrast exactly: one panel shows
the disciplined figure (packing a gym bag), the other the figure who
"kept meaning to start" (slumped at a desk). Appears AI-generated
(Canva or similar), not a stock photo or a real photo of Luke. Where an
idea's hook is built on a two-sided contrast (see the post-structure
model above), pair it with this device rather than leaving it text-
only — it's a confirmed, real, repeatable pattern, not a guess. Not yet
confirmed whether Luke's other top posts (marathon-CEOs, by-4pm, etc.)
use a similar image or rely on text alone, so don't assume every idea
needs one, only ones with a genuine two-sided contrast to mirror.

## Vocabulary / recurring devices

- **The "engine" metaphor** — training capacity as a literal engine
  that "compounds quietly" or gets "protected." Appears across multiple
  top posts, seems to be Luke's core recurring image for this brand.
  Distinct from the personal brand's voice, which has no equivalent.
- **Real, specific numbers, never rounded**: "113 studies. 4,390
  people," "2.9% better running economy," "$500... resale... $3,000,"
  "145+ / 150+ / 160+ subscribers" (growing count used as live proof,
  update per post, never left stale).
- **Direct second-person address**, present tense: "You already know
  this in your body."
- **"PS" line reused as a secondary hook** for the newsletter's current
  topic, separate from the main CTA (seen in the tick-off-tasks post).
- **Named research citations** as the credibility mechanism, replacing
  the personal brand's "actually"/scare-quote skepticism device
  entirely — this voice trusts data, it doesn't undercut received
  wisdom with irony.

## How this differs from `brands/personal/voice.md`

Do not reuse the personal-brand voice here. That voice is blunt,
self-deprecating, deliberately anti-career-framing, and skeptical/ironic
("actually," scare quotes). This voice is the opposite on the one axis
that matters most: **career and professional identity are the entire
point here**, not something to strip out. Where personal-brand content
retires any workplace framing, this brand's central move is bridging
training discipline directly into career/professional capacity.

## Hard rules

- No em-dashes (universal rule, `voice-rules.md`).
- No generic productivity-guru language ("unlock your potential,"
  "10x your output," "hustle") — this audience is senior/skeptical
  professional-services people, that register reads as junior and gets
  ignored (see `audience.md`).
- Never cite a study, number, or research claim that isn't traceable to
  something real — same P39-style rule as the other brands. If a claim
  can't be sourced, don't state it as fact.
- Every post needs a real, single, named CTA to the newsletter with a
  live subscriber count — never a vague "link in bio" or omitted CTA,
  the whole funnel depends on this line.
