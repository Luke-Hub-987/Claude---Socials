# Content Pillars — The Analytical Athlete (LinkedIn)

Clustered from the real top-10 posts in `swipe-file.md` (6/10/2026-
9/7/2026 export). **Status: draft** — inferred from what already
performed, not yet confirmed by Luke. Flag anything that reads wrong
and it gets revised, same as the personal-brand pillars were.

A clear pivot is visible in the data: pre-April 2026, the account
posted tax/accounting news content (Trump tariffs, Irish dividends,
historical tax trivia) and got 4-20 impressions per post, essentially
nothing. From late April 2026 the account pivoted to the pillars below
and impressions jumped 10-800x. **Do not go back to generic
tax/accounting/news commentary as a pillar** — the data says it doesn't
work for this audience, despite them being finance professionals.

- **Pillar 1: Training-as-career-capacity (the "engine" narrative).**
  The core, highest-performing pillar. A contrast or narrative that
  reframes training discipline as the thing that quietly determines
  professional outcomes over years — never a training tip on its own.
  Examples: "Same intake, same bonus, one leaves at 5:30" (2,736
  impressions), "I'll get fit once work calms down" (1,011), "The
  executive/CEO you pictured isn't the one closing deals anymore"
  (475), "By 4pm your brain is running on whatever oxygen this
  morning's training sent it" (700). This is the pillar that most
  directly matches the audience (see `audience.md`) — write more of
  this than anything else.

- **Pillar 2: Research-backed performance science.** A real study or
  number, translated into what it means for a working professional's
  focus/decisions/output, not for race performance. Examples: the
  "113 studies, 4,390 people" cognitive-battery citation, the BJSM
  VO2-max/executive-function claim, the carbon-plate shoe "2.9% running
  economy" study. This is the credibility layer underneath Pillar 1 —
  most Pillar 1 posts actually cite one of these, so the two overlap
  rather than being fully separate.

- **Pillar 3: Practical training protocols for time-crunched
  professionals.** Concrete, specific "how" content aimed at someone
  who can't train like a full-time athlete. Examples: "What's the
  fewest runs a week that still gets me to a marathon?" (3-day plan,
  818 impressions), heat training as "poor man's altitude" (2,327),
  Dublin marathon training-monotony advice (1,114). This is the pillar
  most directly reusable from the existing IG/TikTok running-technique
  content (`brands/personal/pillars.md` Pillar 1), translated into
  LinkedIn's professional framing rather than copied verbatim.

- **Pillar 4: Personal proof-of-concept / credibility narrative.**
  Luke's own results and races, told as evidence he's not a theorist.
  Examples: "I've run more ultras than 5Ks, but the 5K is the one that
  frightens me" (8,115 impressions, best engagement of the period),
  Death Valley heat-training story, Cork marathon race report. Use
  sparingly relative to Pillars 1-2 — per `audience.md`, the account's
  pull is the training-to-career bridge, not Luke's race results on
  their own; the celebrity-namedrop posts (Bezos, Branson, Ramsay,
  "CEO of a $1.4T company") that lean purely on someone else's story
  without Luke's own specific angle underperformed everything else in
  the new era (23-86 impressions) and should not be repeated as a
  format.

## Mix guidance (draft, revise once more data exists)

Given Pillar 1 is both the best performer and the most differentiated
from every other channel Luke runs, default new-idea batches toward it:
roughly 4 Pillar 1, 2 Pillar 2, 2 Pillar 3, 2 Pillar 4 out of every 10,
unless Luke redirects.
