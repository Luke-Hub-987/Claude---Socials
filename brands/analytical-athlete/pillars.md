# Content Pillars — The Analytical Athlete (LinkedIn)

Clustered from the real top-10 posts in `swipe-file.md` (6/10/2026-
9/7/2026 export). **Status: draft** — inferred from what already
performed, not yet confirmed by Luke. Flag anything that reads wrong
and it gets revised, same as the personal-brand pillars were.

Per Luke's direct instruction (2026-09-07), the pre-4/26/2026
tax/accounting/news era is excluded from this brand entirely — see
`swipe-file.md`'s correction note. **Do not go back to generic
tax/accounting/news commentary as a pillar regardless** — Luke's own
call, and consistent with what evidence exists (that era's numbers were
uniformly dead, 4-20 impressions, even accounting for the data-quality
issue documented in `swipe-file.md`).

**CORRECTION, 2026-09-07:** an earlier draft of this file (and of
`swipe-file.md`) ranked posts using the xlsx export's impressions
column as if it were a lifetime total. It isn't, for any post published
before the export's 6/10/2026 window start — see `swipe-file.md`'s
correction note for the full explanation (the marathon-running-CEOs
post: 56 impressions in the xlsx, 65,693 phone-verified lifetime). That
means the celebrity-namedrop pattern flagged below as "underperforming"
in the original draft was wrong — the marathon-CEOs post is actually
the single best-performing post in the account's history by real data,
and it's a Pillar 1/2 post (research citation + career-capacity
bridge), not a bare namedrop. Pillar 4 guidance is corrected below.

- **Pillar 1: Training-as-career-capacity (the "engine" narrative).**
  The core, highest-performing pillar. A contrast or narrative that
  reframes training discipline as the thing that quietly determines
  professional outcomes over years — never a training tip on its own.
  Examples: **"Companies led by marathon-running CEOs are worth
  measurably more" (65,693 phone-verified impressions, 175 link
  clicks — the strongest real performance in the account's history)**,
  "Same intake, same bonus, one leaves at 5:30" (2,736 impressions),
  "I'll get fit once work calms down" (1,011), "The executive/CEO you
  pictured isn't the one closing deals anymore" (475), "By 4pm your
  brain is running on whatever oxygen this morning's training sent it"
  (700). This is the pillar that most directly matches the audience
  (see `audience.md`) — write more of this than anything else.

- **Pillar 2: Research-backed performance science.** A real study or
  number, translated into what it means for a working professional's
  focus/decisions/output, not for race performance. Examples: the
  "113 studies, 4,390 people" cognitive-battery citation, the BJSM
  VO2-max/executive-function claim, the carbon-plate shoe "2.9% running
  economy" study. This is the credibility layer underneath Pillar 1 —
  most Pillar 1 posts actually cite one of these, so the two overlap
  rather than being fully separate.

- **Pillar 3: Practical training protocols for time-crunched
  professionals.** Concrete, specific "how" content aimed at someone
  who can't train like a full-time athlete. Examples: "What's the
  fewest runs a week that still gets me to a marathon?" (3-day plan,
  818 impressions), heat training as "poor man's altitude" (2,327),
  Dublin marathon training-monotony advice (1,114). This is the pillar
  most directly reusable from the existing IG/TikTok running-technique
  content (`brands/personal/pillars.md` Pillar 1), translated into
  LinkedIn's professional framing rather than copied verbatim.

- **Pillar 4: Personal proof-of-concept / credibility narrative.**
  Luke's own results and races, told as evidence he's not a theorist.
  Examples: "I've run more ultras than 5Ks, but the 5K is the one that
  frightens me" (8,115 impressions, best engagement of the period),
  Death Valley heat-training story, Cork marathon race report. Use
  sparingly relative to Pillars 1-2 — per `audience.md`, the account's
  pull is the training-to-career bridge, not Luke's race results on
  their own. **Revised guidance on other-people's-story posts (Bezos,
  Branson, Ramsay, "CEO of a $1.4T company"):** the original version of
  this file flagged these as a failed format based on the xlsx export's
  numbers, which are now known to be unreliable for posts published
  before 6/10/2026 (see `swipe-file.md`'s correction note) — one post
  in that exact shape (marathon-running CEOs) turned out to be the best
  performer in the account's history once verified. The real
  distinguishing factor isn't "avoid other people's stories," it's
  **whether the story carries a real, specific, checkable research
  citation** (marathon-CEOs cites a named peer-reviewed S&P 1500 study;
  the still-unverified Bezos/Branson/Ramsay posts read more like
  received wisdom/anecdote in the slug titles alone). Treat this as
  genuinely open until those posts' real numbers and full text are
  checked — don't repeat the old "avoid celebrity stories" rule as
  settled fact, and don't assume they're secretly great either.
