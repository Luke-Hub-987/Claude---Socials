# Voice — Engine Room Nutrition (Company, @engineroomnutrition)

Built from real on-screen text + view counts across ERN's own IG grid,
confirmed by Luke. Per P15 in `principles.md`, on-screen text is the
actual headline for these formats, so these are pulled from the
on-screen overlays, not captions.

## Voice signature

Founder-led, first-person "us/we" brand voice — Luke and his
co-founder are visibly the product's own test subjects (climbing
mountains, running PBs, on the ERN bar), so the brand voice reads as
proof-by-doing rather than claims-by-marketing. Shares the same
skeptical "actually" framing and self-deprecating running humor as
Luke's personal voice (unsurprising, same person, overlapping
audience), but adds an origin-story/underdog angle ("2 accountants
made a carb bar") and recurring behind-the-scenes product-development
bits ("Rice Crisp[y] Graveyard" appears to be a recurring series, at
least Ep. 4 exists).

## Vocabulary / phrases ERN actually uses (drafted from the samples above)

- First-person plural for the brand itself: "us," "we," "Nobody
  expected us 2"
- "The bar" as the product's casual name, not "our product" or a full
  product name
- Same "actually" scare-quote contrarian marker as the personal brand
  ("actually need," "actually eat")
- Proof framing tied to a real endurance event: "survived bcause of
  the ERN bar," "Can the ERN Carb Bar Get us a PB"
- Origin-story self-deprecation: "2 accountants made a Carb Bar,"
  "Nobody expected us 2"

## Hard rules

- **No overclaiming on availability.** ERN is pre-manufacturing —
  working with a food scientist, progress has been slow, possibly
  bringing development in-house. Never imply the product is available
  now or state a firm launch date unless that's actually changed.
  Content CTAs point to the email/newsletter list, not a purchase.
- **The rice-krispie-square framing is deliberate**, keep it: it's the
  familiarity/trust mechanism that makes an unfamiliar "engineered"
  product feel approachable. Don't drift into generic "engineered
  performance nutrition" language that loses that hook.

- **Mild profanity/slang is fine** ("asf" etc.) — confirmed, same as
  the personal brand, no extra polish requirement for the company
  account.
