# Audience / ICP — Engine Room Nutrition

## Who they are

Working professionals who are also into endurance sport — the same
profile as the founders themselves (Luke: associate tax director,
marathon + 3 ultras including Connemara 60k, Connemara 100k, Seven
Sisters 55k). Not a huge market on its own (the endurance-specific
niche is real but not massive), which is why the product and content
are deliberately built to expand beyond pure endurance occasions over
time (see below).

## What they're struggling with

The specific, narrow problem ERN is built around: **the 30 minutes
before a race/event**. Right now the only real option in that window
is a gel, and most people who use gels don't actually like them — it's
tolerated, not enjoyed. There isn't a good, likeable product built
specifically for that pre-activity window.

## What they follow this account for

Two things at once: real fueling/performance advice (general, not
always product-pitchy — see Pillar 1), and to watch the founders
actually test the product themselves in real endurance conditions
(Pillar 2, the strongest-performing distinctly-ERN content so far).
Right now, since ERN is pre-manufacturing, the account is also
functioning as a pre-launch audience-builder — the content funnel's
CTA points to an email/newsletter signup, not a purchase, until the
product exists.

## Product context (for accurate claims — don't oversell)

- The product itself: a high-carbohydrate, low-fat bar built as a
  performance-optimized take on a classic rice krispie treat. The
  familiar format is deliberate — everyone already knows what a rice
  krispie square is, which lowers the trust/adoption barrier compared
  to an unfamiliar "engineered" product. (This is what the "Rice Crisp
  Graveyard" series, Pillar 4, is actually testing iterations of.)
- Longer-term positioning: not limited to endurance racing. The vision
  is broader pre-activity fuel occasions — halftime of a match, before
  a match, on the way to training. Maurten is the explicit reference
  model here: started narrow and endurance-specific, later broadened
  into other categories once established.
- Current stage: **pre-manufacturing.** Working with a food scientist,
  progress has been slow, considering bringing development in-house.
  Content should stay honest about this — no claims that imply the
  product is available now or has a firm launch date unless that
  changes.
- Possible audience-widening signal (low confidence — came through
  garbled in a voice message, worth confirming with Luke directly
  rather than acting on it): the bar may be intended as accessible to
  "anybody looking to get into running or endurance," not just
  already-serious athletes. If true, this softens "who they are"
  above toward including beginners, not just committed endurance
  athletes. Don't treat this as settled.
