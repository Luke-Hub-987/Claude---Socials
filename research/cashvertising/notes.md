# Cashvertising (Drew Eric Whitman), the parts Luke marked (26 Nov; photos in this folder)

Ad-agency secrets #18 to #20, pp.133-143, with Luke's notes (mental movies, VAKOG, internal representations, long copy, USP, specifically unique).

1. **Mental movies (#18).** People act on the picture they build in their head, not on facts. Make the picture sharp with the five senses, VAKOG: visual, auditory, kinesthetic (feeling), olfactory, gustatory. Cherry pie beats fruit tart; black widows "blowing out 750 eggs" beats "a few bugs".
2. **Facts alone don't sell.** "You did not buy because you were not sold": you hadn't built enough internal pictures. Take them on a tour of using it (the Squidly pool cleaner letter: the "chuga-chuga" sound, the brown pool now "like a Caribbean lagoon").
3. **Long copy outsells short** when every line adds a picture (the caption and the email, not the title).
4. **Don't be afraid to write it this way.** Mediocre copy comes from copying everyone else's.
5. **USP (#20).** If they can't tell you apart from the rest, they have no reason to prefer you. Name it: "Don't be just a hardware store, be The Hardware Superstore." Tell them why you're great; don't hope they notice.
6. **Specifically unique.** "19-Year-Old College Kid Discovers Clever Way to..." beats "Work From Home and Turn Your E-Mail Into Big Profits": a person they can picture, a discovery, a promise. "Be truthful, but find an intriguing way to say it." Starch research: something new and fresh in the headline gets higher read-most scores.
7. **The cover itself:** "How to Use More Than 100 Secrets of Ad-Agency Psychology to Make BIG MONEY Selling Anything to Anyone": a count, a hidden-knowledge word (secrets), the big benefit in capitals, the widest audience, and a REVEALED! badge.
