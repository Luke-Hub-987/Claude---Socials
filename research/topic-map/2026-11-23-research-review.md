# Research review: what our audience watches, and why

## 1. The five findings
1. **Food as permission beats food as rules.** The single biggest cluster across every platform is "you're allowed to eat, and it makes you faster":
   cheat days (kelsey.poulter 191K, 16x), stop fearing real food (774K, 12x), eat straight after training (223K, 29x), eating is strictly business
   (849K, 22x), "Elite ultrarunner: you are almost certainly not eating enough" (The Running Effect, 4.8x), "you will gain weight training for a marathon" (10x).
   Reddit: runners worried about gaining 5kg in a block. This is where we touch weight without being a weight-loss account, and it is the bar's lane.
2. **Easy running is the biggest training topic, and its pain is fear.** Zone 2 wrong numbers (cnozrun 1.53M, 183x), "You need to run SLOWER" (Magness 234K),
   "Build a killer base" (382K, 8.6x), "Most runners train too hard" (7.7x), zone 2 done wrong (307K, 20x). Reddit: "Slower runners, how do you stay motivated
   when people mock your pace?" (674 comments, the most-discussed post in r/running); the bundle: "ridiculously slow". Seth's freedom from fear, word for word.
3. **The pros against you.** What Ashley Young ate the day before (24x), Sawe at 2:50 a K, could you? (865K), Femke Bol's speeds (10x), 130-mile weeks,
   Almgren's long runs (AdvancedRunning: recreated for your paces, 325 votes), "I copied Clayton to break 2:30" (454). Status: borrow the edge.
4. **Team sport performance lives on IG and TikTok, not Reddit.** Six match-day accounts win (half-time drink 581K, post-game meals 1.4M, GAA "how
   inter-county players last 70 minutes" 11x); r/GAA and r/bootroom are match chat and boot collections. So team-sport topics get tested on reels only.
5. **Identity carries Reddit.** The top posts are transformations (depressed to a 3:14 marathon, 4,139 votes; couch to sub-3; a 71-year-old's first 5K;
   143kg first 5K) and age ("started at 39, what happened in years 2 to 4?", 349 comments; "44 and fit, can I still improve?", 231). Our 30s-and-40s
   restarter is real, and they want proof that people like them do it (affiliation), not tips.

## 3. The shapes that pull (proven on 2+ accounts)
- **The experience line:** "If I only had 5 minutes with most runners, I'd change these 5 things" (Ben Parkes 599K); "after 10 years, the best had one thing in common" (conor.talks 35x); "30 marathons, 20 years, one thing I learned" (18x).
- **You're doing X wrong / you need to do less:** "You need to run slower", "Most runners train too hard", "zone 2 done completely wrong", "your marathon workouts aren't long enough".
- **The real reason:** "The real reason you can't hold your marathon pace" (Scullion), "the secret and science behind it" (maya).
- **The pro's number against yours:** 2:50 a K, 130 miles a week, 6:00 easy pace, 10 hours' sleep.
- **10 tiny habits / 10 tricks** (2.4M; 912K): the again series shape.
- **Team X or Team Y? / Rating 1 to 10:** sides and scores pull comments.
- **The familiar beats the famous** (Cheesecake Factory beat Nvidia, 112x).
- **Comment the word, get the thing:** everyone's call to action; ours (word to email) is normal.

## 4. What doesn't travel to us (dropped)
Shoes and kit (the biggest outliers on Ben Parkes, beyond.pavements and ben_is_running, but product-driven), race vlogs and travel, famous guests
(Rich Roll, Untapped's Jamie Laing clips), stunts (Pritch, 100-mile days), elite race news, form drills. We don't have them, and they don't bring our viewer.

## 5. Gaps and caveats
- Reddit comments weren't pulled (titles and votes only); three subreddits hit the time limit at 43 to 52 posts.
- Not found: Slow Internet Club, Some Work All Play and On Coaching on YouTube, Rory's YouTube (Luke to send the links if wanted).
- Instagram saves and shares aren't public, so IG outliers are by views only; TikTok gives saves and shares.
- Most creators are US or UK with bigger bases; the ratio corrects for size, not for the calendar.
