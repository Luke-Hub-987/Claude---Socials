# The 10/10 format

## Why it works, slot by slot

> **10/10 uncomfortable habits of runners who never get injured** — 2,156

Four slots, each doing a different job. Change one slot at a time.

```
[10/10]  [PRICE]  [UNIT]  [BRIDGE]  [IDENTITY or OUTCOME]
 score   adjective  noun    to/that   who they want to be
```

**1. "10/10" is a quality score, not a count.** This is the part that
was nearly missed. It reads as *these score ten out of ten*, which
signals a curated, quality-controlled, collectible set filtered from a
larger pile. It is not "here are ten things". **That is why the video
worked with three items in it** — Luke cut five to three at filming and
called them "3 elite habits" out loud, while the on-screen text still
said 10/10. The count is free. Never pad to hit a number.

**2. The adjective is a price tag.** "Uncomfortable" prices the content
as costly, which signals substance to an audience that is jaded about
easy hacks. It runs against the painkiller rule deliberately, and
**pricing is a dimension with at least six settings, not one word.**
That is where the variants come from.

**3. The unit has to be possessable.** Habits, quotes, rules, lines,
signs, swaps, checks, receipts. Someone can repeat one after hearing it
once. "Things" and "tips" are not units, they are categories (P58).

**4. The tail is a pre-existing desire.** "Runners who never get
injured" is an identity they already want. If the tail has to sell them
on wanting it first, the line is dead before it starts (P54).

## The variants

Each one changes the pricing slot. Rotate them; never run the same
setting twice in a row (P52).

### 1. The price tag — costly, therefore real
> 10/10 **uncomfortable** habits of runners who never get injured *(2,156, proven)*
> 10/10 **unglamorous** rules of runners who finish the last 10k faster than the first

Power words: uncomfortable, unglamorous, humbling, boring, awkward,
expensive, thankless.

### 2. The forbidden — transgression attached to a soft outcome
> 10/10 **ruthless** things fast runners do at the start line
> 10/10 fuelling habits that feel like **cheating** at 30k

Power words: ruthless, illegal, banned, unhinged, cheating, unfair,
dangerously. Proven in the swipe file: "So F***ing Fast It Feels
Illegal", "dangerously charismatic".

### 3. The confession — in-group recognition
> 10/10 things every marathoner does at 6am and **nobody admits**
> 10/10 things runners **choke down** on race morning and pretend to enjoy

Power words: nobody admits, never says out loud, quietly, secretly,
pretend, off camera.

### 4. The delayed detonation — a time bomb in the tail
> 10/10 things elite runners say that sound **insane until mile 30** *(his own, approved)*
> 10/10 race-morning habits that make no sense **until 34k**

The power is the named moment, not the adjective. Any kilometre marker,
clock time or mile number works here.

### 5. The borrowed authority — a named tier of person
> 10/10 fuelling rules from runners who go **sub-2:30** ⚑
> 10/10 lines from **coaches** that change how you eat before a race ⚑

Named-person subjects are the most reliable multiplier in the 82-title
dataset (38.4x, 10.7x, 8.8x). P40 applies: the vehicle has to be real —
an interview, a cited source, an analysis — never a lift.

### 6. The self-incrimination — they are guilty and don't know it
> 10/10 **signs** your easy pace is quietly wrecking your half marathon *(approved)*
> 10/10 **signs** you under-ate before the race and won't find out until 30k

Power words: signs, tells, symptoms, quietly, without knowing.

### 7. The reversal — they have it backwards
> 10/10 fuelling rules most runners have **backwards**
> 10/10 race-week habits that are **the wrong way round**

Carries the corrected-diagnosis mechanism **without** the banned
it's-not-X-it's-Y construction (P60). One positive statement only.

### 8. The effort-to-outcome gap — tiny input, large return
> 10/10 things that take **20 seconds** and change your last 10k
> 10/10 **one-line** checks that stop you blowing up at 30k

The extreme version of this is the reference line "10/10 famous quotes
to completely alter your sense of reality": ten sentences in, an entire
worldview out.

### 9. The money — the most literal painkiller there is
> 10/10 things worth buying before you spend **£200** on carbon shoes *(approved)*
> 10/10 ways runners waste **£40 a race** on fuel that comes straight back up ⚑

### 10. The literal score — where 10/10 means what it says
> Every gel in the shop, **scored out of 10** by what's still fine at 30k
> 10/10 race breakfasts, **ranked** by what stays down

Ties the format to the tier-list lever, which is proven in the swipe
file and unused by us.

## The power-word bank, by the job it does

Words are not decoration. Each of these does one structural job, and
the job is what you are choosing (P52).

| Job | Words |
|---|---|
| **Price the content as costly** | uncomfortable, brutal, unglamorous, humbling, boring, awkward, thankless |
| **Make it transgressive** | ruthless, illegal, banned, unhinged, cheating, unfair, dangerously |
| **Make it secret** | quietly, secretly, nobody admits, never says out loud, off camera, unspoken |
| **Make it counterintuitive** | backwards, wrong way round, insane, absurd, makes no sense |
| **Borrow authority** | elite, sub-2:30, world-class, coach, Olympic, championship |
| **Make it irreversible** | for good, permanently, never again, ends your race, gone |
| **Set the bomb** | until mile 30, at 30k, at 6am, with 2 miles to go, at 34k |
| **State the consequence** | wrecks, costs you, ends, blows up, comes back up, hand back |

### The three rules for using them

1. **One power word per line, in one slot.** Two is where it starts
   sounding written. The rest of the line stays plain.
2. **Never bolt the same word across a batch.** That is the P52
   failure: one example of "insane" became "insane" on every line.
   Rotate the *job*, not just the word.
3. **A power word cannot rescue a weak structure.** If the stakes and
   the specificity are not already there, the adjective just makes the
   flatness louder.
